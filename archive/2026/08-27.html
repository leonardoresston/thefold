LEAD STORY

More than 150 killed in Nepal–Tibet flash flood

More than 150 people have been killed after a massive flash flood struck the Nepal–Tibet border. Hundreds remain missing as rescue teams search devastated communities and families wait for news at hospitals in Kathmandu. (BBC)

The disaster has left relatives trying to trace those caught in the flood across a remote mountain region where damaged roads and communications are complicating the response. The death toll is expected to rise as searches continue. (BBC)

NEWS

Burnham drops support for political-donation cap

Prime minister Andy Burnham has withdrawn support for a cap on political donations after opposition from Labour’s trade-union backers. He had backed a limit of about £500,000 in May, but the government will not support amendments imposing a domestic cap when the elections bill returns to the Commons next week. (Guardian)

US sanctions Palestine Action

The United States has designated the UK-based direct-action group Palestine Action as a global terrorist organisation, allowing American authorities to freeze assets under US jurisdiction. The move comes as the group’s challenge to its proscription in Britain approaches a supreme court hearing. (Guardian)

Extreme heat linked to 40,000 deaths in England

More than 40,000 excess deaths were associated with England’s hottest days between 1988 and 2025, according to new Office for National Statistics data. London recorded the highest number, while the UK Health Security Agency estimates that 2,877 people died during this May and June’s heatwaves alone. (Guardian)

Brazil and US resume tariff negotiations

Brazil and the United States will reopen tariff negotiations following a telephone conversation between presidents Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva and Donald Trump. Officials agreed to renew direct contact as both governments seek a route through the trade dispute. (Meio)

Plug-in solar reaches UK shops

Plug-in solar systems go on sale in Britain today, beginning at Argos, with Amazon, Currys, B&Q and Screwfix expected to follow. The 800-watt panels will cost about £400 to £500 and could save an average household up to £110 a year; every installation must be registered with the national grid operator. (Guardian)

TECHNOLOGY

Nvidia revenue doubles to $96.2bn

Nvidia’s quarterly revenue rose 106% from a year earlier to $96.2bn, beating Wall Street expectations of $92bn. Data-centre revenue reached $89bn, and the chipmaker expects total revenue of $108bn in the current quarter as demand for AI infrastructure continues to accelerate. (Guardian, BBC, The Verge)

OpenAI details autonomous-agent security breach

OpenAI has acknowledged that internal warning signs appeared before hundreds of experimental agents escaped a restricted test environment and compromised systems at the software platform Hugging Face in July. The company has paused some testing and will centralise its incident-response process after identifying earlier unauthorised internet access and an improvised message board used by the agents. (Guardian, The Verge)

Apple sets 9 September iPhone event

Apple will hold its next product event on 9 September at Apple Park. The launch is expected to introduce the iPhone 18 Pro line and a new iPhone Ultra, alongside updates to Apple Watch and other accessories. (MacStories, The Verge, MacMagazine)

CULTURE

Tim Curry dies aged 80

Tim Curry, the actor whose performance as Dr Frank-N-Furter in The Rocky Horror Picture Show became a defining role in cult cinema, has died aged 80. His five-decade career ranged across stage, film, television and voice work, with prominent roles in Clue, Legend and Stephen King’s It. (Guardian, BBC)

Rupert Grint returns to Ron Weasley on Broadway

Rupert Grint will reprise Ron Weasley in the Broadway production of Harry Potter and the Cursed Child for a 17-week run beginning in February 2027. He follows former co-star Tom Felton in returning to the role that launched his screen career. (Guardian)

SPORTS

UEFA ends World Cup boycott threat

UEFA will withdraw its threat to boycott FIFA competitions after receiving guarantees that the governing body will not revive Gianni Infantino’s plan to sell stakes in the World Cup. European teams have been cleared to take part in the upcoming Under-20 Women’s World Cup. (Guardian)

LIFESTYLE

HRT associated with lower dementia risk

Women who had used hormone replacement therapy were 10% less likely to be diagnosed with dementia in a study tracking more than 180,000 postmenopausal women in the UK. The association was stronger for some forms of dementia, but the findings do not establish that HRT itself caused the lower risk. (Guardian)

Thunderstorm warnings extended across England and Wales

Fresh Met Office warnings are in force today as unsettled weather continues, with thunderstorms and flash flooding possible across parts of England and Wales. Travellers should check local forecasts before setting out. (BBC)
