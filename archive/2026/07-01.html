NEWS

Starmer's defence plan became Burnham's first fiscal trap

The defence investment plan put £15bn of extra spending into the armed forces over four years, including equipment and capabilities that ministers said were needed to keep Britain safe. The political problem is timing. BBC Verify asked whether the figures get the UK to its Nato spending pledge, while the Guardian reported that Burnham could inherit a £4.7bn shortfall. The immediate news is a military plan; the larger story is that the next prime minister's room for manoeuvre is already being narrowed by bills signed on the way out. (BBC, BBC Verify, The Guardian)

No 10 North moved from rhetoric to real estate

Burnham's plan for a northern centre of government gained a practical outline, with a brownfield site on the edge of Manchester identified for the permanent base and an interim city-centre office expected first. That makes the devolution promise less abstract. The test is no longer whether “No 10 North” sounds like a clean break with Whitehall; it is whether money, staff and authority actually move with the nameplate. Guardian comment kept pressing the same issue from different angles: warmth and optimism will not be enough unless legislation and budgets follow. (The Guardian, The Guardian, The Guardian letters)

The US supreme court checked Trump on citizenship while widening the power argument

The supreme court upheld birthright citizenship, blocking a core part of Donald Trump's agenda on who automatically counts as American. The BBC's term-end analysis still found a mixed institutional picture: the court delivered heavy defeats to Trump in some areas while expanding presidential power elsewhere. The result is not a simple win-or-loss story. It is a boundary map, with citizenship protected in one ruling and executive authority still becoming more muscular across the term. (The Guardian, BBC)

Venezuela's rescue effort became a legitimacy test

Angry Venezuelans accused the government of negligence after the twin earthquakes, while rescuers pulled a three-year-old from rubble six days after the disaster. Folha added Brazil's role, reporting that the Brazilian navy had set up a field hospital in La Guaira with capacity for 150 patients a day. The human story is the child pulled alive from the debris. The political story is whether public institutions and regional neighbours can turn that one rescue into sustained shelter, treatment and recovery. (BBC, The Guardian, Folha Poder)

Brazil's pre-campaign politics hardened into money, tickets and court perks

Folha's Brazil feed showed the campaign taking shape before the formal campaign fully owns the stage. Lula released R$520m for government advertising in the first half of the year, more than double Bolsonaro's comparable 2022 spend. Gilberto Kassab agreed to run as Ronaldo Caiado's vice on an all-PSD presidential ticket, while Michelle Bolsonaro left PL Mulher and complained of attacks on allies. In the judiciary lane, the STF released part of the extras it had previously blocked and Fachin called for a structural review of public-sector payments. The day's Brazil thread is allocation: who gets money, visibility, office and protection before voters get a ballot. (Folha Poder, Folha Poder, Folha Poder, Folha Poder)

Britain's grid bill put clean power into household-scale terms

The energy system operator said rewiring Great Britain's electricity networks through the 2030s could cost £90bn, about 50% higher than earlier estimates. The reason is straightforward: clean power targets, rising demand and the physical job of moving electricity around the country are all becoming more expensive at once. This is the economy story behind the climate story. Net zero does not only need targets and turbines; it needs cables, planning consent, consumer patience and enough public trust to survive the bill. (The Guardian)

Food, homes and defence all pointed to price pressure

Sainsbury's said grocery inflation had not been as bad as feared so far, helped by price matching and a hot-weather, World Cup sales lift. That milder note sat beside harder cost stories: housebuilders face a class action claim worth up to £4.5bn over alleged collusion to inflate new-build prices, and the defence plan shifts civilian budget choices toward military spending. These are different markets, but the household reading is consistent: essentials are not calm just because headline inflation is less alarming. (The Guardian, The Guardian, BBC)

Consumer trust is becoming an economic asset

Martin Lewis described the fight against fake investment scams using his face and name as a battle he is losing, even though he argued the government could stop much of it with simple rules. Another Guardian money item had a driver paying £450 a month for an EV that would not charge, while the company refused to cancel the lease because it considered the car driveable. The shared issue is not only fraud or customer service. It is whether people can trust the market enough to make ordinary financial decisions without becoming forensic investigators first. (The Guardian money, The Guardian money)

The lesson is practical: AI's importance is now measured less by what it can generate and more by what institutions must repair, verify or defend after using it. Google pushed AI deeper into meetings, notes and short-form summaries

MacMagazine also covered Google's Nano Banana 2 Lite and short-video features for NotebookLM. That is a clear product direction: AI is being moved from a separate assistant into the surfaces where people already work, meet, study and skim.
