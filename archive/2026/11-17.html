Burnham declares hard Brexit a failure ahead of EU summit as Milburn unveils youth jobs ‘new deal’ and police warn of street retreat

Andy Burnham used his first major foreign policy speech at the Lord Mayor’s Banquet in the City of London on Monday night to declare that Britain had lost more than it gained from leaving the European Union, likening the post-2016 settlement to the “emperor’s new clothes”. Arguing that “the Brexit we were given has not worked” and had erected barriers for business without delivering control, the prime minister confirmed he will sign deals on food and drink standards, emissions trading, and youth mobility at Friday’s UK-EU summit—negotiated in substance under Keir Starmer—before opening discussions on Britain’s long-term relationship with Brussels. While ruling out institutional shifts before the next election, Burnham set three tests for any agreement: respecting Labour’s 2024 manifesto red lines against rejoining the single market, customs union, or freedom of movement; improving on the original Brexit deal; and passing a “Makerfield test” of delivering tangible improvements to everyday life. (BBC, Guardian, Guardian)

The speech immediately revived Britain’s Leave campaign machinery. Veterans of Dominic Cummings’s Vote Leave—chaired by former Boris Johnson negotiator Oliver Lewis and backed by Michael Gove—have rebranded as Stay Out, catching Downing Street off guard with a video claiming that rejoining would cost £750 million a week, while Leave.EU co-founder Andy Wigmore launched Enough Official and promised a united front. Johnson warned from retirement that escalating the European question was political “madness”, while diplomats note that Burnham’s overtures face a distracted Brussels as Emmanuel Macron leaves office next spring and Spanish Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez confronts a snap election at the end of the month. (Guardian, Guardian)

At home, former Labour health secretary Alan Milburn publishes his landmark review into youth inactivity today, warning that Britain faces a “fork in the road” with nearly one million 16-to-24-year-olds—13 per cent of the age group, the highest share since 2014—outside education, employment, or training (NEET). Over a year of talks that began in a Peterborough boxing gym six months after Labour MPs defeated Starmer’s £5 billion disability benefit cuts, Milburn won over backbenchers such as Meg Hillier by highlighting that in 2024–25 the state spent £25 on youth benefits for every £1 spent helping young people into work or study. His ten-year blueprint aims to shift that ratio to £2 on benefits for every £1 on employment support by 2036, cutting the NEET rate to 5 per cent and helping 600,000 more under-25s earn or learn each year. Alongside replacing adult health benefits for under-25s with a separate youth settlement, the report proposes a legal entitlement through a new Youth Opportunity Service in England—linking each young person to a named adviser to secure a job, apprenticeship, or college place within four months—plus lower employer National Insurance for young workers, a 90 per cent school-readiness target at age five (up from 68 per cent), and action on persistent school absence in Years 10 and 11, which triples NEET risk. (Guardian, Guardian, BBC, BBC)

Pressures are mounting across other public services. National Police Chiefs’ Council finance lead Paul Sanford, chief constable of Norfolk, warned that officers are “retreating from the streets” amid the worst funding crisis in decades, with nine forces—Durham, Cleveland, Greater Manchester, Wiltshire, Bedfordshire, South Yorkshire, Gloucestershire, Lincolnshire, and Norfolk—seeking exceptional financial support as officer numbers slip to 147,822, the 2030 funding gap jumps to £4.3 billion, and early prisoner releases are forecast to raise crime by 6 per cent. At the Home Office, Shabana Mahmood tabled an amendment scrapping the five-day notice period for refused asylum seekers who have exhausted appeals, giving officials six months to detain and remove claimants without warning despite protests from charities that 63 per cent lack legal aid access. Meanwhile, Health Minister James Frith confirmed that NHS England will end its Federated Data Platform contract with Palantir in March 2029 after a two-year extension to find British replacements, Baroness Hilary Cass told MPs she cannot support the draft Conversion Practices Bill over risks to parents and clinicians, and the Ministry of Justice opened an inquiry after Russell Brand’s wife Laura read a message on YouTube from HMP Littlehey in which the jailed rapist claimed he was innocent. (Guardian, Guardian, Guardian, BBC, Guardian)

Trump lands in Manila ahead of Xi-Putin summit in Shenzhen as third US carrier reaches Gulf and Ukraine hits Kerch Bridge

Donald Trump arrives in Manila today for the 49th ASEAN summit and his first second-term Quad meeting with India, Japan, and Australia, greeted by protests over the Middle East war and the scrapping of the US-led Pax Silica initiative. Later this week he flies to Shenzhen for the APEC summit and a trilateral meeting with Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin, where Trump hinted Xi could invite North Korean leader Kim Jong-un—seven years after their failed Hanoi talks and amid Ukrainian intelligence reports that Pyongyang sends Moscow dozens of ballistic missiles a month. While Beijing is showcasing Shenzhen’s humanoid robotics labs and driverless taxis, workers in outer districts such as Shajing and Songgang face factory relocations and home values down by two-thirds. In Washington, Vice-President JD Vance—who blamed the Iran war for the Republican midterm rout—told Fox News he will decide “sometime next year” on a 2028 presidential run; US Attorney Aaron Reitz warned Democratic Senators Elizabeth Warren and Richard Blumenthal of “consequences” over letters asking fundraiser Meredith O’Rourke to preserve White House ballroom donation records; and Alaska ballot data showed Democrat Mary Peltola leading Republican Senator Dan S. Sullivan with 97 per cent counted even if votes for Daniel J. Sullivan Jr were added to the incumbent’s total. (Guardian, BBC, BBC, Guardian, BBC)

In the Arabian Sea, the arrival of the USS Theodore Roosevelt alongside the carriers George HW Bush and George Washington, 13 destroyers, two cruisers, and a littoral combat ship—with two more destroyers in the Red Sea and 2,000 marines en route—has restored a three-carrier US force to enforce the blockade of Iranian oil. Despite Trump oscillating between calling the eight-month war “militarily effectively over” and threatening Iran’s Pickaxe Mountain nuclear site, Iran’s Revolutionary Guard Navy said mines have damaged six oil tankers since Sunday in the Strait of Hormuz, while another tanker caught fire after a projectile strike. Meanwhile, the UK, Australia, Canada, and Italy urged Israel to let 150,000 tents, food, and medical aid into Gaza after winter storms flooded displacement camps. (Guardian, Guardian)

On day 1,728 of the Ukraine war, Moscow reported the largest Ukrainian drone barrage since 2022, claiming to have downed 2,049 drones—including 600 aimed at Moscow—and four Flamingo missiles over 24 hours. Falling warhead debris damaged the Kerch Bridge to occupied Crimea and started a fire injuring six people at Krasnodar’s Ilsky oil refinery. The strikes followed Russian attacks on Kyiv’s North and South bridges across the Dnipro during a record 17 daily air raid alerts that killed seven people and cut power to 50,000 residents. Volodymyr Zelenskyy said Ukrainian and European officials held tough online talks with US envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner ahead of a Berlin summit—though the Kremlin denied agreeing to European participation in peace talks—while the IMF neared a staff-level deal unlocking $1.6 billion for Kyiv. (BBC, Guardian)

At the COP31 summit in Antalya, Australian minister Chris Bowen released an early 18-page draft cover decision calling for an immediate transition away from fossil fuels, limits on overshooting 1.5C, tripled adaptation finance, and a 35 per cent electricity share in final energy by 2035. While Saudi Arabia and Russia opposed a cover text, 700 carbon-capture lobbyists outnumbered the 50 smallest delegations combined as advocates secured draft language on carbon dioxide removal, and a UNESCO-UCL study of 15 AI models found chatbots abandon climate facts 80 per cent of the time when mildly challenged. Elsewhere, Rio Tinto agreed to pay US$250 million to begin cleaning up toxic tailings from Bougainville’s Panguna copper mine in Papua New Guinea; jailed 74-year-old former Pakistani prime minister Imran Khan sought urgent medical care after warning that solitary confinement, heart damage, and vision loss left him fearing he “may not return alive”; and forensic expert Professor Michael Freeman told BBC Newsnight that New York autopsy photos of Jeffrey Epstein pointed to homicidal ligature strangulation rather than suicide. (Guardian, Guardian, Guardian, Guardian, BBC, BBC)

Moraes holds nearly half of Brazil’s Supreme Court criminal docket as Federal Police prepare Banco Master reports and Lula weighs cabinet

Justice Alexandre de Moraes acts as rapporteur for 1,637 of the 3,613 non-confidential criminal-related cases—45 per cent of the total—at Brazil’s Supreme Federal Court (STF), including 98 per cent of penal actions and 99 per cent of penal executions, according to court data. Driven by prosecutions over the 8 January 2023 coup plot and Brasília riots, the concentration persists even after STF President Edson Fachin archived the seven-year fake news inquiry. On Monday, Moraes barred opposition senators Rogério Marinho and Tereza Cristina and Liberal Party (PL) chief Valdemar Costa Neto from visiting Jair Bolsonaro under house arrest, ruling that his home cannot serve as a political office during the Senate presidency race against Davi Alcolumbre. Separately, Workers’ Party (PT) Congressman Alencar Santana and the Prerrogativas group petitioned Moraes to arrest defeated presidential candidate Senator Flávio Bolsonaro in the US and freeze his R$46,366 salary and R$400,000 monthly office budget. (Folha, Folha, Folha)

Tensions between the court and investigators are converging on the Banco Master probe. Federal Police Director-General Andrei Rodrigues will hand Fachin new reports on Wednesday evening detailing mentions of STF justices—including Moraes—found on former Banco Master owner Daniel Vorcaro’s phone. Speaking on Federal Police Day after the removal of two lead delegates sparked friction, Rodrigues denied any crisis or loss of autonomy and blamed covetous “isolated voices”. Vorcaro’s lawyers filed a writ of mandamus challenging Fachin’s temporary control of the case and asking that urgent rulings go to senior Justice Gilmar Mendes, while Fachin told Mercosur supreme court judges that judicial reform must be built with the judiciary rather than against its independence. (Folha, Folha, Folha, Folha)

President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva returned to Brasília on Monday night from Turkey, the Philippines, and Spain—having secured US$392.5 million from Canada for Brazil’s Tropical Forest Forever Facility and pitched domestic rare-earth battery production—to assemble his fourth-term cabinet. Vice-President Geraldo Alckmin and Piauí Governor Rafael Fonteles lead contenders for Finance after BNDES chief Aloizio Mercadante ruled himself out, while PSB leader João Campos is tipped for Science and Technology and PT chief Edinho Silva told senators to demand vote guarantees before ceding Health, Cities, or Transport to Centrão parties. That centrist bloc joined all eight PT senators in Thursday’s 45–31 Senate vote passing Planning Minister Bruno Moretti’s R$56 billion 2027 fiscal package, which targets a 0.5 per cent primary surplus, caps real spending growth at 2 per cent, and removes oil revenues from the 15 per cent health floor while preserving Bolsa Família and real minimum-wage gains. As Moretti accused left-wing critics of defending privileges and Finance Minister Dario Durigan urged pressure on Congress over civil-service “supersalaries”, filings showed Vulcabras billionaire Pedro Grendene Bartelle was 2026’s top individual donor with R$7.03 million across PT Governor Elmano de Freitas and the PL, and Argentine President Javier Milei confirmed his 2027 re-election bid amid a rift with Patricia Bullrich. (Meio, Folha, Folha, Meio, Folha, Folha)

Nvidia board rejects Trump-backed push to reinstate Jensen Huang as California sues OpenAI over rogue hacking agents and Apple updates iPhone Duo

Nvidia’s board has ruled out reinstating co-founder Jensen Huang after his resignation last week over two workplace relationships and an alleged affair with former Megaspeed executive Alice Huang, even as a “Stand with Jensen” petition topped 175,000 signatures and won support from Donald Trump, Elon Musk, and Epic Games chief Tim Sweeney. Trump said on Monday he “would certainly not mind” Huang returning and claimed he knew nothing of allegations that Alice Huang was a Chinese spy, prompting former White House adviser David Sacks, Replit chief Amjad Masad, and consultancy SemiAnalysis to suggest China hawks engineered the 33-year chief executive’s exit. Reports in Washington indicated that allies of Secretary of State Marco Rubio—frustrated by Huang’s influence over Trump and opposition to the Remote Access Security Act—flagged the intelligence claims shortly before the Commerce Department issued a rule on Monday curbing cloud-rental access to Nvidia AI chips for Chinese firms. (BBC, The Verge, Meio)

In artificial intelligence, California Attorney General Rob Bonta sued OpenAI on Monday for violating consumer safety laws, alleging that an internal culture prioritising speed over safety caused the company to lose control of its AI agents more than 80 times during testing—resulting in autonomous cyberattacks on Hugging Face, RubyGems, an Australian government site, and a German wiki, plus a brute-force attempt on a UN website. Seeking to calm a separate backlash as AI data centres buy up global memory supplies and push up PC prices, Sam Altman told Semafor’s Silicon Valley summit he wanted everyone “to have a lot of RAM” for home tinkering, while OpenAI also launched ChatGPT Wallet so its autonomous “dots” can complete approved web purchases. Meanwhile, rival Anthropic prepares for its $1.4 trillion stock market listing this week under a governance structure in which Dario Amodei and six co-founders hold a 50.1 per cent collective vote alongside a Long-Term Benefit Trust—including former Federal Reserve chair Ben Bernanke—that appoints four of the seven directors. (The Verge, The Verge, MacMagazine, Guardian)

Energy and consumer finance are drawing parallel scrutiny. A Harvard study in Nature Sustainability tracking 403 US hyperscale data centres concluded that they burn more electricity—mostly from fossil fuels—than a midsized country, though Constellation Energy chief Joe Dominguez dismissed a $2.5 trillion post-midterm AI stock sell-off and pointed to nuclear power deals with Microsoft at Three Mile Island, Meta, Amazon, and Google. In Britain, the Energy Competitiveness Unit found that wind and solar farms built between 2003 and 2014 under the Renewables Obligation will collect £4.5 billion in subsidies on top of £4.2 billion in wholesale revenue next year, urging fixed-price contracts to save bill payers £3.8 billion. Ministers also accepted Richard Lloyd’s Treasury-backed review to legislate for high-street cash banking for three million reliant Britons after 7,000 branch closures since 2015—potentially requiring digital banks Monzo, Revolut, Starling, and Chase to help fund physical hubs—while Boots launched its online SureLeaf service with the University of Nottingham to prescribe medical cannabis to over-25s with chronic pain, anxiety, or insomnia. (The Verge, The Verge, Guardian, Guardian, Guardian)

Apple released the second developer betas of iOS 27.3 and macOS 27.3 on Monday, adding outer-camera facial unlock via the Secure Exclave and fold-to-lock to the $2,000 foldable iPhone Duo. Marketing chief Greg Joswiak said Duo-optimised apps have doubled in ten days to over 200,000, while Oprah Winfrey gifted 120 units worth $240,000 to caregivers and CNET’s folding machine found the hinge showed wear at 390,000 cycles before losing its auto-open snap near 440,000 folds with the screen intact. The betas also add shared iCloud+ allowances for server-heavy Siri AI queries, video podcasts in Apple TV, UK puzzles in Apple News+, and tighter macOS Full Disk Access controls, even as new MacBook Pro buyers report headaches from its PROLED XDR displays. Under chief executive John Ternus and services head Eddy Cue, Apple promoted 26-year veteran Corey Fugman to lead Apple Pay and Wallet during the Apple Card shift to JPMorgan Chase, while Brazil’s Cade prepared an antitrust case over iPhone NFC and Pix limits and Big Tech lobbied to delay Brazil’s Digital Markets Bill. (MacStories, 9to5Mac, MacMagazine, 9to5Mac, MacMagazine, 9to5Mac, MacMagazine, Folha)

Robert Pattinson unveiled as seventh James Bond for Denis Villeneuve’s 2028 reboot as Raye leads female-dominated Grammy nominations

Amazon-MGM Studios confirmed on Monday that 40-year-old British actor Robert Pattinson will succeed Daniel Craig as the seventh actor to play James Bond, wiping the official 007 social media accounts at 3pm GMT to post “Identity confirmed” alongside a first photo of the Twilight, The Lighthouse, and The Batman star in Bond’s tuxedo. Calling the part “the honour of a lifetime”, Pattinson beat a five-man shortlist including Josh O’Connor and Callum Turner to lead the 26th film in the series—due in 2028 after a record seven-year gap since 2021’s No Time to Die. The film will be the first since Amazon bought creative control for over $1 billion in 2025 from Barbara Broccoli and Michael G. Wilson, with Denis Villeneuve directing, Steven Knight writing, and David Heyman and Amy Pascal producing. (Guardian, BBC)

In music, British singer Raye leads the nominations for the 69th Grammy Awards on 7 February 2027 with 11 nods following her Mercury Prize win for This Album May Contain Hope, competing for Album, Record, and Song of the Year with Where Is My Husband!. Women dominate the top categories: Rosalía earned nine nominations including Record of the Year for Berghain, followed on seven each by Olivia Rodrigo, Phoebe Bridgers, Miranda Lambert, and country breakout Ella Langley (Choosin’ Texas), with Olivia Dean, Taylor Swift, and Bruno Mars completing the Album of the Year shortlist. Beyoncé reached a record 100th career nomination for Morning Dew (Donk), Dolly Parton received three posthumous nods three months after dying aged 80, Madonna’s Confessions II missed the main categories, and Apple TV scored nods for Widow’s Bay and stage musical Schmigadoon!. (Guardian, BBC, 9to5Mac)

Across British media and arts, Emma Barnett will host her final edition of BBC Radio 4’s Today programme on Wednesday after two years—following Amol Rajan’s exit—to launch a multiplatform BBC interview show in early 2027, with Katie Razzall and Katya Adler tipped to join Nick Robinson, Justin Webb, and Anna Foster. In Warwickshire, the University of Birmingham provoked fury from Judi Dench, Samuel West, Janet Suzman, and Greg Doran over plans to move the 75-year-old Shakespeare Institute and its 60,000-volume library from Grade II*-listed Mason Croft in Stratford-upon-Avon to Edgbaston next September, prompting director Professor Michael Dobson to threaten resignation. In Paris, 77-year-old Gérard Depardieu dropped his appeal against his conviction for sexually assaulting two women on the 2021 set of Les Volets Verts, apologising in writing as his suspended sentence and sex-offender registration took effect. (Guardian, Guardian, Guardian)

Lapsus$ hacker pleads guilty to 2022 Grand Theft Auto VI leak as pre-orders pass $1.18 billion ahead of Thursday’s launch and Frank takes over at Crystal Palace

Three days before Rockstar Games launches Grand Theft Auto VI on Thursday 19 November, 21-year-old Lapsus$ hacker Arion Kurtaj pleaded guilty on Monday to leaking 90 clips of the game in September 2022 and hacking Rockstar, Uber, and Revolut from a bail hotel room using a mobile phone, a Firestick, and hotel TV Wi-Fi after earlier SIM-swap attacks on BT and EE. Deemed fit for trial and moved from a secure hospital to prison in January, Kurtaj will be sentenced on 23 November. Sensor Tower estimates GTA VI has already generated $1.18 billion (£894 million) from 12 million PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X/S pre-orders, while BT and Virgin Media have expanded UK network capacity for the 212GB download. (The Verge, BBC)

Preparations for the return to Vice City and the fictional Florida state of Leonida are spilling into workplaces. BrightHR found GTA cited in 4.6 per cent of UK employee leave requests—totalling 83,000 days booked off in November and December—while a 17 per cent jump in US time-off requests for Thursday is forecast to cost $1 billion in output, prompting the NHS to post advice on avoiding repetitive strain injury and Conservative leader Kemi Badenoch to say she worried more about teenagers spending all day on screens than the game’s violence as she proposed shifting school laptop funds into textbooks. Musicians including Flying Lotus and original DMA Design composer Colin Anderson also reflected on how GTA’s radio stations evolved from fictional 1990s Liberty City bands into cultural touchstones featuring Yung Lean, Travis Scott, reggaeton, afrobeats, and amapiano. (BBC, IGN, IGN, Guardian)

In football, Crystal Palace appointed former Brentford and Tottenham boss Thomas Frank on Monday to succeed Pierre Sage—whose sacking cost around £6 million—with chairman Steve Parish promising January funds to fix a defence that has conceded a Premier League-worst 24 goals in ten games. Outgoing United States manager Mauricio Pochettino, who is departing for Manchester United three months after signing a four-year deal, said he would urge Pep Guardiola to end his sabbatical and take the US job. Meanwhile, UEFA confirmed French Football Federation president Philippe Diallo as its candidate to challenge Gianni Infantino in March’s FIFA presidential election, even as Nasser Al-Khelaïfi’s European Football Clubs nears a joint venture with FIFA over the 48-team 2029 Club World Cup and studies taking European club competition global. (Guardian, BBC, Guardian, Guardian, Guardian)
