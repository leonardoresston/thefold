Overloaded routers trigger longest US nationwide flight ground stop since 9/11 as Microsoft apologises for global 365 outage

The US Federal Aviation Administration lifted the longest nationwide ground stop in American aviation since 11 September 2001 on Friday evening after a telecommunications failure across all Air Route Traffic Control Centers halted takeoffs for over four hours. Ordered at 3.54pm Eastern Time and lifted at 8.15pm, the freeze delayed over 9,100 flights and cancelled nearly 8,000 while leaving 3,500 airborne aircraft to land or divert. At the FAA’s request, Eurocontrol suspended US-bound departures across its 42 member states—including the UK, EU, Ukraine, Turkey and Israel—while Sydney Airport grounded transpacific flights. (Guardian, BBC, The Verge)

Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy and Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin ruled out a cyber-attack or any connection to the week’s Microsoft disruption, blaming ageing analogue switches and routers in Terminal Radar Approach Control facilities that overloaded with data and fed inconsistent radar and radio information to controllers. The failure began in Chicago before cascading to Boston, Washington, New York and Minneapolis after a reported update and reboot on contractor L3Harris’s private network. Air Space Intelligence denied any link to its new $875m cloud-based SMART system, while Duffy urged Congress to fund a full replacement of US air traffic infrastructure. (Guardian, The Verge)

The aviation halt capped a day of digital paralysis after Microsoft apologised for a nearly 24-hour global outage across Microsoft 365—hitting Outlook, Teams, Word, Excel, SharePoint, OneDrive and Copilot—that generated 130,000 incident reports across 74 countries. Serving 430 million users and over 90% of Fortune 500 firms, the platform suffered server configuration errors during a capacity upgrade that caused machines to fail automated health checks and knocked out connectors used by OpenAI and Anthropic. In the UK, NHS hospitals, GP surgeries and schools faced disruption, several police forces asked the public to report crimes by telephone, and National Rail warned that emailed tickets might be inaccessible. (Guardian, BBC, The Verge)

Worldwide, the Microsoft failure stalled legislative business in the US House of Representatives, disrupted rail alerts and hospital email in Canada, and hit government systems in Japan, Singapore, France, Australia and the Philippines. In Brazil, Supreme Federal Court President Edson Fachin, acting as head of the National Council of Justice, granted an emergency request from the Brazilian Bar Association to suspend procedural court deadlines until Monday 16 November after state courts in São Paulo and Santa Catarina reported widespread tool failures. (BBC, Folha)

Trump, Putin and Xi plan three-way nuclear talks in China as Europe warns of Russian peace traps and Iran targets US radar in Bahrain

Donald Trump, Vladimir Putin and Xi Jinping plan to hold an unprecedented trilateral summit next week on the sidelines of the Apec gathering in Shenzhen to discuss nuclear arms reduction and Iran, White House spokeswoman Olivia Wales announced on Friday. Trump will attend Tuesday’s Asean summit in the Philippines before arriving in China on Wednesday for a fourth bilateral meeting with Xi on Thursday, followed by the three-way talks with Putin. Although US officials expect no formal agreement, the meeting comes nine months after the last US-Russia nuclear treaty expired in February, leaving the largest atomic arsenals without guardrails as Trump seeks a pact covering Beijing. (BBC)

Ukraine is not on the Shenzhen agenda, and at an EU summit in Dublin on Friday foreign policy chief Kaja Kallas warned Western capitals against Russian traps after Moscow used Abu Dhabi talks to revive its December 2021 demands to halt Nato enlargement and roll back forces in eastern Europe. German Chancellor Friedrich Merz and European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen rejected any overhaul of Europe’s security architecture, while European Council President António Costa announced a joint European negotiating team combining the EU with Britain, France, Germany, Italy and Poland. In Kyiv, Volodymyr Zelenskyy said speed curbs on SpaceX’s Starshield network persist along the entire front line, hampering Shahed drone interceptions as Russian strikes hit a Kyiv hospital and killed three people in Zaporizhzhia and Odesa, while car bombs in Moscow and St Petersburg killed a Russian colonel and injured former Ukrainian aide Sergei Zavorotniy. (Guardian, BBC, Guardian)

In the Gulf, Iran’s Revolutionary Guards claimed they destroyed a US early-warning radar in Bahrain in retaliation for a US strike on a tower in Sirik overlooking the Strait of Hormuz, though US Central Command said it intercepted two Iranian drones. While Iran’s Assembly of Experts urged a nuclear doctrine review, Trump disclosed that Saudi Arabia asked him to hold back from major strikes and that he told Vice-President JD Vance to drop Iran peace diplomacy. A YouGov poll found 65% of Britons and 79% of 2024 Labour voters oppose resuming the war, with 54% against US strikes from UK bases, even as US Air Force crews responded to suspected drones over RAF Lakenheath on Friday night. In Israel, prime ministerial designate Gadi Eisenkot accused Benjamin Netanyahu and Itamar Ben Gvir of waging a racist campaign against Arab Israeli voters and MPs. (Guardian, Guardian, BBC, Guardian)

Met Police open criminal inquiry into David Walliams as probation officers vote for industrial action and No 10 weighs wider benefit reforms

Metropolitan Police detectives from the complex sexual offences unit have opened a criminal investigation into comedian and children’s author David Walliams after several people came forward this week alleging non-recent serious sexual offences dating back to the 2000s. Led by Detective Superintendent Mike Cagney, the inquiry examines complaints separate from the grooming and harassment claims published earlier this week after Walliams lost a High Court bid to block coverage, as bookshops and parents clear his books from shelves. Walliams, 55, who is in a medical facility and has not been arrested, denies breaking the law. Meanwhile, Culture Secretary Lisa Nandy has sent rules for cabinet approval ahead of a December vote extending Britain’s under-16s social media ban to Depop, eBay and Facebook Marketplace—and enforcing age checks on Vinted—after warnings that predators use them to solicit children’s worn underwear and scrape photos for AI deepfakes. (Guardian, BBC, Guardian, Guardian)

Across the justice system, probation union Napo voted by up to 87% for overtime bans from 23 November and up to 75% for strikes after rejecting a 5% pay offer and protesting over caseloads swollen by prison early releases. Around 2,000 inmates have been freed since 1 October—rising to 2,500 by year-end, with nine further cohorts including prisoners serving over 12 years due out by June—even after inspectors found nearly half of electronic tags were fitted late. In Whitehall, Prime Minister Andy Burnham and Work and Pensions Secretary Pat McFadden are weighing whether to expand Alan Milburn’s “participation first” overhaul of under-25 disability benefits to claimants over 25 alongside the Stephen Timms PIP review, despite clashes over drawing £6bn in training funds from health and education budgets. (Guardian, BBC, Guardian)

In Westminster, Reform UK spent over £150,000 from party funds in the past year on a security team and two cars for Nigel Farage after he refused a Home Office bodyguard, prompting Labour and the Conservatives to question Farage’s claim that Christopher Harborne’s £5m gift was for his personal security. Ahead of Burnham’s 20 November UK-EU summit, Lord Gove and Oliver Lewis launched the Stay Out campaign, claiming EU re-entry would cost £39.3bn a year. In the courts, Eleanor Donaldson, 60, wife of jailed ex-DUP leader Jeffrey Donaldson, was placed on the sex offender register for five years for aiding his child sex offences; and former Hamas bodyguard Mohammed Alfatih, 40, was convicted of a Birmingham knife attack while awaiting extradition to Germany over a plot against Israelis. (Guardian, BBC, Guardian, Guardian)

Brazilian Senate approves fiscal adjustment package as rejected police report links Supreme Court justices to Banco Master owner

Brazil’s Senate approved the base text of President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva’s fiscal adjustment bill by 45 votes to 31, backing measures projected by Planning Minister Bruno Moretti to save R$46bn to R$56bn (£6.2bn–£7.5bn) in 2027 before Tuesday’s vote on amendments and transfer to the Chamber of Deputies. Two-thirds of the adjustment—R$33bn to R$38.3bn—comes from curbing tax breaks and corporate tax credit offsets, which São Paulo industry leader Paulo Skaf called a R$40bn tax hike. While the economic team prepares a constitutional amendment to cut R$20bn from mandatory spending including the Fundeb education fund and Federal District fund, Workers’ Party leader Lindbergh Farias is drafting an alternative to tax banks, large fortunes and LCI and LCA bonds. (Meio, Meio, Folha)

Meanwhile, the crisis at the Federal Police (PF) and Supreme Federal Court (STF) deepened after details emerged from the report on Banco Master owner Daniel Vorcaro that PF leadership rejected before dismissing anti-corruption chiefs Daniel Mostardeiro Cola and Wedson Cajé Lopes. Phone messages show Vorcaro told associates in February 2024—three months before a May ruling—that justices Alexandre de Moraes and Kassio Nunes Marques would vote for his interests in a constitutional case (ADC 81) on opening private medical courses, where Vorcaro was a creditor to the Lutheran University of Brazil via Fundo Calêndula; Nunes Marques denied communicating with or voting for Vorcaro, while Moraes did not comment. A 2 October dispatch showed PF chief Andrei Rodrigues had forwarded STF President Edson Fachin’s request for findings before sacking the delegates, prompting the PF delegates’ union to call Rodrigues’s position unsustainable as Fachin and Justice Gilmar Mendes moved to bar police and military officers from STF chambers. (Folha, Folha, Meio)

Elsewhere in Brazil’s courts, Federal Judge João Paulo Pirôpo de Abreu ordered the government to invest R$597m (£80m) in Canudos, Bahia, as reparations for the Army’s 1896–97 massacre of Antônio Conselheiro’s community and the 1969 flooding of its ruins. Speaking in Rio de Janeiro, Fachin urged Lula to fill the Supreme Court seat vacated by Luís Roberto Barroso—after the Senate rejected attorney-general Jorge Messias—with the first Black woman in the court’s 135-year history. Electoral Court Justice Dias Toffoli also gave Lula’s campaign three days to explain subcontractor and travel records, while STF Justice Flávio Dino allowed companies to spread the new 42-hour workweek across five shifts of eight hours and 24 minutes without overtime pay during the transition starting on 16 December. (Folha, Folha, Folha, Meio)

Economy

Disclosures reveal Trump bought millions in Nvidia stock while loosening China chip rules as cabinet feud erupts over Megaspeed

Public financial disclosures show that Donald Trump bought millions of dollars in Nvidia stock immediately before and after his administration dismantled export restrictions on selling advanced AI chips to China. Following Jensen Huang’s resignation as Nvidia chief executive on Thursday and the appointment of chief financial officer Colette Kress as interim head, anti-corruption group DeKleptocracy found that Trump bought $1m to $5m (£760,000–£3.8m) of Nvidia shares in May 2025 days before rescinding a Biden-era rule restricting AI chip exports to Beijing. After Trump announced in December 2025 that Nvidia could sell H200 processors to China—approved in January 2026 despite warnings that the chips were six times faster than Chinese alternatives—he made at least three more Nvidia share purchases of $250,001 to $500,000 each. (Guardian, The Verge)

The fallout has sparked cabinet infighting, with Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent blaming Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick on Friday for failing to blacklist Megaspeed, the Singaporean data centre firm run by Huang’s former partner and suspected Chinese operative Alice Huang. Bessent noted ahead of Lutnick’s Senate testimony next month that US agencies had pushed for months to blacklist Megaspeed to halt chip leakages above the H100 threshold. In Singapore—which accounted for nearly a fifth of Nvidia’s billing last year although under 1% of those chips physically entered the country—authorities confirmed investigating Megaspeed since February 2025 over suspected false export declarations. Greenpeace also urged Kress to curb Nvidia’s supply-chain emissions, up 725% since 2020 to 10.7m metric tons of CO2, with chip electricity use generating 21m metric tons annually. (BBC, BBC, The Verge)

IEA declares ‘age of electricity’ at Cop31 as Jaguar Land Rover posts £451m loss and UK insurers face climate subsidence surge

The world has left fossil fuel dominance behind and entered the “age of electricity”, International Energy Agency chief Fatih Birol declared on Friday at the Cop31 climate summit in Antalya, Turkey. Driven by a 60% jump in electric car sales outside China over the past nine months, AI datacentres and surging air-conditioning demand, global power capacity is set to grow by 12,000 gigawatts over the next decade—more than double the 5,000GW added in the past ten years—as 185 nations debate a target of deriving 35% of final energy from electricity by 2035 despite Saudi and Russian opposition. As world leaders departed Antalya, outgoing UN Secretary-General António Guterres used his farewell Cop speech before stepping down in December to warn that peace and poverty reduction are impossible on a burning planet. (Guardian, Guardian)

In UK manufacturing, Jaguar Land Rover reported a £451m quarterly loss after £530m in restructuring charges to cut 4,000 jobs, even as revenues rose to £5.9bn. Chief executive PB Balaji urged the UK and EU to scrap 10% “rules of origin” tariffs due on 1 January to counter Chinese EV competition as JLR rolls out its electric Range Rover and finalises a US manufacturing deal with Stellantis. Meanwhile, insurers Aviva, Axa and Hastings Direct—where claims jumped 140% in August—reported a surge in domestic subsidence claims averaging a record £20,000 after two record-hot summers shrank clay soils beneath foundations, with the British Geological Survey warning 4.2 million UK homes face subsidence risk by 2070. (Guardian, Guardian)

In regional finance, unions and employers in Bournemouth—where 2,500 firms employing 24,000 staff, led by JP Morgan’s 5,300-worker campus processing $1tn (£750bn) daily, provide 10% of Dorset’s output—warned that banks are cutting entry-level and compliance roles after 5,000 AI-linked cuts at Barclays and 7,000 at Standard Chartered. Elsewhere, all 197 people aboard survived when the tanker Atlantic Asphalt lost steering on the Mississippi River in New Orleans and T-boned the cruise ship American Heritage, hospitalising 32 with minor injuries; the US Supreme Court agreed to review Trump’s border asylum ban; and Hungary seized $19.5m (£14.8m) in cash buried in forests and safes during a graft probe into former minister Miklós Seszták. (Guardian, Guardian, BBC, Guardian, Guardian)

AI decodes hundreds of lost theorems in Kurt Gödel’s shorthand notebooks as SpaceX launches Starship Flight 15 and OpenAI rolls out agent wallet

Researchers at Helsinki University have used Anthropic’s Claude AI to unlock hundreds of previously unknown proved theorems inside 16 unpublished notebooks written in the early 1940s by Austrian logician Kurt Gödel, whose 1931 incompleteness theorems laid the foundations of modern computing. Led since 2017 by Finnish logician Jan von Plato under a €2.5m (£2.1m) European Research Council grant, the team spent years transcribing Gödel’s 1,009 pages of obsolete Gabelsberger German shorthand at a page a week before feeding them into Claude, which produced a 650-page digest in days revealing 14 unexplored themes and results anticipating discoveries from the 1970s and 1980s. In Texas, SpaceX launched its 407ft (124m) Starship on Flight 15 to deploy 26 Starlink satellites over 14 orbits, though it aborted the first half of a double-catch test when the Super Heavy booster splashed down in the Gulf of Mexico ahead of Saturday’s upper-stage catch attempt. (Guardian, Guardian, BBC)

Across the AI sector, OpenAI launched ChatGPT Wallet for US Pro subscribers holding Amex, Chase, Mastercard or Visa cards, using biometric checks and tokenised credentials so its “Dots” agents can buy items online without exposing card numbers. Rival Amazon is building a “World Product Graph” slated for October 2027 to index every product sold online, aiming to keep shoppers and AI bots on its site while fixing errors in Alexa for Shopping. Meanwhile, Google released Gemini 4 Hydrogen—running 2.5 times faster than Gemini 3.8 Flash at $1.50 per million input tokens and $7.50 per million output tokens alongside Carbon and Argon in Antigravity—while Anthropic added Claude Sheets, Claude Whiteboards, Google Meet note-taker Claude Meetings, and connectors for Adobe, Atlassian and Box. (The Verge, 9to5Mac, The Verge, MacMagazine, MacMagazine)

Apple began deliveries on Friday for its $299 HomeView smart display—a 7-inch Siri AI hub with an A18 chip, 12MP camera, Face ID profile switching and a 0.3-nit display—alongside the $299 A19-powered Apple TV 4K and the first LG and Schneider doorbell and cameras in the Apple Home Collection. Early Apple TV 4K buyers hit a YouTube app block fixed by installing tvOS 27.2.1—which also brings Siri AI to older Apple TV 4K models—and reinstalling the app, while filings showed Apple preparing to launch the HomePod mini 2 in Brazil. In Washington, Epic Games filed its Supreme Court response urging justices to uphold the contempt ruling against Apple over its 27% fee on external payment links. (9to5Mac, MacStories, 9to5Mac, The Verge, MacMagazine, 9to5Mac)

Grand Theft Auto VI’s 212GB pre-load exposes UK broadband blackspots as Robert Pattinson leads James Bond race and Ireland unseals banned books

Ahead of Rockstar Games’ release of Grand Theft Auto VI next Friday, 19 November, after a 13-year wait, a Broadband Genie study of 17,000 speed tests warned that 870,000 UK gamers on connections of 2.8Mbps or slower face download waits of over a week for its 212GB pre-load on PS5 and Xbox, led by Newport where 35% of tests fell below that mark. Rockstar has guarded the game—which pairs protagonists Jason and Lucia, runs at 30fps and includes an in-game animated TV series—so closely that original 1997 GTA co-creator Dave Jones and writer Brian Baglow confirmed in Dundee they are waiting on standard pre-orders. Elsewhere, Sony rolled out Quick Spectral Super Resolution (QSSR) AI upscaling on base PS5 consoles for Marvel’s Spider-Man 2, with updates following for Arc Raiders, The Finals, No Man’s Sky, Control Resonant and Hell Let Loose: Vietnam. (IGN, BBC, IGN, IGN)

In film and literature, Robert Pattinson has emerged as Amazon MGM’s frontrunner ahead of Nicholas Hoult, Callum Turner, Jack Lowden and Josh O’Connor to succeed Daniel Craig in the 26th James Bond film—directed by Denis Villeneuve and written by Steven Knight—under a reported three-film deal, while an original Darth Vader lightsaber prop sold at auction for nearly $5m. In Dublin, Ireland’s Department of Justice is moving 12,000 books from its closed “banned books room”—outlawed between 1926 and 1996, including works by Edna O’Brien, Oscar Wilde, Brendan Behan, DH Lawrence, Ian Fleming and Iris Murdoch—alongside censors’ ledgers to the National Archives. Tributes were also paid to British-born journalist and author Simon Winchester, who died aged 82 after reporting on Bloody Sunday, Watergate and the Falklands and writing 30 books including The Surgeon of Crowthorne. (Guardian, Guardian, Guardian, Guardian)

Sports

Tearful Mauricio Pochettino leaves US national team for Manchester United as Crystal Palace sack Pierre Sage and Arsenal hold Barcelona

Mauricio Pochettino broke down in tears at US Soccer’s training centre on Friday after Manchester United paid roughly $10m (£7.5m) in compensation to appoint him as Michael Carrick’s successor until June 2029. Although Pochettino signed an extension in August to lead the US through the 2030 World Cup—with part of his $6m salary funded by billionaire Ken Griffin—he said United first made contact on Sunday with an offer too difficult to refuse. He will coach the US in Saturday and Tuesday’s Concacaf Nations League quarter-finals against Haiti before starting at Old Trafford on 18 November, where former Spurs defender Toby Alderweireld warned players to expect a fitness-obsessed “control freak”. Elsewhere in the Premier League, Crystal Palace sacked French manager Pierre Sage in an 83-word statement six months after hiring him, with Palace averaging 43.2% possession and sitting bottom for goals from high turnovers. (Guardian, BBC, Guardian)

In the Women’s Champions League, Arsenal earned a 1-1 draw against reigning European champions Barcelona at the Camp Nou on Thursday night in a match delayed by 24 hours because of Wednesday’s general strike in Spain. Playing in the No 10 role behind Alessia Russo—who has 12 goals in nine games for club and country—Frida Maanum put Arsenal ahead in the first half after Russo beat two defenders on the left and crossed for Caitlin Foord, whose shot was parried by Cata Coll into Maanum’s path. Caroline Graham Hansen equalised after half-time, but Renée Slegers’s low block held firm before 1,000 travelling fans to keep Arsenal fourth in the Champions League table and inflict a rare home draw on the Spanish champions. (Guardian)
