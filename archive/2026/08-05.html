LEAD STORY

Iran’s president fought a resignation row as Hormuz talks advanced

Masoud Pezeshkian denied claims that he had repeatedly threatened to resign as hardline factions resisted a settlement with the US over the Strait of Hormuz. Iran’s presidential office and the supreme leader’s office rejected reports of a rupture at the top of the state. (Guardian)

The US and Europe expect an agreement to reopen the waterway, while Oman fears the proposed temporary shipping routes could leave Iran with enduring control over the oil corridor. The dispute inside Tehran now runs alongside the final bargaining over a deal. (Guardian)

NEWS

Electronic tags are missing possible probation breaches

The system intended to monitor thousands of prisoners released early is failing to alert authorities automatically when conditions may have been broken. England and Wales’s probation inspector said police and probation staff must search individual cases and decide which incidents need investigation, leaving a growing scheme dependent on an understaffed process. (Guardian)

Fuego’s eruption forced hundreds from their homes

Guatemala issued its second-highest alert after the Fuego volcano sent lava down its slopes and ash almost four miles into the sky. Hundreds of people evacuated communities around Central America’s most active volcano as the eruption intensified into Monday night. (Guardian)

MI6 led a European intelligence ranking

A peer review by 60 intelligence professionals from 25 countries placed Britain’s Secret Intelligence Service ahead of its French and Dutch counterparts. MI6 scored highest for recruiting senior informants, sustained operations and access to ruling circles in Russia, China, Iran and Turkey. (Guardian)

Unproven cancer remedies reached vulnerable patients

Animal dewormer fenbendazole and the scabies treatment ivermectin were sold as cancer treatments despite the absence of clinical evidence. An undercover purchase in south Wales yielded a two-to-three-month supply for £100; Amazon removed separate listings after being contacted, while doctors warned that the products could cause harm or delay conventional care. (BBC)

Brazil investigated a suspected election-period attack plan

Police searched eight people suspected of planning attacks on the Palácio do Planalto and other government buildings in Brasília during October’s election period. The operation placed an alleged threat to the seat of the presidency inside an already tense campaign calendar. (Folha)

The TSE ordered Lula publicity off government channels

Electoral court president Kassio Nunes Marques gave the presidency 24 hours to remove official posts that he found amounted to concealed institutional advertising centred on Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva. The order tightened the boundary between government communication and campaigning days after Lula entered the race. (Folha)

The third Lulinha inquiry was authorised

Supreme court justice Flávio Dino approved a third investigation into Fábio Luís Lula da Silva, the president’s son, over suspected influence peddling and opened a separate inquiry into leaks. The case followed disclosures that a businesswoman under investigation transferred money to a former Lula aide, who described it as a repaid personal loan. (Folha, Meio)

Republicanos chose neutrality in Brazil’s presidential race

Seventeen state branches backed neutrality at the party’s national convention, nine favoured an alliance with Flávio Bolsonaro and one supported Lula. The decision denied Bolsonaro another national coalition partner and deepened the pressure on his campaign in south-eastern Brazil. (Folha)

Brazil ended compulsory retirement as judges’ harshest penalty

The National Council of Justice confirmed that judges found responsible for serious misconduct will no longer receive compulsory retirement as the maximum sanction. The change removes a punishment that allowed disciplined judges to leave office with retirement benefits. (Folha)

Aécio Neves ended a 40-year run in elected office

The PSDB’s national president decided not to contest any post in Brazil’s October election. The former presidential candidate and Minas Gerais governor has held elected office continuously for four decades. (Folha)

Eight oil majors made $93bn in three months

The Iran war’s effect on energy prices helped eight of the world’s largest oil companies collect more than $90bn (£67bn) in quarterly profit. The windfall revived calls for producers including Saudi Aramco and BP to pay more towards climate damage and the transition to renewable energy. (Guardian)

HSBC’s £7.5bn quarter revived a bank-tax campaign

HSBC’s second-quarter profit rose 60% from a year earlier to $10.1bn, helped by wealth-management fees, insurance and higher interest rates. Campaigners said a windfall levy on UK banks could raise £19bn for cost-of-living support. (Guardian)

Used electric-car leasing rose 170%

More British drivers turned to secondhand EVs as petrol prices climbed and the supply of used electric cars expanded. Leasing across members of the vehicle rental and leasing trade body increased 170% in a year, opening a cheaper route away from petrol without a new-car purchase. (Guardian)

TECHNOLOGY

Telegram returned to the App Store after a brief removal

Apple removed the messenger after child sexual abuse material was reported in a public chat, then restored it after Telegram deleted the content and banned the account. Pavel Durov said an extortionist had planted altered material to trigger the takedown; Apple said the content violated its store rules. (The Verge, MacMagazine)

Electronic Arts became a private company

The $55bn acquisition led by Saudi Arabia’s Public Investment Fund, Silver Lake and Affinity Partners closed, taking the games publisher off the public market. The Saudi fund is expected to own 93.4% of the company behind EA Sports FC, The Sims and Battlefield. (The Verge)

AI infrastructure drove AMD’s data-centre surge

AMD’s data-centre revenue reached $6.7bn in the latest quarter, up 107% from a year earlier and ahead of the previous quarter’s $5.8bn. The performance placed demand for AI computing at the centre of the chipmaker’s growth while gaming became a smaller part of the business. (The Verge)

SpaceX’s AI revenue overtook its space business

Revenue from supplying computing capacity to AI companies more than tripled to $2.6bn from a year earlier. Quarterly figures ahead of SpaceX’s planned public listing showed its AI division producing more revenue than its space operations. (The Verge)

Apple began work on shared clipboards with Windows

Apple moved Microsoft’s request for cross-device copy and paste into development under the EU’s interoperability rules. The planned system would pair an iPhone and Windows PC once, then sync compatible clipboard content; a developer beta is expected in late 2027, with wider availability likely in 2028. (MacMagazine)

Signal added secure linking for extra phones

One Signal account can now connect to an additional iPhone or Android handset as well as PCs and iPads. Linked phones can send and receive new messages under the same number, extending the service’s multi-device system to the devices people carry most. (The Verge, MacMagazine)

CULTURE

The Odyssey pushed UK poetry sales towards a record

Poetry sales passed £8m in the first 30 weeks of 2026, 13.3% above the same period last year, as Christopher Nolan’s film sent readers to Homer’s 2,700-year-old epic. The market is now on course for its strongest year. (Guardian)

Star Wars received its first Welsh-language dub

S4C will broadcast the 1977 film in Cymraeg with a cast including Rhys Ifans, Matthew Rhys, Aimee-Ffion Edwards and Mark Lewis Jones. The production gives Luke Skywalker, Han Solo and Princess Leia Welsh voices for the first time. (Guardian)

Vinyl editions left parts of new albums behind

Fans who pre-ordered physical records from artists including Charli xcx and Olivia Rodrigo have received versions that differ from the finished digital releases. Charli xcx’s vinyl album omits David Cronenberg’s spoken contribution to its closing track, exposing the cost of pressing records before late production changes are complete. (Guardian)

SPORTS

Fifa faced a blackmail claim over Infantino’s re-election

Jordan’s football association president, Prince Ali bin al-Hussein, accused Fifa of threatening to withhold development funds unless his federation backed Gianni Infantino. Infantino summoned senior leaders to an executive meeting as criticism also mounted over his failed plan to sell stakes in Fifa’s commercial and World Cup operations. (Guardian, BBC)

Vinícius began pre-season under Mourinho amid Arsenal interest

Vinícius Júnior returned to Real Madrid training with one year left on his contract and speculation continuing over an Arsenal move. The Brazil winger gave an upbeat account of his first sessions under José Mourinho, leaving his future unresolved as the transfer window advanced. (Guardian)

LIFESTYLE

Blushing works as an involuntary social signal

Humans appear to be the only animals whose faces redden with embarrassment, producing a signal that cannot be performed on command. Researchers say the visible reaction can demonstrate sincerity after a social mistake and help restore trust, even when the person experiencing it would rather disappear. (Guardian)

A £7 ice-cream cone carried the summer’s cost pressures

Milk, chocolate, diesel and electricity have all become more expensive for Britain’s ice-cream vans, pushing the price of a 99 with a Flake as high as £7 in some locations. Climate shocks and energy costs now reach the customer through every part of the familiar summer treat. (Guardian)
