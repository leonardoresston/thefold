LEAD STORY

Norway mourns King Harald V

King Harald V of Norway has died in Oslo aged 89, ending a 35-year reign. Europe’s oldest reigning monarch was remembered by thousands who gathered outside the royal palace as the country began a period of national mourning. (Guardian)

His son succeeded him immediately as King Haakon VIII. Harald, the first Norwegian king born in the country since 1370, had opened the royal palaces to the public and built a reputation for moderation and accessibility. (Guardian)

NEWS

Thirty-three Britons missing after Nepal floods

Thirty-three British nationals are missing after the floods that devastated valleys along the Nepal–Tibet border. A 13-year-old girl and a 14-year-old boy are among those unaccounted for, while rescue teams monitor a newly formed barrier lake that could unleash another flood. (BBC, BBC)

Russia’s monthly battlefield losses exceed recruitment

Russia is losing about 6,000 more troops in Ukraine each month than it can recruit, according to western officials. Moscow is still bringing in an estimated 1,000 recruits a day, but casualties have outpaced that flow for the past two months. (Guardian, BBC)

Drumcree ruling revives Northern Ireland tensions

The high court has overturned the latest restriction on an Orange Order march past the nationalist Garvaghy Road in Portadown. The decision has reopened a dispute that triggered years of sectarian confrontation before settling into a de facto truce two decades ago. (Guardian)

Four admit destroying Jewish community ambulances

Four men have pleaded guilty over an arson attack that destroyed four Jewish community ambulances in Golders Green and caused about £1m of damage. Prosecutors allege that the vehicles were deliberately targeted in the March attack. (Guardian)

Brazil’s election court demands answers from X

Brazil’s Superior Electoral Court has ordered X to explain failures in an algorithmic filter after a request from President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva’s campaign. The platform must now respond to the court during the final stretch of the election campaign. (Folha)

UK petrol reaches 161.6p a litre

Average petrol prices have risen to 161.6p a litre, their highest level in almost four years, as the bank holiday getaway begins. Diesel now averages 183.4p a litre, adding to household and business transport costs. (Guardian)

Asda records first growth in more than two years

Asda’s sales at established stores, excluding fuel, rose 0.2% in the seven weeks to 18 August. The supermarket had recorded a 2.3% fall over the preceding three months and credited lower prices and an improved online service for the turnaround. (Guardian)

TECHNOLOGY

US court overturns Pentagon blacklist of Anthropic

A federal judge has ruled that the Pentagon’s blacklisting of Anthropic was unconstitutional. The AI company had accused the Trump administration of retaliating after it set limits on military uses of its technology. (The Verge)

Google expands AI summaries above search links

Google has begun automatically opening full AI Overviews at the top of some search results. The expanded summaries sit above an “Ask anything” box and push the conventional list of links farther down the page. (The Verge)

ChatGPT and Codex support multiple Google accounts

ChatGPT and Codex can now connect to more than one Google account through their app integrations. The change lets people reach separate personal and work services without repeatedly disconnecting and reconnecting accounts. (MacMagazine)

CULTURE

Erykah Badu returns with first album in 11 years

Erykah Badu is returning with her first album in 11 years. Before the World Blows, made with producer the Alchemist, extends a career that has moved across soul, hip-hop, acting and her work as a birth and death doula. (Guardian)

Dark Matter begins its second season

The second season of Apple TV’s multiverse thriller Dark Matter has premiered with Joel Edgerton and Jennifer Connelly returning. The opening episode expands the story beyond the parallel lives and duplicate identities of its first season. (The Verge, MacMagazine)

SPORTS

Manchester City beat Crystal Palace 4–1

Rayan Cherki and Erling Haaland scored twice each as Manchester City won 4–1 at Crystal Palace in the Premier League. Cherki’s first goal broke open a match that Palace had kept competitive before City pulled clear in the second half. (Guardian, BBC)

LIFESTYLE

Doctors agree first universal heart-attack definition

Four leading cardiovascular health groups have adopted the first universal definition of a heart attack. The guidance recognises overlooked causes and introduces sex-specific diagnostic thresholds intended to improve treatment for women. (Guardian)

UK salmonella outbreak reaches 474 cases

The UK’s salmonella outbreak has reached 474 confirmed cases and one death, with infections expected to rise until the source is found. Investigators believe imported eggs served in cafes and restaurants may be responsible. (Guardian)

Restored Cheshire wetland becomes grebe stronghold

Woolston Eyes in Warrington now hosts 64% of Britain’s breeding population of black-necked grebes. Since 2021, the former dumping ground for Manchester Ship Canal dredgings has been restored as freshwater pools, reedbeds, willow scrub and grassland. (Guardian)
