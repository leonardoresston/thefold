LEAD STORY

Burnham put Ukraine first in his opening foreign-policy test

Volodymyr Zelenskyy will become Andy Burnham's first international visitor since entering Downing Street, meeting the prime minister on Monday at a UK naval base where Ukrainian forces have been trained. The visit gives the new government an immediate defence and security marker after Keir Starmer's final foreign trip as prime minister was to Kyiv. (Guardian, BBC)

Burnham is using the meeting to reaffirm British support for Ukraine at the start of his international calendar. The choice of a naval base ties the new government's first diplomatic set-piece to training, weapons and operational continuity. (Guardian, BBC)

NEWS

The US held back from striking Iran again

The United States paused attacks on Iran for a second consecutive night while talks continued on a permanent end to the war. Senior military advice to Donald Trump pointed to a campaign near the limit of its effectiveness and to pressure on munition stocks, leaving Benjamin Netanyahu's expected White House visit on Tuesday to land in a narrower diplomatic window. (Guardian, BBC)

German police killed the Berlin Pride attack suspect

German police shot dead Abdul Ballout, 21, during an operation in Berlin's Spandau district after the fatal attack near Pride. Authorities said the suspect had driven a van into a crowd and then carried out a knife rampage; police said he was killed after rushing at officers with a knife. (Guardian, BBC)

West Bank attacks widened after a deadly clash

Palestinians said Israeli settlers set fire to mosques, cars and farm land in the West Bank. The attacks followed a clash near the village of Tal two days earlier that left four Palestinians and two Israelis dead, adding another flashpoint to already strained security on the occupied territory. (BBC)

Brazil escalated the dispute with Milei

Brazil summoned Argentina's ambassador after Javier Milei attacked STF minister Alexandre de Moraes at the PL convention that launched Flávio Bolsonaro's presidential candidacy. Fernando Haddad also criticised Milei and turned the row back toward Brazil's electronic voting system, keeping the diplomatic dispute inside the election campaign. (Folha, Folha, Folha)

The convention's use of an AI video of Jair Bolsonaro also drew legal pressure. Allies of Lula asked the PGR to investigate whether the clip and the event breached restrictions imposed on the former president. (Folha, Folha)

Social care moved back into the tax argument

Kemi Badenoch offered to work with Burnham on adult social care reform but told him to rule out tax rises to fund it. The letter puts the first cross-party opening on care inside the larger fight over how the new government pays for early domestic promises. (BBC)

Miliband took the World Bank seat himself

Foreign secretary Ed Miliband is taking Britain's seat on the World Bank board, a role usually delegated to a junior minister. The move places climate and development policy directly inside the government's foreign-policy machinery as poorer countries push for changes to the global lender. (Guardian)

The World Cup gave pubs a £150m lift

Late opening hours and England's run to the semi-final delivered the strongest football-linked uplift for pubs in recent years. The British Beer & Pub Association said pubs poured 30m extra pints during the tournament and took about £150m in additional sales. (Guardian)

The Burnham bounce met weak household finances

UK consumer confidence rose at its fastest monthly pace for almost three years in June, helped by the change in Downing Street, sunshine and England's World Cup run. The rebound still sits beside fragile spending and falling savings among poorer households, leaving the recovery dependent on whether wages and bills start moving in the same direction. (Guardian)

Brazil's transport lobby put R$2tn on the table

The CNT is sending presidential candidates a 10-point agenda for transport and infrastructure that calls for R$2tn in investment. The proposal puts roads, logistics and public works back into the economic programme debate as Brazil's campaign calendar opens. (Folha)

TECHNOLOGY

Apple's smart-glasses pitch moved toward privacy

Apple is preparing its first smart glasses for a possible WWDC reveal next June and a launch expected by the end of 2027. The company is working on privacy features and messaging as it tries to separate its device from Meta's camera-first wearables. (The Verge)

A border phone-wipe case reached federal court

US prosecutors charged citizen Sam Tunick after his phone was allegedly wiped by a duress password during a border search at Atlanta's Hartsfield-Jackson airport in January 2025. The case puts phone access, compelled searches and digital self-protection into a criminal proceeding. (The Verge)

macOS Golden Gate's glass refinements kept rolling

Apple's next macOS visual cycle continued to take shape through refinements to Liquid Glass and other interface details. The update keeps the desktop release focused on polish as much as headline features. (MacMagazine)

CULTURE

Marvel reset two headline roles

David Jonsson will play the new Black Panther in Ryan Coogler's third film in the series, appearing as the son of Chadwick Boseman's T'Challa alongside Letitia Wright and Winston Duke. Marvel also used Comic-Con to announce Ryan Gosling as Ghost Rider in a Shawn Levy film planned for 2028. (Guardian, The Verge)

V&A staff balloted over heat and pay

Workers at the V&A are balloting for strike action over pay, access to drinking water and conditions during heatwaves. The union wants the museum to become an accredited London living wage employer and to let staff carry water during shifts. (Guardian)

Jessica Morgan inherited Tate's repair list

Jessica Morgan, a former Tate curator and head of the Dia Art Foundation in New York, takes over Tate after Maria Balshaw's nine-year tenure. Her arrival comes with visitor, budget and morale pressures across an institution still able to draw large audiences for major shows. (Guardian)

Osamu Dazai found a new generation

The bleak mid-century novels of Osamu Dazai have become a publishing surprise in Britain more than seven decades after his death. No Longer Human, completed shortly before he died in 1948, has sold more than 75,000 copies in the UK as Japanese translated fiction keeps expanding beyond recent cosy categories. (Guardian)

SPORTS

Chiesa asked for a new Liverpool chapter

Federico Chiesa said he is happy at Liverpool but wants a fuller role under Andoni Iraola after two frustrating seasons under Arne Slot. The forward scored in a friendly win against Sunderland and remains linked with a possible return to Italy. (Guardian, BBC)

Chelsea's Xhaka pursuit was shut down

Sunderland manager Régis Le Bris said Chelsea's pursuit of Granit Xhaka is closed and insisted the club will not sell its captain. Xhaka helped Sunderland qualify for the Europa League after promotion back to the Premier League, while Chelsea's new manager Xabi Alonso had wanted a reunion with his former Bayer Leverkusen midfielder. (Guardian)

Tottenham faced Romero talks and a record tour crowd

Inter opened talks with Tottenham over Cristian Romero, who captained Spurs and helped Argentina reach a second straight World Cup final. Tottenham's pre-season tour also began with a 2-0 win over Auckland FC before 40,112 people, a record crowd for a men's football match in New Zealand. (Guardian, Guardian)

LIFESTYLE

England's life-expectancy gap widened

Life expectancy in England reached a record high in 2025, with men living for about 80 years and women for nearly 84 years on average. The King's Fund found that the gains were concentrated in the south, while northern regions faced more illness from heart disease, cancer, suicide and deaths linked to alcohol and drug misuse. (Guardian)

New homes entered the heat debate

The Liberal Democrats called for all new UK homes to be built with air conditioning as extreme heat becomes a housing issue. The party also backed passive cooling measures such as more trees in residential areas, arguing that Britain's housing stock is poorly prepared for higher temperatures. (Guardian)

Fake Spotify emails became a payment trap

Fraudsters are sending fake Spotify payment emails that lead users to cloned pages collecting login, personal and card details. The scam works because renewal notices can look routine when subscription cards are already close to expiry. (Guardian)

Functional drinks filled the supermarket shelf

Electrolyte lemonades, probiotic pops and caffeinated soft drinks are moving from wellness marketing into ordinary meal deals. The new cans promise focus, calm, energy or digestive benefits, leaving shoppers to separate useful ingredients from bright-label claims. (Guardian)

Shingles rose among younger women

Doctors and patients are paying closer attention to shingles among women in their 30s and 40s, with stress, long working hours, cost pressures and intense exercise all raised as possible contributors. The illness can bring severe pain, blistering and weeks of disrupted sleep. (Guardian)
