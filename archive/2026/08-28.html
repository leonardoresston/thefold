LEAD STORY

Nepal–Tibet flood toll reaches 469

At least 469 people have been killed and more than 1,300 remain missing after flash floods swept through valleys along the Nepal–Tibet border. Entire villages, bridges and roads were destroyed as a torrent of water, mud and debris cut a 64-kilometre path through the Himalayan region. (Guardian, BBC)

International aid is moving towards the stricken communities while authorities monitor a new lake formed at the disaster site, which is rising and could burst. Damaged transport and communications are slowing searches across the remote border area. (Guardian)

NEWS

Ratko Mladić dies in prison aged 84

Ratko Mladić, the Bosnian Serb commander convicted of genocide for orchestrating the 1995 Srebrenica massacre, has died in prison in The Hague aged 84. He was serving a life sentence for crimes committed during the Bosnian war, including the killing of at least 8,000 Muslims at Srebrenica. (Guardian, BBC)

Russia threatens British military targets

Russia has threatened retaliation against British military targets inside and outside Ukraine after the UK agreed to share classified technology for Kyiv to produce Storm Shadow long-range cruise missiles. The warning came alongside reports that the CIA director used a secret visit to caution the Kremlin against attacking Nato. (Guardian)

Sex-offence specialists promised in every police force

Every police force in England and Wales will have dedicated rape and sexual-offence specialists by 2027 under an accelerated government timetable. The national rollout had been due to finish in summer 2029. (BBC)

Brazil seeks R$500m from Discord

Brazil’s attorney-general has filed a lawsuit seeking R$500m from Discord for alleged collective moral damage and ten breaches of national law. The government wants immediate changes to age verification, parental supervision and content moderation; Discord’s video functions remain suspended in Brazil while messages, servers and voice calls continue to operate. (Meio)

Brazilian leaders strike pre-election voting pact

President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva and the heads of Brazil’s Chamber and Senate have agreed to accelerate government priorities before October’s election. Congress will first consider a measure ending the levy on low-cost imported purchases before it expires on 8 September, followed by proposals on public security and ending the six-day working week. (Meio, Folha)

UK household energy debt reaches £6bn

Household energy debt in Britain reached a record £6bn by the end of June and is forecast to hit £7bn by the end of the year. The government cap on gas and electricity prices is due to rise to a three-year high in October, adding pressure to struggling households. (Guardian)

TECHNOLOGY

Meta agrees $17.1bn US child-safety settlement

Meta will pay up to $17.1bn to settle claims brought by dozens of US states over child privacy and consumer-protection laws. The agreement will impose a two-hour daily limit on Instagram and Facebook for teenagers, block use between midnight and 6am and restrict beauty filters and like counts for younger users. (Meio)

Claude Cowork gains an in-app browser

Anthropic has added an integrated browser to Claude Cowork on macOS, allowing the assistant to interact with websites from inside the desktop app. The browser uses the Mac’s local session and can work with sites where the user is already signed in. (MacStories, MacMagazine)

ChatGPT adds custom iPhone widgets

ChatGPT for iOS now supports customisable Home Screen widgets for ChatGPT Work and Codex Remote. Users can choose the shortcuts shown in the widget, creating one-tap routes into frequent work and coding actions. (MacStories)

CULTURE

Yayoi Kusama dies aged 97

Yayoi Kusama, the Japanese artist whose polka-dotted paintings and infinity-mirror installations made her one of the world’s most recognisable contemporary artists, has died aged 97. Her seven-decade career also spanned sculpture, film, performance, fiction and poetry. (Guardian, Meio)

BBC prepares to revive Top Gear

Top Gear will return to the BBC after four years off air. The motoring programme was suspended after presenter Andrew Flintoff suffered serious injuries in a crash during filming at Dunsfold aerodrome in Surrey; its new presenters have yet to be announced. (Guardian)

Bronze Age gold stolen in four-minute raid

Thieves have stolen part of the Treasure of Villena, a collection of 66 mostly gold Bronze Age objects valued at more than €1.7m, from a museum in eastern Spain. The carefully planned raid took about four minutes and targeted one of Europe’s most important prehistoric troves. (Guardian)

SPORTS

Arsenal draw Real Madrid and Bayern Munich

Arsenal will host Real Madrid and travel to Bayern Munich in the Champions League league phase. Mikel Arteta’s side will also face Borussia Dortmund, Real Betis, Lille, Napoli, Sabah and Slavia Prague as they seek a first European Cup after losing last season’s final to Paris Saint-Germain on penalties. (Guardian)

Uefa prepares criminal complaint against Infantino

Uefa is preparing a criminal complaint in Switzerland against Fifa president Gianni Infantino over alleged criminal mismanagement connected to the failed plan to sell stakes in the World Cup. It has also applied in the United States for documents from Fifa and investment firm Thrive Capital. (Guardian, BBC)
