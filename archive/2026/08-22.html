LEAD STORY

TikTok agrees $400m settlement over children’s privacy

TikTok and its former parent company ByteDance have agreed to pay $400m to settle a US government lawsuit over the collection of children’s personal data. The justice department accused the companies of allowing under-13s to create accounts without parental consent and failing to delete their information when asked. (Guardian, BBC, The Verge)

The agreement resolves a case filed in 2024 under the Children’s Online Privacy Protection Act. It is among the largest US settlements involving children’s online privacy and places fresh pressure on social platforms to verify age and honour parents’ deletion requests. (BBC, The Verge)

NEWS

Three governments condemn decision over Gaza aid convoy killings

Britain, Australia and Canada have condemned Israel’s decision to forgo a criminal investigation into the April 2024 airstrikes that killed seven World Central Kitchen workers in Gaza. Citizens of all three countries were among those killed when the convoy was struck. (Guardian)

Powerful El Niño brings warning of wetter British autumn

The Met Office expects the developing El Niño to become the strongest in more than a century. Britain faces increased autumn rain and storms, while the heat released across the Pacific could help push global temperatures temporarily beyond 1.5C above preindustrial levels and make 2027 another record-hot year. (Guardian)

MPs declare almost £400,000 in hospitality

Members of parliament have accepted almost £400,000 in concert, sports and event hospitality this year. The declarations include Harry Styles tickets worth £858, Chelsea flower show tickets worth £1,839 and £3,432 of hospitality at the Brit awards. (Guardian)

Fourth member of family dies after Shoreham sea tragedy

Six-year-old Saja El-Khawas has died in hospital three days after being pulled from the sea off Shoreham in West Sussex. Her sister Sara, 14, and parents Hassan El-Khawas and Zeina Alayn also died after the family got into difficulty in the water on Tuesday. (Guardian, BBC)

Lula leads Flávio Bolsonaro as campaign begins

Lula has 39% of first-round voting intentions against 33% for Flávio Bolsonaro in the first Datafolha poll since Brazil’s presidential campaign formally began. Lula leads a possible second round by 47% to 43%, while the share judging his government bad or terrible has risen from 38% to 41%. (Folha, Folha)

Lobbyist’s messages draw Lula deeper into federal inquiry

Messages obtained by Brazil’s federal police show lobbyist Roberta Luchsinger claiming that Lula offered her a government post, which she declined to pursue business with the president’s son. Investigators also say she paid R$217,000 in card bills, vehicle finance and other expenses for a former Lula chief of staff while seeking government business. (Meio, Folha)

Unexpected July deficit tightens budget arithmetic

The UK government borrowed £1.8bn in July, a month when tax receipts usually produce a surplus and economists had expected the books to balance. Public debt reached £2.98tn, equal to 94% of GDP and £96bn higher than a year earlier, as chancellor John Healey prepares his first budget. (Guardian)

Retail sales fall despite heat and World Cup demand

Retail sales volumes across Great Britain fell 0.5% in July even as hot weather and the men’s World Cup lifted demand for food and drink. June’s increase was revised down to 0.7%, following growth of 1.3% in May. (Guardian)

TECHNOLOGY

Apple cuts more than 200 Siri and Vision Pro jobs

Apple has laid off more than 200 employees, with the largest cuts falling on Siri and Vision Pro teams. The company is largely closing a Vision Pro gaming group and reducing the team that produces immersive content for the headset. (The Verge, MacMagazine)

Walmart finally opens its tills to Apple Pay and Google Pay

Walmart will introduce Apple Pay and Google Pay at selected US stores from 24 August, then extend tap-to-pay to every American location by the end of the year and to petrol stations in 2027. The retailer had resisted the payment systems for more than a decade while promoting its own app-based service. (The Verge, MacMagazine)

CULTURE

The Odyssey becomes the biggest R-rated film in history

Christopher Nolan’s adaptation of Homer’s epic has taken $1.35bn worldwide, overtaking Deadpool & Wolverine to become the highest-grossing R-rated film. The 2024 superhero film had earned $1.34bn. (Guardian)

Mike D returns with his first solo album

Mike D is releasing his first solo record after years away from music following the death of Beastie Boys bandmate Adam Yauch. The album arrives 40 years after Licensed to Ill made the New York trio global stars and explores mortality, greed and the legacy of their early work. (Guardian)

SPORTS

Arsenal begin title defence with a 3-0 win

Arsenal swept aside newly promoted Coventry at the Emirates in the Premier League opener. Kai Havertz scored after 15 minutes, Bukayo Saka added the second eight minutes later and Martin Ødegaard completed the 3-0 victory shortly after half-time. (Guardian, BBC)

LIFESTYLE

Baby foods remain too sweet a year after new guidelines

Almost a quarter of 224 branded infant foods and snacks sold by UK supermarkets contain excessive sugar. More than half breach at least one voluntary guideline introduced last August on sugar, salt, marketing to babies and health claims. (Guardian)

Vulnerable diners advised to avoid runny eggs

Pregnant women, children under five, over-65s and people with underlying health conditions have been advised to avoid runny eggs when eating out during a salmonella outbreak. The warning applies even to eggs carrying the British Lion mark when restaurants cannot confirm how they were stored and prepared. (BBC, Guardian)
