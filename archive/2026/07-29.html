LEAD STORY

Burnham put social care at the centre of his first domestic test

Andy Burnham moved his opening week in Downing Street onto one of Britain’s hardest domestic files, inviting Kemi Badenoch and Ed Davey into talks on urgent adult social care reform. The Conservatives will send shadow health secretary Stuart Andrew while Badenoch has minor surgery, and Davey is expected to join by video call. (Guardian)

The same governing push reached schools and youth employment. Pupils in England will be offered technical subjects from 14, including courses tied to AI, manufacturing and local jobs, while Pat McFadden set out support designed to stop families on benefits losing income when teenagers start apprenticeships. (BBC, Guardian, BBC)

NEWS

Wildfires pushed holiday sites into evacuation

Nearly 4,000 people were evacuated from campsites, holiday villages and tourist accommodation around Lacanau, west of Bordeaux, as fires continued across France and Spain. Firefighters in Gironde were braced for temperatures near 40C on Wednesday after wind and extreme heat complicated containment work. (Guardian, BBC)

Floods and typhoons tore through Asia

Heavy rainfall across Asia brought flash floods, typhoons and damaged homes from Afghanistan to Taiwan. In eastern Afghanistan, days of rain washed out roads, damaged crops and destroyed houses built from mud and stone, adding immediate losses to a wider pattern of extreme weather. (Guardian)

A US airman case stayed out of British courts

A US airman accused of rapes and sexual assaults in England avoided a UK trial after Suffolk police handed part of the investigation to the American military. James Loubeau was acquitted at court martial after British women alleged they had been drugged and raped following a night out in Bury St Edmunds. (Guardian)

Brazil’s Argentina dispute moved into the campaign

Brazil’s fight with Javier Milei deepened as the Planalto treated relations with Argentina as their most serious rupture in four decades. Milei kept attacking Lula online, Alckmin accused him of crude interference in Brazil’s election, and the dispute folded into Flávio Bolsonaro’s launch for the presidency. (Meio, Folha)

The legal front moved to the TSE, where Kassio Nunes Marques became rapporteur for an action over an AI-generated Jair Bolsonaro video shown at the PL convention. Flávio Bolsonaro’s campaign defended the video as lawful and said it did not mislead voters. (Meio, Folha)

US diplomats walked out at the UN

American diplomats left a UN security council meeting after France criticised the Trump administration’s human-rights record. The walkout came during a session on the Ukraine-Russia war and followed Washington’s opposition to the reappointment of the UN human-rights chief. (Guardian)

Biden tapes reopened a documents argument

Released audio from 2016 and 2017 appeared to show Joe Biden discussing sensitive information with the ghostwriter of his memoir and describing classified documents in his possession. No classified material was alleged to have appeared in the book itself. (Guardian)

Led By Donkeys won a protest payout

Led By Donkeys received £27,000 in compensation after a 40-metre Gaza protest banner in Parliament Square was seized by Greater London Authority officials and destroyed. Waste firm Veolia paid the settlement after the December 2024 banner removal. (Guardian)

GSK traded jobs for Cambridge research spending

GSK set out job cuts under a £1.9bn cost-saving plan while committing £400m to UK life sciences over three years. More than 1,000 scientists will move to a new Cambridge biomedical campus site, the Stevenage research site will close by 2029, and laboratories at Ware will be upgraded. (Guardian)

AI spending gave markets a harder number

Google lifted its capital-spending estimate to $195bn-$205bn, up from a prior ceiling of $190bn, putting a larger price tag on the AI buildout just as earnings season tested investor patience. The revision fed a wider selloff in AI and semiconductor stocks. (The Verge)

Apple crossed the $5tn line

Apple became the second company to pass a $5tn valuation as its share price rose on product demand and a lighter AI spending profile than many peers. The stock touched a session high of $342.89 before easing back near the threshold. (Guardian, MacMagazine)

TECHNOLOGY

New York age checks reached algorithmic feeds

New York finalised rules that will require age verification before users can access algorithmic social feeds or receive late-night notifications under the SAFE for Kids Act. The rule targets platforms such as Instagram and TikTok by putting age gates around recommendation systems. (The Verge)

AI workers pressed Washington on automated systems

Employees from OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Meta, Microsoft, Mistral, Thinking Machines and other AI labs signed a statement supporting stronger US action and global coordination on advanced automated AI. The call adds insider pressure to the debate over frontier-system governance. (The Verge)

Wikimedia’s union drive spread to the US

US employees at the Wikimedia Foundation joined a union drive after UK staff became the first group at the nonprofit to announce organising plans in June. Management declined voluntary recognition, setting up a formal labour fight inside the organisation behind Wikipedia. (The Verge)

Apple launched leasing with a payment-status caveat

Apple introduced Apple Upgrade, a hardware leasing programme for iPhones, iPads, Macs and Apple Watches offered with Klarna. The company also said devices obtained through the programme will not have capabilities restricted when a customer misses a payment. (MacMagazine, MacStories, The Verge)

WhatsApp brought calls to the web

WhatsApp added voice and video calls to its web version after more than a year of testing. The update extends a core mobile feature to browser sessions and gives desktop users a fuller messaging setup. (MacMagazine)

eBay settled its cyberstalking case

eBay and three former executives agreed to pay $55.7m to resolve litigation from a Massachusetts couple targeted in a 2019 harassment campaign over critical coverage of the company. The settlement closes one of the stranger corporate-abuse cases to reach the courts. (The Verge, Guardian)

CULTURE

The Booker longlist put former winners beside debuts

Douglas Stuart and Marlon James, both previous Booker winners, made the 2026 longlist alongside Elizabeth Strout and ten first-time longlistees. The 13 selected novels span seven nationalities, three continents and three debut novelists. (Guardian)

Betye Saar died at 99

Betye Saar, a pioneering artist of the Black arts movement, died in Los Angeles aged 99. Her assemblage work reclaimed Jim Crow-era objects as images of Black liberation and stretched across sculpture, printmaking and film. (Guardian)

Naga Munchetty left the BBC Breakfast sofa

Naga Munchetty will leave BBC Breakfast after 17 years to become a co-presenter of Radio 5 Live’s breakfast show. Rachel Burden and Rick Edwards, the current 5 Live Breakfast hosts, are due to step down in December. (Guardian)

Ariana Grande took leaked work to court

Ariana Grande filed a Los Angeles lawsuit against unidentified hackers accused of stealing and selling unreleased music, private photos and video footage. The case is designed to uncover the defendants’ identities before moving to claims over the leaks. (Guardian, The Verge)

Mike Leigh neared his final film

Mike Leigh said Tender Loving Care is likely to be his final film as he manages myositis, an immune-system condition. The announcement puts a closing note on a British film career shaped by sharply observed social drama. (Guardian)

SPORTS

Fifa’s World Cup rights plan drew a European backlash

Fifa’s plan to seek private investment in competitions including the World Cup triggered criticism from Uefa, leading European clubs and Andy Burnham. The proposal would sell commercial rights into a new venture, with Uefa considering its legal position. (Guardian, BBC)

Premier League squads kept moving

Manchester City winger Savinho is open to leaving the club and wants to join Tottenham, while Xabi Alonso played down Chelsea links to Danny Welbeck and Jordan Henderson after his first match in charge. Chelsea beat Western Sydney Wanderers 6-4 in that debut. (BBC, Guardian)

Hearts started with a Champions League exit

Wouter Vrancken’s time at Hearts opened with a damaging Champions League elimination, prompting former forward Ryan Stevenson to question the early direction of the side. The result gives the new manager an immediate European setback to absorb. (BBC)

LIFESTYLE

Heat made sleep harder to protect

Warm nights pushed sleep advice back into everyday health, with specialists warning that anxiety about rest can compound the physical strain of high temperatures. The practical advice centred on cooling the body, keeping routines simple and avoiding choices that make hot nights worse. (Guardian)

Comfort food beat salad in the heat

Waitrose saw cottage pie sales rise 110% over the past month while South African red wine sales rose 68% year on year, part of a summer swing toward familiar colder-weather food. Other gains included chicken korma, chicken and ham gratin, and retro pie dishes. (Guardian)

A late hotel arrival became a consumer warning

A couple who had booked a Travelodge room at London King’s Cross Royal Scot six weeks ahead were moved after arriving around 1am because the hotel had overbooked. They had called earlier to confirm an 11pm arrival and were told it would be fine. (Guardian)

An alcohol recovery story put the risk in plain sight

Holly Dyson described being given six months to live at 26 after years of drinking left her liver badly damaged and her body shutting down. Her account puts early alcohol harm, debt, withdrawal and recovery into a single personal case. (Guardian)
