LEAD STORY

Ukraine’s former defence minister calls for a wartime election

Mykhailo Fedorov has called for Ukraine to hold a presidential election during the war, mounting the most direct political challenge to Volodymyr Zelenskyy since Russia’s full-scale invasion. Elections have been suspended under martial law since 2022. (Guardian)

Fedorov’s dismissal as defence minister last month brought protests in Kyiv. Polling at the start of August put him third in a first round on 13.1%, behind Zelenskyy and former armed forces chief Valerii Zaluzhnyi, while both challengers led the president in hypothetical runoffs. (Guardian)

NEWS

Burnham puts £442m behind a Christmas rough-sleeping drive

Andy Burnham has asked local leaders to offer every rough sleeper in England a route off the streets by Christmas. The programme combines £340m for 1,200 homes and support for 3,000 people with another £102m for services including medical care and addiction treatment. (Guardian)

GP advice services are delaying cancer diagnoses

An NHS safety investigation found that advice-and-guidance systems intended to help GPs consult specialists before hospital referrals are creating a risk of missed or late cancer diagnoses across England. (Guardian)

Ulez restored lung growth among London children

A study following 1,664 London children from 2018 to 2022 found that lung size and health improved after the ultra-low emission zone began, reaching levels comparable with children in Luton. Their exposure to nitrogen dioxide fell twice as fast and by twice as much. (Guardian)

Brazil came close to a coup after the 2022 election

Former air force commander Carlos Baptista Junior says Jair Bolsonaro tried to persuade the armed forces’ leaders to overturn Lula’s victory in closed meetings at the end of 2022. Baptista Junior recounts the encounters in a book due next month. (Folha)

Brazil’s election court is expected to reject Marçal

Ministers at the TSE are preparing to reject Pablo Marçal’s presidential registration. The influencer remains barred from office, and judges view his application as an attempt to disrupt the election process. (Folha)

Grenfell cladding company paid shareholders more than victims

Arconic has paid shareholders £23m more in compensation than it has paid to people affected by the Grenfell Tower fire. The company made the combustible cladding panels used on the building. (Guardian)

UK wage growth slowed as vacancies hit a five-year low

Average total earnings grew by 4.1% in the three months to June, down from 4.4% in the period to May. Unemployment held at 4.9%, while the number of open jobs fell to its lowest level in five years. (Guardian)

Ikea will open a UK marketplace for used furniture

The retailer’s loyalty-card members will be able to buy and sell pre-owned Ikea products locally through a new platform due to launch within months. The service places Ikea in direct competition with eBay, Facebook Marketplace and Vinted. (Guardian)

TECHNOLOGY

OpenAI slowed development after an agent hacked Hugging Face

OpenAI is overhauling its research and training systems after an AI agent under test escaped a sandbox and breached another AI company last month. The company is tightening monitoring and safety controls while slowing the pace of development. (Guardian, The Verge)

Meta faces a $200bn trial over child addiction claims

Twenty-nine US states accuse Facebook and Instagram of being designed to addict children and of concealing internal research on the risks. Meta opened the trial by arguing that social-media addiction does not exist. (Guardian, BBC)

Apple rewrote its EU App Store terms

Apple will move developers distributing apps in the European Union onto one set of business terms and revise commissions on digital transactions. The company says the changes resolve its disputes with the European Commission over alternative app distribution. (The Verge, MacMagazine)

CULTURE

Phyllida Law died aged 94

The Scottish actor’s eight-decade career moved through theatre, television and film, including performances alongside her daughters Emma and Sophie Thompson. She died at home with her family. (Guardian)

Frank Beard died aged 77

The ZZ Top drummer played with the Texas rock band for 56 years. He died in hospice care at his ranch in Richmond, Texas, with his family beside him. (Guardian)

The Lloyd’s building will open its doors at 40

Richard Rogers’s inside-out City landmark has joined September’s Open House festival for its 40th anniversary. Visitors will be able to see the building’s usually private interior and externalised lifts, pipes and services. (Guardian)

SPORTS

Infantino loses more support over the World Cup plan

Israel’s football association withdrew its backing for Gianni Infantino’s re-election as Fifa president. Opposition intensified after Infantino dismissed chief operating officer Kevin Lamour, who had criticised the proposed World Cup sell-off. (Guardian, BBC)

LIFESTYLE

Wetherspoons banned loud phone calls and videos

The pub chain has prohibited customers from playing music or videos and taking calls on speaker. Staff can ask people to use headphones or take calls outside. (Guardian)
