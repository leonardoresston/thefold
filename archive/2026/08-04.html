LEAD STORY

Washington gave Tehran a final warning

Donald Trump said new talks with Iran would be Tehran’s last chance to reach a deal before US strikes escalate. He expected negotiations within a day or two on reopening the Strait of Hormuz and addressing Iran’s nuclear programme. (Guardian)

Iran disputed the premise, saying its discussions with Oman concerned the shipping route and did not amount to renewed talks with Washington. The conflicting accounts left diplomacy moving under the immediate threat of a wider war. (Guardian)

NEWS

Serious sexual offenders were removed from the early-release plan

Rapists, child sexual abusers and groomers will be excluded from measures intended to ease critical overcrowding in prisons across England and Wales. Prime minister Andy Burnham also delayed the planned release of thousands of other prisoners by one month, until October. (Guardian)

Ceuta’s leader accused Morocco of enabling the crossings

Juan Jesús Vivas described last week’s mass crossing into the Spanish enclave as an attack on Spain’s territorial integrity and said it had been encouraged or permitted by Rabat. Spanish officials said about 69,500 people had returned to Morocco and roughly 2,500 remained in Ceuta, while the EU called for stronger borders ahead of an emergency meeting. (Guardian, BBC)

Russia said Ukrainian drone attacks killed 11

Russian officials said attacks on a Black Sea resort and occupied Crimea killed 11 people. Seven, including three children, died after what the Krasnodar regional taskforce called drone debris struck a beach at Arkhipo-Osipovka; Moscow also blamed Ukraine for an explosion that killed five people in a restaurant. (Guardian)

Southern England recorded its driest month

July 2026 was the driest month in southern England since records began nearly 200 years ago. England and Wales provisionally had their driest July since 1836; Wales registered its joint-warmest July by mean temperature and England its second warmest. (Guardian, BBC)

Farage’s timeline reopened questions about a £5m gift

Nigel Farage acknowledged that he discussed returning as Reform UK leader months before the 2024 general election. The admission raised fresh questions over his earlier assertion that a £5m gift received while he said he was outside politics had no connection to his political career. (Guardian)

Lula launched his re-election campaign

President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva formally entered Brazil’s presidential race in São Paulo. He promised to challenge Congress over control of parliamentary-amendment spending in a possible new term, while his government programme is expected to reaffirm the fiscal framework. (Meio, Folha)

Brazil’s presidential contest narrowed

A BTG/Nexus poll put Lula on 41% in the first round and Flávio Bolsonaro on 37%, reducing their gap from nine points to four. Lula’s allies see difficulty expanding his support and fear the election will require a second round. (Folha, Folha)

A third Lulinha inquiry reached Brazil’s supreme court

The Federal Police asked the STF to open a third investigation into Fábio Luís Lula da Silva over suspected influence peddling. Justice Flávio Dino will decide whether the inquiry into the president’s son can proceed. (Folha)

Fachin proposed tighter conflict rules for judges

STF president Edson Fachin’s proposal would govern judicial attendance at events, the receipt of gifts and private activities, alongside a map of family connections that could compromise impartiality. The plan places the court’s own conduct inside a clearer disclosure framework. (Folha)

UK factory growth reached a near two-year high

Manufacturers increased production for a fourth consecutive month in July and at the fastest pace in almost two years, according to S&P Global’s survey. Factories remained upbeat as tariff disruption eased, while the risk of a longer Middle East war kept energy supplies and production costs under pressure. (Guardian)

AstraZeneca lost £17bn on merger talks

AstraZeneca shares fell 8.9% after discussions over a possible combination with Bristol Myers Squibb emerged. More than £17bn was erased from the British drugmaker’s value; a deal would create a pharmaceutical group worth close to $400bn and the world’s fourth largest. (Guardian)

TECHNOLOGY

Apple challenged Britain’s new iCloud access demand

Apple launched a case at the Investigatory Powers Tribunal against a fresh Home Office request for access to highly encrypted iCloud data belonging to British users. The complaint follows last year’s withdrawal of an earlier demand for a route into customer data. (Guardian, MacMagazine)

Europe’s AI labels took effect

The EU’s new transparency rules require companies to disclose when people are interacting with AI systems and when content has been generated or altered by them. Providers must build clear identification into their systems, while platforms deploying the technology face their own labelling duties. (The Verge)

Fitbit data can move directly into Apple Health

Google Health 5.05 adds direct transfer of Fitbit workouts, steps, vital signs and other data into Apple Health. The connection reverses the previous one-way arrangement and removes the need for third-party syncing apps. (The Verge, MacMagazine)

Microsoft prepared Xbox 360 games for PC

A developer document set out a programme for bringing Xbox 360 games to PC, the next-generation Project Helix console and handheld devices. The licensing work would allow older Xbox titles to follow players across a wider range of hardware. (The Verge)

CULTURE

Spider-Man delivered a $932m opening

Spider-Man: Brand New Day earned $932m worldwide in the second-biggest opening weekend on record. Its $360m North American total passed the unadjusted opening figure for Avengers: Endgame, giving the domestic box office a new record. (Guardian, The Verge)

Nolan sent readers back to Homer

Christopher Nolan’s The Odyssey has driven a rush for the nearly 2,700-year-old poem at US libraries and bookshops. Stores are assembling displays across translations, graphic novels and children’s editions as younger cinemagoers seek out the epic behind the blockbuster. (Guardian)

The Met will stage a John Galliano retrospective

New York’s Costume Institute will open a solo exhibition on John Galliano next May, 15 years after his conviction over racist and antisemitic remarks. The show will address both four decades of influential design and the conduct that ended his tenure at Dior. (Guardian)

Jimmy Cricket died aged 80

Northern Irish comedian Jimmy Cricket, a fixture of British television in the 1980s, died at 80. His family-friendly act, own ITV series and wrong-footed wellington boots made him a household name; he received a papal knighthood for charity work in 2015. (Guardian)

SPORTS

Arsenal opened talks for Bruno Guimarães

Newcastle rejected Arsenal’s first formal offer, worth £70m, for captain Bruno Guimarães. The Brazil midfielder joined Newcastle’s pre-season camp in Spain and met incoming manager Matthias Jaissle as the Premier League champions pressed their move. (Guardian)

Tzolis made his early mark at Arsenal

Christos Tzolis arrived from Club Brugge for £34m and topped one of Arsenal’s demanding pre-season running tests within his first week. The Greek winger returns to the Premier League after rebuilding his career following a difficult spell at Norwich. (Guardian)

European associations withdrew support for Infantino

The Football Association, Wales, Serbia and Finland withdrew their backing for Gianni Infantino’s proposed fourth term as Fifa president. Opposition intensified after his failed plan to sell private investors a stake in the World Cup and other competitions. (Guardian, BBC)

LIFESTYLE

Hard training carried a hidden surgery bill

Recreational athletes who train through pain can accumulate injuries that end in major surgery, from double hip replacements to ACL reconstruction. Longer recovery time, changing movement and persistent pain can signal an injury that needs investigation. (Guardian)

Britain’s piers widened their horizon

Seaside piers are adding fine dining, live music and contemporary design to the familiar arcades and fish and chips. Worthing’s new art deco Ebb restaurant sits 300 metres out to sea, while the floor below keeps the classic pier menu alive. (Guardian)
