LEAD STORY

Iran set new conditions for reopening the Strait of Hormuz

Iran said the Strait of Hormuz would remain closed until the United States changed its conduct, leaving talks over the strategic waterway without a deal. The warning came from the country’s supreme national security council as the United Arab Emirates said an Iranian missile had targeted one of its ships. (Guardian)

The continued closure keeps pressure on one of the world’s most important energy routes. Mohammad Bagher Zolghadr, the council’s secretary and an Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps commander, delivered Tehran’s conditions through state television. (Guardian)

NEWS

British Columbia declared an emergency as wildfire forced 20,000 to flee

A fast-moving fire destroyed homes and trapped residents around Okanagan Lake in western Canada. More than 100 wildfires were burning across the province, with nearly half out of control amid hot, windy weather and prolonged drought. (Guardian)

The Royal Navy spent more time shadowing Russian vessels

British patrols monitored Russian ships around UK waters for 21 days in July. The Navy said its activity in the Dover Strait and North Sea rose by 25% from the same month last year as Moscow increased its presence. (Guardian)

A US grant programme drew accusations of political interference in Britain

The American embassy in London offered grants worth up to $500,000 for public-education projects promoting shared US-UK values. The notice gave priority to programmes celebrating freedom of speech and limited government, prompting criticism that Washington was trying to export Maga politics. (Guardian)

Lula renewed a promise for a public security ministry

Brazil’s president put the proposed ministry in his re-election programme, making its creation conditional on a constitutional amendment that remains stalled in the Senate. The pledge was not fulfilled during Lula’s three terms in office. (Folha)

Brazil’s candidates filed new financial declarations

São Paulo governor Tarcísio de Freitas declared R$2.696m in assets as he registered to seek re-election. Lula’s filing showed a personal fortune R$2.6m below the amount declared for the 2022 presidential race. (Folha, Folha)

Train operators targeted digital ticket cheats

South Western Railway and GTR prepared a crackdown on passengers who buy the cheapest possible mobile ticket only as they approach their destination. The move away from paper tickets has made the last-minute practice, known as “donutting”, easier to detect. (Guardian)

Wasted medicine could fill 75 swimming pools a year

England disposed of an estimated 3,400 tonnes of partly used medicine in 2024-25, enough to fill 75 swimming pools. The National Pharmacy Association called for more regular reviews of repeat prescriptions to reduce the waste. (Guardian)

TECHNOLOGY

UK children faced a surge in explicit deepfakes

The Report Remove service recorded a rapid rise in sexualised images that had been manipulated or generated to resemble children. The safety service can block intimate images from circulating online, while watchdogs warned that AI nudification tools had made abuse easier to produce. (Guardian)

Demis Hassabis stepped back from daily control of Google DeepMind

The co-founder moved from chief executive to chair of Google’s AI division and became chief scientist at parent company Alphabet. The change placed new attention on DeepMind’s independence as its research moves deeper into Google’s commercial operations. (Guardian)

Amazon backed a gas plant to power a Texas data centre

The planned Pecos County power station could become one of the largest single sources of greenhouse-gas pollution in the United States. Amazon invested in its construction to supply a new West Texas data centre. (The Verge)

X replaced creator revenue sharing with original-content rewards

The platform will end its existing payment programme and launch Original Content Rewards on 8 September. Creators will need at least 500 verified followers and 500,000 home-timeline impressions from verified users to qualify. (The Verge)

CULTURE

The Mad King opened at the Royal Shakespeare Theatre

The Royal Shakespeare Company premiered its Game of Thrones prequel in Stratford-upon-Avon after five previews were cancelled. The decade-long project arrived 30 years after the first novel in George RR Martin’s series; all 870 seats for the run had sold out. (Guardian)

Phoebe Bridgers returned with her third solo album

Lost Weekend arrived six years after Punisher and brought Bridgers her first solo charting single in the UK. Her writing and production have become a reference point for a generation of pop musicians, from Olivia Rodrigo and Gracie Abrams to established collaborators including Taylor Swift and SZA. (Guardian)

SPORTS

Arsenal completed a £75m deal for Bruno Guimarães

The Brazil midfielder joined from Newcastle after more than four years on Tyneside. His ability to play deep or provide creativity further forward gives Mikel Arteta new options against compact defences before the Premier League season. (Guardian)

Fifa attacked the campaign to remove Gianni Infantino

World football’s governing body alleged a concerted effort to undermine its president as pressure over his leadership intensified. The statement followed Uefa’s confirmation of a departure payment to a female employee and Norway’s call for Infantino to resign. (Guardian, BBC)

LIFESTYLE

Britain prepared for its first near-total solar eclipse this century

The moon will cover between 90% and 96% of the sun across the UK and Ireland on Wednesday evening, the strongest eclipse visible throughout the country since 1999. Another comparable view will not arrive until 2081. (Guardian)

Cottage cheese returned on a wave of high-protein eating

The once unfashionable staple has regained supermarket attention as social media and demand for protein-rich food drive a revival. Its mild flavour and versatility have moved it beyond the diet-food niche that defined earlier decades. (Guardian)
