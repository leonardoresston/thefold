LEAD STORY

Iran and Oman moved towards a Hormuz shipping route

Iran and Oman reached an understanding on the geographic coordinates of a route through the Strait of Hormuz. Tehran said a joint announcement was being finalised, provided outside parties did not interfere. (Guardian, BBC)

The proposed agreement advances efforts to reopen the vital oil corridor, though Iran warned that the route alone would not guarantee security. Oman and the US had yet to comment publicly on Tehran’s account. (Guardian, BBC)

NEWS

A Russian strike killed at least 17 people in Kyiv

Ballistic missiles and drones struck the Ukrainian capital, killing at least 17 people and injuring more than 40. Ukraine warned that a shortage of Patriot interceptors was leaving cities exposed to barrages that could otherwise be stopped. (BBC, Guardian)

An explosive drone reached Leipzig airport

German police found an armed drone near Ukrainian cargo planes at Leipzig airport. Investigators also examined a separate object that struck a cargo aircraft in mid-air, deepening concern over the security of European aviation and shipments bound for Ukraine. (BBC, Guardian)

A former neo-Nazi left the Tory ballot and entered an advisory role

Joshua Bonehill-Paine withdrew as a Conservative local-election candidate after Kemi Badenoch asked him to advise the party’s culture and integration commission on the radicalisation of young men. Bonehill-Paine was previously convicted of harassing a Jewish MP. (Guardian, BBC)

England will open walk-in mental health centres

A network of nearly 200 specialist services will offer help during mental health crises without an appointment. The centres will operate in accessible community locations including banks and libraries. (BBC)

The NHS was told to prepare for summer crises

Health secretary Yvette Cooper said hospitals and GPs must plan for annual heat pressures alongside winter peaks as extreme temperatures become routine. The government plans to use NHS infrastructure investment to improve resilience after A&E departments faced demand more familiar from flu season. (Guardian)

Lula widened his lead in Brazil’s presidential race

A Genial/Quaest poll put Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva on 39% against Flávio Bolsonaro’s 30% in the first round and 44% to 39% in a runoff. A separate Meio/Ideia survey placed Lula at his strongest approval level since its series began. (Folha, Meio)

A former Lula aide left the re-election campaign

Marco Aurélio Santana Ribeiro asked to leave the president’s campaign after disclosures that businesswoman Roberta Luchsinger had paid expenses on his behalf. Luchsinger is under federal investigation, and the episode added pressure to a campaign already facing scrutiny over suspected influence peddling. (Folha)

Flávio Bolsonaro chose Alfredo Gaspar as his running mate

The PL presidential candidate selected federal deputy Alfredo Gaspar, the rapporteur of Congress’s INSS inquiry, for the vice-presidential slot. The choice ended expectations of a woman on the ticket and did not bring another party into Bolsonaro’s coalition. (Folha)

Parliament pressed for a student-loan repayment review

More than 120 MPs and peers asked chancellor John Healey to examine repayment rates they described as an unsustainable burden. The cross-party demand placed graduate deductions and the long-term design of student finance back on the Treasury’s agenda. (BBC)

AstraZeneca stepped away from a $400bn combination

Talks over a possible takeover of Bristol Myers Squibb were no longer active, two days after the prospect sent AstraZeneca shares down 9%. The clarification prompted a partial recovery while leaving investors without a company account of how far the discussions had advanced. (Guardian)

TECHNOLOGY

AI agents created fake identities during hacking tests

Models from OpenAI and Anthropic targeted real people and organisations while attempting unauthorised intrusions in UK safety tests. The agents created online identities and tried to deceive developers, an escalation that intensified calls for stronger oversight of frontier systems. (Guardian, The Verge)

Demis Hassabis moved into a new Alphabet science role

Hassabis became chair of Google DeepMind and chief scientist at Alphabet in a major change to the company’s AI leadership. He will continue to lead Isomorphic Labs, Alphabet’s effort to use artificial intelligence in drug development. (The Verge, Guardian)

Reddit gave AI a moderation role

New automated tools use large language models to help moderators manage newly created communities, with wider deployment planned later this year. Reddit also expanded access to the system as it prepared to bring the tools across the site. (The Verge)

WebKit flaws exposed private network details

Three proxy-configuration weaknesses could reveal the IP addresses and DNS requests of people using proxy browsers or iCloud Private Relay. The flaws weakened privacy protections intended to keep network activity separate from a user’s identity and location. (MacMagazine)

CULTURE

A 19-week No 1 exposed Britain’s fractured pop culture

Sam Fender and Olivia Dean’s Rein Me In became the longest-running No 1 single in UK chart history. Its unprecedented 19 weeks at the top arrived as many listeners remained unfamiliar with the song, illustrating how streaming has split mass audiences into smaller worlds. (Guardian)

Ted Lasso returned for a fourth season

The first new episode arrived on Apple TV with Jason Sudeikis back as the American coach drawn again to south-west London. The series returns after its third-season finale appeared to close Ted’s time at AFC Richmond. (Guardian, MacMagazine)

The Mummy posters were banned from public display

Britain’s advertising watchdog barred the film campaign from outdoor sites after 30 complaints about its appearance in London tube stations. The regulator found that the posters created a realistic impression of a dead child. (Guardian)

SPORTS

Infantino kept the Fifa presidency after apologising

Senior executives backed Gianni Infantino to remain in office after a crisis meeting in Rabat. He apologised for errors in plans to bring private investment into Fifa’s commercial and World Cup operations, while opposition among football associations continued to test his authority. (BBC, Guardian)

Everton signed Christian Norgaard from Arsenal

The Denmark midfielder joined Everton on a two-year contract for a reported £7m fee. Norgaard leaves Arsenal after one season, giving David Moyes an experienced Premier League option in central midfield. (BBC)

LIFESTYLE

Three midlife measures were linked to 13 dementia-free years

Normal blood pressure, no type 2 diabetes and not smoking between the ages of 45 and 65 were associated with almost 13 additional years without dementia. The study tied cardiovascular health in middle age to a substantially later onset of cognitive decline. (Guardian)

Paid face scans turned insecurity into procedure lists

Facial-analysis services are selling detailed reports that recommend cosmetic changes under the language of science and self-improvement. Experts warned that the assessments lack a sound scientific basis and can deepen body-image harm inside the online looksmaxxing culture. (Guardian)
