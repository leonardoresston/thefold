LEAD STORY

An arrest moved the Ann Widdecombe murder inquiry to South Yorkshire

A 28-year-old man was arrested in South Yorkshire on suspicion of murdering Ann Widdecombe, the former Conservative minister and MP found dead at her Devon home on Thursday. Police said the suspect was in custody after being detained on Saturday evening. (The Guardian)

The case remained a live murder inquiry across two police areas, with Devon still the centre of the scene investigation and South Yorkshire now tied to the first arrest. The late update put a political death inside a criminal timetable, with public tributes running alongside detective work. (The Guardian)

NEWS

Venezuela's earthquake toll passed 4,300

Venezuela said at least 4,333 people were killed and 16,740 injured by the twin earthquakes that hit La Guaira on 24 June. Thousands more were still listed as missing, leaving the government and the UN facing a recovery bill that now reaches far beyond emergency rescue. (The Guardian)

The White House opened a press-freedom fight over Air Force One

The Trump administration subpoenaed New York Times journalists to testify before a federal grand jury after reporting on alleged security concerns with the president's Qatar-gifted plane. The summonses put the justice department, national-security reporting and the new Air Force One project into one confrontation. (The Guardian, BBC)

A US congressman described detention by armed settlers

Ro Khanna said armed Israeli settlers detained him during a visit to the occupied West Bank and that Israeli forces refused to intervene. The California Democrat said the episode took place during a delegation visit to a Palestinian village in the southern West Bank, an area repeatedly hit by settler attacks. (The Guardian)

Musk's family name entered the Tommy Robinson Russia story

Errol Musk said Elon Musk's family foundation took Tommy Robinson to Russia, where the far-right activist met Russian business figures and encouraged anti-migration protests in Britain. Robinson, whose real name is Stephen Yaxley-Lennon, had appeared from a Moscow hotel after a knife attack in Belfast. (The Guardian)

Burnham's inheritance narrowed around tax and aid

Rachel Reeves said Andy Burnham would need a worked-through plan from the start of government, while housing tax reform and council tax entered the early argument over his economic programme. Labour MPs also pressed him to return overseas aid spending toward the 0.7% target set under Gordon Brown. (BBC, BBC, The Guardian)

Brazil's right tried to settle around Flávio Bolsonaro

Jair Bolsonaro used a letter to describe Flávio Bolsonaro as his political voice and chosen candidate after a public crisis involving Michelle Bolsonaro. The move landed as Ricardo Salles accused the Bolsonaro family of demanding absolute obedience and splitting the right, while Canal Meio's polling put Michelle Bolsonaro and Janja at the top of a ranking of powerful women in Brazil. (Folha Poder, Folha Poder, Canal Meio)

The Valdemar emendas case reached Congress's leadership

Hugo Motta, the president of Brazil's lower house, called Justice Flávio Dino's action against PL president Valdemar Costa Neto an improper judicial intervention in Congress. Federal police attributed a volume of committee amendments to Valdemar that exceeded the annual total of 512 of the 513 deputies, while his wife called the investigation indecent and criminal in its conduct. (Folha Poder, Folha Poder, Folha Poder)

Sky's ITV deal raised a British television survival question

ITV's planned sale of its TV and streaming business to Sky became a wider test of British broadcasting scale, jobs and US influence. The takeover pressure leaves BBC and Channel 4 facing renewed questions about whether domestic consolidation is the answer to Netflix, YouTube and the advertising shift. (The Guardian)

AI-proof work became a career market of its own

Workers entering or changing careers are now being pushed toward roles where human judgement, care, craft and complex service remain harder to automate. Teaching, medicine, hotels, law and other people-heavy fields are being treated as resilience bets as AI moves from office software into hiring and training decisions. (The Guardian)

Weight-loss drugs started showing up in retail data

GLP-1 users in the UK and US are changing wardrobes as bodies change, with some shoppers buying across several sizes and retailers watching the spending pattern. The drugs have turned a health-market shift into a clothes-shopping and consumer-behaviour story. (The Guardian)

TECHNOLOGY

Meta killed an Instagram image tool after privacy criticism

Meta discontinued Muse Image, an AI feature that let users generate images using content from public Instagram accounts. The company had launched the tool only days earlier and withdrew it after criticism from users and a Hollywood union over consent and privacy. (The Guardian)

Big Tech's datacentre emissions kept rising

Microsoft, Amazon and Google emitted 119m metric tonnes of CO2 equivalent in the financial year ending March 2026, about a third of France's annual emissions. Their collective total rose by nearly a fifth in a year, driven largely by datacentre construction even as the companies kept net-zero targets in place. (The Guardian)

Paramount's Warner Bros deal lost one state-level delay

Oregon's attorney general withdrew an effort to delay Paramount's takeover of Warner Bros. Discovery by 60 days while seeking deal documents. The retreat removed one procedural obstacle from a media merger still drawing antitrust and political scrutiny. (The Verge)

ChatGPT security controls reached the weekend how-to cycle

ChatGPT account hardening moved into everyday controls, including two-factor authentication, Face ID or Touch ID access for Codex on iPhone and iPad, sensitive-content reduction and bulk archive tools. AI accounts are now settling into the same security routines as banking and email apps. (MacMagazine, MacMagazine, MacMagazine)

CULTURE

Dermot Murnaghan died at 68

Dermot Murnaghan, the former BBC, ITV and Sky News presenter also known for hosting Eggheads, died after prostate cancer. His family said he died peacefully at home in London, sending one of British television news's familiar faces into the centre of the weekend's tributes. (The Guardian, BBC)

Lizzo prepared a public return after the case that changed her career

Lizzo's next phase arrived under the shadow of allegations from former dancers over sexual harassment, a hostile work environment and discrimination after her 2023 tour. The claims, denials and online backlash still frame the commercial question around her next album and public image. (The Guardian)

Jay-Z took Reasonable Doubt back to Yankee Stadium

Jay-Z marked 30 years of Reasonable Doubt with a New York concert built around the debut album, large-scale visuals and guest appearances including Beyoncé. The anniversary show turned a 1996 Brooklyn rap breakthrough into a stadium history lesson for hip-hop. (The Guardian)

The Sentinels gave television another alternate-history gamble

The BBC's The Sentinels arrived as an eight-part adaptation of a French comic about wounded first world war soldiers turned into experimental super-soldiers. The series put steampunk, war drama and comic-book violence into a schedule often dominated by safer franchise revivals. (The Guardian)

SPORTS

England came from behind to reach the World Cup semi-finals

England beat Norway in Miami and moved into the World Cup semi-finals after falling behind. The result kept Thomas Tuchel's side alive in the tournament and set up another high-pressure knockout week. (The Guardian)

Spain kept finding late World Cup answers from Merino

Mikel Merino's decisive goals from the bench gave Spain its first World Cup knockout wins since the 2010 title run. His late scoring habit became one of the clearest patterns in Spain's path through the tense end of the bracket. (The Guardian)

Premier League business moved around teenagers and old power

Manchester City beat Arsenal to the £12.5m signing of 17-year-old winger Jeremy Monga from Leicester, while Chelsea mourned Ken Bates, the former owner and chairman who died aged 94 after shaping the club across a turbulent 21-year spell. (The Guardian, The Guardian)

LIFESTYLE

The heatwave carried into another week

Temperatures in parts of England and Wales were expected to stay above 30C on Sunday and into the following week. Heat-health alerts and wildfire warnings kept the weather inside ordinary planning for travel, work and outdoor events. (The Guardian)

Male fertility fears returned to the science agenda

New work on testosterone and sperm counts revived concern about male reproductive health, with scientists debating the role of chemicals, pollution and modern lifestyles. The headline finding was stark: average male testosterone levels may have halved over 50 years. (The Guardian)

Family flying stayed expensive after Ryanair's seating change

Ryanair stopped charging parents to sit next to their children, but the broader cost of flying with children remained crowded with infant fees, taxes and airline-by-airline rules. For some families, a baby on a parent's lap can still cost more than an adult seat bought in a cheap fare window. (The Guardian)
