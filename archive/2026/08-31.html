LEAD STORY

Government narrows early prison release scheme

The government has removed people convicted of manslaughter, rape, child sexual abuse and grooming from the early-release scheme due to begin in October. The change affects about 1,400 prisoners and keeps the men convicted of killing PC Andrew Harper behind bars. (Guardian, BBC)

About 4,600 prisoners are now expected to have sentences shortened, down from as many as 6,000. Prisons in England and Wales are operating at 98% capacity, and reform groups warn that the reduced release programme leaves the estate one major incident from disaster. (Guardian)

NEWS

Eight dead after ferry sinks off northern Cyprus

At least eight people have died and 18 remain missing after a ferry carrying nearly 270 passengers and crew capsized off northern Cyprus. Rescuers brought 237 people ashore; the vessel, travelling from Kyrenia to Taşucu in Turkey, sank in water 514 metres deep. (Guardian)

Iceland votes against reopening EU talks

Iceland has rejected a plan to restart European Union accession talks, with 52.8% voting no and 47.2% voting yes. Turnout reached 82.5%; Reykjavík was the only part of the country to back the proposal. (Guardian)

Prosecutors examine charges in spycops scandal

The Crown Prosecution Service is considering misconduct charges against 12 former police officers involved in covert operations against political groups. The wider scandal involved 139 undercover officers spying on tens of thousands of mainly leftwing activists over more than four decades. (Guardian)

Lula campaign takes election complaints to TSE

Lula’s campaign has filed a group of actions at Brazil’s electoral court against Flávio Bolsonaro, Ronaldo Caiado and Romeu Zema. The complaints concern alleged false information, use of artificial intelligence, personal attacks and abuse of power during the Barretos rodeo festival. (Folha)

South West Water ranks worst for environmental harm

South West Water recorded England’s worst overall environmental performance in a Friends of the Earth analysis. Its catchment averaged 574,323 hours of sewage spills a year since 2024 and logged 666 pollution incidents or warnings at bathing sites in 2025. (Guardian)

Nissan seeks Chery deal for Sunderland plant

Nissan’s Sunderland factory is seeking an agreement to build cars for China’s Chery as it operates at barely half capacity. The plant made 273,000 vehicles in 2025, far below the half-million annual output reached a decade earlier, while new post-Brexit trade rules threaten its export model. (Guardian)

Adult builders lift Lego revenue by 21%

Growing demand from adult fans helped Lego increase revenue by 21%, with sales reaching £4.8bn in the first half of 2026. Sets aimed at older buyers and collaborations with the World Cup and entertainment franchises have widened the Danish company’s market. (Guardian)

TECHNOLOGY

Roman telescope begins journey to map the universe

Nasa has launched the Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope on a mission to study dark matter and dark energy. The observatory will travel for three months to the second Sun–Earth Lagrange point, about one million miles away, before beginning a wide-field survey of the universe. (The Verge, BBC)

Texas freezes funding for Flock cameras

Texas governor Greg Abbott has halted further state spending on Flock’s AI-powered licence-plate cameras amid a growing surveillance backlash. The state had spent more than $30m on the network, much of it financed by a $1 levy on insurance policies. (The Verge)

Steam leak exposes 12TB of old game builds

A 12-terabyte leak contains builds of games uploaded to Steam between 2003 and 2013. Early finds include assets from the abandoned Half-Life 2: Episode Three, material cut from Portal 2 and a previously unseen Team Fortress 2 map. (The Verge)

CULTURE

Notting Hill Carnival marks 60 years

Notting Hill Carnival opened its 60th anniversary weekend with J’ouvert celebrations and a family day across west London. Crowds filled the streets in bank holiday sunshine ahead of Monday’s main parade. (BBC, Guardian)

Lola Young returns with surprise Reading set

Lola Young made a surprise appearance on the final day of Reading Festival as she continues a gradual return to live performance. The singer had stepped away from touring last year and has since begun playing selected shows again. (BBC)

SPORTS

Arsenal asks Premier League for larger matchday squads

Arsenal has asked the Premier League to raise its matchday limit from 20 players, with Mikel Arteta arguing that excluding senior squad members harms welfare and team cohesion. La Liga allows 23 players and Serie A 25; Arsenal travel to Aston Villa on Monday night. (Guardian)

LIFESTYLE

Moderate weekend lie-ins linked to lower blood pressure

About 90 minutes of catch-up sleep at the weekend was associated with lower odds of high blood pressure in a seven-year study of 40,000 adults. The benefit faded with much longer lie-ins, while a regular sleep schedule remained the recommended baseline. (Guardian)

Millennials trade doom-scrolling for gardening

More younger adults are taking up gardening for calm, community and time away from screens. Shared plots and informal groups are helping people without large gardens make the habit social as well as restorative. (BBC)
