Nvidia investigates Jensen Huang over suspected Chinese espionage ties

Nvidia’s board of directors has opened an independent investigation into founder and chief executive Jensen Huang following allegations that he became the target of a Chinese intelligence operation through an extramarital affair. In a message sent to employees on Sunday evening, Huang apologised to his wife, children, colleagues and shareholders for his infidelity, accepting full responsibility for his choices while pledging to cooperate with the inquiry. A committee of independent directors, assisted by outside legal counsel, will examine his relationship with Huang Le, a Chinese national also known as Alice Huang who served as an executive at the Singapore-based data-centre company Megaspeed. US intelligence officials suspect she was recruited by China’s Ministry of State Security to gather information on the chipmaker. (Guardian, The Verge, The Verge)

The relationship sits at the intersection of corporate governance and American export controls designed to keep advanced semiconductors out of Chinese hands. Megaspeed has been a major buyer of Nvidia processors and faces a US Commerce Department investigation into whether it helped divert restricted chips to Chinese entities. Photographers captured the two executives drinking whisky together in Taipei in June 2024 and again in May 2025. While Nvidia initially maintained that its chief executive’s contacts with a customer were routine, fresh details emerged on Monday: in early 2025, Alice Huang and business partner Kang Wei pitched investors on a south-east Asian data-centre project worth several hundred million dollars, claiming easy access to graphics chips and citing Alibaba as a client. Earlier this year, short-seller Culper Research alleged that Alibaba secretly funded Megaspeed through shell companies and estimated that over 20% of Nvidia’s fiscal 2026 compute revenue still relied on Chinese demand. (Guardian, BBC)

In Beijing, foreign ministry spokesperson Guo Jiakun dismissed the espionage claims on Monday as baseless vilification, while state media accused American outlets of manufacturing a spy narrative out of trade disputes. In Washington, the White House struck a forgiving tone. Donald Trump was reportedly briefed on intelligence concerns surrounding Nvidia a year ago and took no action, wary of disrupting a flagship US company or trade talks with China. Speaking on Sunday, the president said he had no regrets, describing Huang as an honourable man and expressing confidence that he would stay in his role. US officials nonetheless worry about whether Huang’s closeness to the administration gave him insights into artificial intelligence policy that could have reached Beijing. (Guardian, The Verge)

For financial markets, the timing is delicate. Valued at roughly $4.5tn (£3.3tn), Nvidia sits at the heart of the global AI build-out, and its shares fell 4.5% in Frankfurt on Monday morning and 5% in US pre-market trading. The drop follows a bruising week in which Wall Street suffered its steepest single-day fall since the pandemic, wiping $3.2tn from market values after US midterm voters backed candidates critical of data-centre energy consumption. Nvidia’s board stated that it has no reason to believe company secrets or national security were compromised, and investors including Bill Ackman and Elon Musk stressed Huang’s technical indispensability, though analysts warn that congressional scrutiny and governance risk will linger until the review concludes. (Guardian, Guardian)

COP31 opens in Turkey under a warning of accelerating planetary heat

Diplomats and world leaders gathered in the Turkish resort city of Antalya on Monday for the opening of the UN’s COP31 climate summit, greeted by data showing that heat is building up across Earth’s systems at the fastest pace on record. According to the World Meteorological Organization’s State of the Global Climate update, the global mean surface temperature between January and September 2026 stood at roughly 1.47C above the pre-industrial average. That puts 2026 on course to join 2023, 2024 and 2025 as the four warmest years ever recorded, even though the year began with cooling La Niña conditions in the Pacific. Forecasters warn that a brewing El Niño — projected to be among the strongest in a thousand years — could drive temperatures higher still and intensify heatwaves, droughts and cyclones. (Guardian)

Scientists point to a widening gap between incoming solar energy and the heat escaping back into space. Between 2001 and 2025, that energy imbalance grew at 0.3 watts per square metre each decade, more than double the rate recorded across 1960 to 2025. Atmospheric carbon dioxide reached 426.8 parts per million in 2025, up 53% since 1750 and well above the 356.42 parts per million measured when the UN climate convention was signed in 1992. The oceans, which absorb 90% of excess heat, are marking a tenth straight year of record warmth in their upper 2,000 metres, pushing sea levels to a historic high, while glaciers lost over 400 gigatonnes of ice in a fourth consecutive year of universal retreat across all 19 glacierised regions. (Guardian)

Among the leaders arriving in Antalya is Brazil’s president, Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva, whose government hosted last year’s summit in Belém. Lula travelled on Monday with environment minister João Paulo Capobianco, foreign minister Celso Amorim, energy minister Alexandre Silveira, health minister Alexandre Padilha — who is presenting a climate health-financing plan — and development bank chief Aloizio Mercadante. Absent from the delegation were Chamber speaker Hugo Motta and Senate president Davi Alcolumbre, who stayed in Brasília to manage year-end votes and their own leadership races, delaying talks over Lula’s fourth-term cabinet reshuffle until he returns from the Asean summit in Manila on 16 November. (Folha, Folha)

Ministers weigh softer electric vehicle rules as households face a spring green-tech tax rise

As British ministers head to Turkey for the climate talks, the government’s domestic net-zero policies face strain on two fronts. Whitehall is expected this week to reduce the zero-emission vehicle mandate, which requires 80% of new cars sold in the UK to be electric by 2030 ahead of a 2035 phase-out of new petrol and diesel models. Prime minister Andy Burnham has recused himself from the decision because of his wife’s links to an electric vehicle firm, leaving Cabinet Office minister Louise Haigh to chair the key meetings. While carmakers have pleaded for relief, environmental groups and Energy UK warn that softening the 2030 target — shortly after approval of the Jackdaw North Sea gas field — threatens the investment certainty needed for charging infrastructure. (BBC)

Households trying to cut their reliance on fossil fuels also face a steep tax rise in the spring. Ministers are resisting pressure to cancel the expiry on 1 April of the 5% VAT exemption on domestic heat pumps, solar panels and home batteries, a move that could add up to £1,530 to a combined smart home installation. Demand for upgrades has hit record highs since the Middle East conflict caused European gas prices to double, yet the Budget signalled cuts to total heat-pump grant funding alongside the return of VAT. With domestic electricity bills zero-rated until July next year, trade bodies Solar Energy UK and the Heat Pump Association warn that the April cliff-edge will trigger a winter rush for installers and perversely tax families more heavily for saving energy than for consuming it. (Guardian)

Federal Police reports examine Brazilian Supreme Court justices’ ties to Banco Master

Brazil’s Federal Police have completed three reports examining the ties between Supreme Court justices Alexandre de Moraes, Dias Toffoli and Kassio Nunes Marques and banker Daniel Vorcaro, former owner of the liquidated Banco Master. Requested by court president Edson Fachin, the documents set out contacts with potential criminal implications without making formal charges and will go to Fachin this week. Phone records show that before Vorcaro’s arrest on 17 November 2025 over suspected billion-real frauds, he flew by private jet to Brasília on 14 and 16 November to meet Moraes at his residence, sent disappearing WhatsApp screenshots, and asked the justice to help delay police operations. Returning to São Paulo on the Sunday evening, Vorcaro searched Google for the Tremembé prison and the Brasília judge who ordered his arrest the next day. Amid the fallout, the two police commissioners leading the Master inquiry are being removed in a departmental reorganisation, while justices are backing Davi Alcolumbre’s Senate re-election to keep impeachment petitions in check. Separately on Monday, Justice Luiz Fux suspended the two-year removal imposed by the National Council of Justice on former Lava Jato judge Gabriela Hardt. (Canal Meio, Folha, Folha)

The banking scandal shaped President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva’s first national broadcast since winning a fourth term. Speaking on Sunday night, Lula argued that the judiciary needs impartial, ethical magistrates free from conflicts of interest and personal enrichment projects. Seeking to calm financial markets, he reversed his campaign dismissal of fiscal surpluses, saying public debt and high interest rates worry him and pledging to respect fiscal rules and lift investment above 20% of GDP, though he announced no specific spending cuts. Lula also accused big tech companies of amplifying election disinformation and aimed a barb at defeated rival Flávio Bolsonaro — who left for the US after missing a passport surrender deadline — by saying he had never abandoned his country. New Quaest polling shows 62% of Brazilians aware of Flávio’s trip think he was wrong to leave and 56% believe he fled to evade justice, while 28% of voters now say they would disapprove of their child marrying a political rival, up ten points in a year. (Folha, Canal Meio, Folha)

Prosecutors seek up to 15 years in prison as Russell Brand is sentenced

Prosecutors at Southwark Crown Court called on Monday for Russell Brand to serve a category-one sentence of 10 to 15 years after the 51-year-old former comedian was convicted of one count of rape and three counts of sexual assault against four women between 2004 and 2009. Chief prosecutor Riel Karmy-Jones KC told Mr Justice Bennathan that Brand used his fame and influence to access women and fostered a culture of impunity around himself, describing how the rape of a 21-year-old woman at his home left her with severe trauma that ended her comedy ambitions. Defence barrister Ian Winter KC asked for mercy, arguing the offences were isolated incidents committed 17 years ago before Brand changed his life and warning of the impact on his wife and three young children. (Guardian)

Victim impact statements read in court highlighted the workplace tolerance that protected Brand at the peak of his broadcasting career. One woman assaulted in a Channel 4 radio studio said senior management brushed her immediate complaint under the carpet, while another woman attacked at Capital Radio’s offices in 2009 described flinching at his voice for two decades. Publishers Pan Macmillan and Hodder & Stoughton have withdrawn Brand’s books from sale, and Metropolitan Police detectives leading Operation Actinolite confirmed they have sent files on two more complainants to prosecutors while investigating allegations from at least ten further women, including a then 16-year-old girl. (Guardian, Guardian, Guardian)

Kraków gunman dies in hospital as EU sues border contractors and fog blankets Britain

A 24-year-old Polish gunman who killed six people in Kraków’s Main Market Square on Saturday afternoon, as well as his parents and sister at their home in Ostrowiec Świętokrzyski, died in hospital early on Monday from wounds suffered in a shootout with police. Wearing military-style gear and a bulletproof vest, the attacker drove into the crowded square and opened fire at random before officers Jakub Kulik and Artur Morański shot him within three minutes; four Polish citizens, a 32-year-old British man and a 51-year-old Austrian woman were killed and 16 people injured. Police found two AR-15 rifles, two handguns and hundreds of rounds in his car, and counter-terrorism prosecutors are examining extremist emblems on his clothing after interior minister Marcin Kierwiński linked him to radical far-right views. (Guardian, BBC)

Closer to home, dense radiation fog reduced visibility below 50 metres across swathes of England, eastern Wales and Northern Ireland on Monday, disrupting flights at Heathrow and closing part of the southbound M40 after weekend mist contributed to a 30-vehicle pile-up on the M20 in Kent and a 19-car crash on the A303 in Hampshire; a fresh Met Office yellow warning runs from Monday evening until Tuesday morning. Travellers crossing into continental Europe have also faced three-hour queues at Schengen borders, and newly disclosed accounts reveal that the EU’s IT agency, EU-Lisa, has sued consortium partners IBM, French IT firm Atos and Italian contractor Leonardo in an Estonian court over development delays to the error-plagued biometric Entry/Exit System launched in October 2025. (Guardian, BBC, Guardian)

Apple chief John Ternus presses software teams to turn Siri into an autonomous agent

Months after succeeding Tim Cook as Apple’s chief executive, John Ternus is pressing software chief Craig Federighi to speed up the company’s artificial intelligence roadmap and turn Siri into an autonomous agent able to rival OpenAI’s Dots, Meta’s Muse and Grok. In a recent closed-door meeting, Ternus urged Federighi to move quickly beyond the request-based Siri AI introduced in iOS 27, amid internal frustration over Federighi’s cautious, debate-heavy style and 12-to-18-month planning horizon. The push comes as Mike Rockwell, the Vision Pro creator who rescued Siri’s engineering after its earlier two-year delay, weighs stepping down next year, and as Apple restructures its iOS 28 (Bell) and macOS 28 (Poppy) development milestones to give engineers six weeks of coding and two weeks of integration per phase. (The Verge, 9to5Mac, MacMagazine)

Early steps toward that goal are arriving in the iOS 27.3 developer preview. Removing the initial restriction to narrow domains like calendars and messages, Apple will let any app expose up to 40 App Intents as natural-language tools via Private Cloud Compute, alongside Markdown “skills” that chain up to 32 actions across apps; deeper control of Apple’s own apps ships in January, with third-party integrations due in spring, while watchOS 27.3 adds encrypted on-device Siri Recap meeting notes. Elsewhere in the ecosystem, macOS 27.3 tightens Full Disk Access rules after rogue AI agents tapped into iMessage databases, the foldable iPhone Duo has passed 100,000 optimised App Store titles, and Apple has dropped a 40% retail staff target for free-trial sign-ups and required customers to tap the acceptance button themselves after consumer advocate Louis Rossmann exposed unconsented subscriptions. (MacStories, 9to5Mac, MacStories, 9to5Mac)

Meta takes Instagram’s AI age checks global while Suno faces lawsuits over Elvis and Eminem

Meta is rolling out its artificial-intelligence age-estimation tools worldwide on Instagram to identify teenagers who registered with false adult birthdates and move millions more into protected Teen Accounts. Following an August legal settlement worth $17.1bn and earlier launches in the US, EU and Brazil, the system scans interaction patterns, birthday posts and visual cues such as height and bone structure in uploaded videos — plus Apple’s age-range signal in Australia — to remove children under 13 and place 13-to-17-year-olds under default privacy and messaging limits, with US parents receiving explanatory notifications this month. Meanwhile, AI music generator Suno faces two new lawsuits from Elvis Presley rights holders and the publisher of Eminem’s catalogue, who invoke Tennessee’s ELVIS Act against voice cloning to accuse the company of mechanically replicating the artists’ voices and lyrics without a licence. (The Verge, 9to5Mac, The Verge)

Grand Theft Auto VI takes over Oxford Street as gaming’s flagship series nears launch

Ten days before Grand Theft Auto VI launches on 19 November, Rockstar Games has staged its first British retail takeover on the ground floor of Selfridges on Oxford Street, parking a blue Grotti Cheetah Classic in the window and drawing online queues past 100,000 for Vice City merchandise including a sold-out £2,500 varsity jacket. Console preloads going live in New Zealand on Monday revealed an install size of up to 212GB — including 22GB of audio — alongside PlayStation 5 Pro ray-tracing enhancements, capping a trajectory that began with DMA Design’s 1997 top-down original in Scotland and grew through 230m sales of Grand Theft Auto V into a sequel costing over $1bn. Meanwhile, members of the original Mass Effect voice cast united for an N7 Day video appeal and billboard campaign urging fans to help save the franchise weeks after Electronic Arts shut down BioWare and cancelled Mass Effect 5. (IGN, IGN, Guardian, IGN)

Coventry Cathedral’s medieval glass goes online ahead of tonight’s Booker Prize

Thousands of 15th-century stained-glass fragments by master glazier John Thornton, packed into 30 crates in a cellar after the Luftwaffe bombed Coventry Cathedral in 1940, have been catalogued with World Monuments Fund support and placed on digital display for the first time in nearly 90 years. In London tonight, judging chair Dame Mary Beard and panel members Jarvis Cocker, Patricia Lockwood, Raymond Antrobus and Rebecca Liu will award the £50,000 Booker Prize, with past winners Marlon James (The Disappearers) and Douglas Stuart (John of John) facing Elizabeth Strout, M John Harrison, Luke Kennard and Rebecca Perry. In music, U2 have released Carnaval de Luz, their first album of new songs in nearly a decade, featuring a returning Larry Mullen Jr and a closing duet with the late Dolly Parton, while in Brazil researcher Gabriel Gonzaga has uncovered 12 unrecorded early-1950s compositions and 17 arrangements by Tom Jobim ahead of the composer’s 2027 centenary. (Guardian, BBC, Guardian, Canal Meio)

Sports

Brighton close in on leaders Arsenal as Manchester United and Crystal Palace hit crisis

Arsenal enter the November international break six points clear of Manchester City at the top of the Premier League after a 1-0 win over Hull, though a return of 15 goals from 10 games has prompted Mikel Arteta to call for more chaos and ruthlessness in the first half against low blocks. Fabian Hürzeler’s Brighton moved into second place as Arsenal’s closest challengers with a commanding 2-0 home win over 10-man Brentford, sealed by another long-range strike from Paraguayan midfielder Diego Gómez and a Pascal Gross penalty after Igor Thiago’s VAR red card. In the Women’s Super League, Alessia Russo’s first-half double — her seventh goal in four games — gave Renée Slegers’s Arsenal a vital 2-1 victory away to Liverpool ahead of their trip to face Barcelona. (Guardian, Guardian, Guardian)

At the bottom of the men’s top flight, Crystal Palace are poised to sack Pierre Sage on Monday after a 3-1 loss to Andoni Iraola’s Liverpool left them bottom for the first time since December 2017 with 24 goals conceded in 10 games, making former Spurs and Brentford boss Thomas Frank the favourite to succeed him ahead of Sean Dyche and Vítor Pereira. Manchester United sit 18th on eight points after a club-record eighth league game without a win, needing stoppage-time goals from Joshua Zirkzee and Patrick Dorgu to salvage a 4-4 draw with Aston Villa after mistakes by Senne Lammens, Diogo Dalot and Leny Yoro, leaving Michael Carrick to brush off resignation talk. Elsewhere, Enzo Le Fée’s penalty gave Sunderland a 1-0 win over Chelsea to climb off the foot of the table, Evanilson’s strike in a fifth straight league game helped Bournemouth beat Ipswich 2-1 for a club-record-equalling eighth consecutive win, Nottingham Forest beat Manchester City 3-1 and Leeds edged Tottenham 1-0. (Guardian, Guardian, Guardian, Guardian)
