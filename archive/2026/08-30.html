LEAD STORY

Russian strike near Kyiv kills at least 37

A Russian drone strike on a warehouse storing explosive material near Kyiv’s western suburbs has killed at least 37 people. Another 42 were injured, including four children, after fire and explosions tore through the site in the Bucha district. (Guardian, BBC)

At least 50 residential buildings were damaged, among them a care home for elderly and disabled people. Ukrainian prosecutors have opened an investigation into why munitions were stored so close to homes. (Guardian)

NEWS

Nepal and Tibet missing toll approaches 3,000

Almost 3,000 people were missing across Nepal and Tibet by Saturday morning after flash floods erased entire villages. Families have covered a Kathmandu hospital wall with photographs as rescuers continue searching isolated Himalayan valleys. (Guardian, BBC)

James Cleverly enters London mayoral race

James Cleverly has resigned from the Conservative shadow cabinet with immediate effect to run for mayor of London. The former home and foreign secretary had been serving as shadow housing, communities and local government secretary; London’s next mayoral election is due in 2028. (Guardian, BBC)

Brazil’s presidential campaign reaches radio and television

Brazil’s free presidential advertising period began with Lula, Flávio Bolsonaro and Ronaldo Caiado receiving radio time among 13 registered candidates. Lula and Bolsonaro attacked each other; Caiado promoted his record as governor of Goiás. Their first television broadcasts followed later on Saturday, with Lula presenting himself against powerful interests and Bolsonaro seeking support from women. (Folha, Folha)

Norway’s new king sets out his reign

King Haakon VIII paid tribute to his father, Harald V, in his first speech since taking the throne. The address began to define the new reign as Norway continues a period of national mourning. (BBC)

EU gas stores fall to 13-year seasonal low

European gas storage was 63% full in the final week of August, compared with an average of about 80% at this point in recent years. The lowest late-August level in 13 years leaves the UK, one of Europe’s largest gas users, exposed to greater price volatility as colder weather approaches. (Guardian)

Fixed energy tariffs could save households £173

Households in Great Britain could save up to £173 a year by moving to a fixed energy deal before the government price cap rises in October. The increase will be the second in three months, with another rise widely expected in January. (Guardian)

TECHNOLOGY

AI loss-of-control reports nearly double in July

More than 300 incidents involving AI systems lying, ignoring instructions or pursuing harmful goals were recorded in July, almost twice June’s total. The Loss of Control Observatory tracks cases flagged by businesses and individuals and also found worsening severity in deception and misalignment. (Guardian)

Sony and Warner sue Anthropic over music copyrights

Sony Music and Warner Chappell have sued Anthropic in California over alleged infringement of tens of thousands of copyrighted works. They are seeking up to $150,000 per work and as much as $25,000 for each case in which identifiable copyright information was removed. (The Verge)

Apple and Corning plan Kentucky expansion

Apple and Corning are preparing another investment in the Kentucky plant that makes glass for the iPhone and Apple Watch. The factory expects to add 200 production jobs and triple capacity after Apple’s $2.5bn commitment last year; a new glass innovation centre is also planned. (MacMagazine)

CULTURE

Laurie Anderson joins the Future Library

Laurie Anderson will write the 13th secret manuscript for Oslo’s Future Library, where it will remain unread until 2114. She joins contributors including Margaret Atwood, Han Kang and Ocean Vuong in a project designed to unfold over a century. (Guardian)

Chris Cantrill wins Edinburgh comedy award

Chris Cantrill has won the 2026 Edinburgh comedy award for Rewilding, a spoof whodunnit inspired by his move to rural Northumberland. The 42-year-old Bradford standup had been nominated three times before; Marty Gleeson was named best newcomer. (Guardian)

SPORTS

Forest hold Liverpool to 2–2 draw at Anfield

Nottingham Forest led twice before Víctor Muñoz levelled in the 82nd minute to deny them a third successive Premier League win at Liverpool. Morgan Gibbs-White scored Forest’s second from the penalty spot while Liverpool were waiting to make a triple substitution. (Guardian)

Liverpool agree £123m Bradley Barcola deal

Liverpool have agreed a deal worth up to £123m for Paris Saint-Germain winger Bradley Barcola. The player is due to undergo a medical this weekend before completing a move that would make him the club’s second-most expensive signing. (Guardian)

LIFESTYLE

Statins protect healthy people over 70, trial finds

A world-first Australian-led clinical trial has found that statins can safely reduce the risk of heart attack or stroke in otherwise healthy people aged over 70. The common cholesterol-lowering medicine carried a low risk of serious side effects in the study. (Guardian)

Britain’s snacks are being reformulated

Food makers are reworking familiar snacks to comply with healthier-eating rules while preserving their taste and texture. Ritz crackers now contain 80% less saturated fat than a few years ago, part of a wider reformulation running from crisps to digestives. (Guardian)
