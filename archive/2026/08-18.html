LEAD STORY

Trump threatens Oman as the Iran deadline passes

Donald Trump threatened military action against Oman if the Gulf state obstructs his attempt to end the US-Israel war on Iran. A two-month window for peace negotiations expired on Monday without an agreement. (Guardian)

Oman is a US ally and is not a party to the fighting. The warning follows another threat against the country in May and deepens the strain on Washington’s relationships in the Gulf. (Guardian)

NEWS

Kushner took the Gaza plan from Hamas to Netanyahu

Jared Kushner met Benjamin Netanyahu after talks with Hamas officials as he sought Israeli backing for the US plan to end the war in Gaza. Netanyahu rejected the 15-point proposal earlier this month, creating a rare public break with the Trump administration. (BBC, Guardian)

Trafalgar Square filled for Jason Arday

About 30,000 people gathered for a vigil honouring the former Cambridge professor, who died on Friday. MPs, friends and campaigners addressed the crowd before a minute’s silence, while mourners left flowers beneath Nelson’s column. (Guardian, BBC)

A Labour MP was suspended over Covid loans

Bayo Alaba, the MP for Southend East and Rochford, referred himself to Labour after questions about government bounceback loans taken by his businesses during the pandemic. The party suspended him while it investigates. (Guardian)

Severe intellectual disabilities cut life expectancy by 24 years

People with severe intellectual disabilities die 24 years younger on average than the wider population, with 40% of deaths classed as avoidable. Minority ethnic patients in the group die 14 years younger than their white counterparts. (Guardian)

Lula and Flávio remain level in a runoff

A BTG/Nexus poll put Lula on 47% and Flávio Bolsonaro on 44% in a second-round contest, leaving the two candidates technically tied as Brazil’s official campaign begins. (Folha, Meio)

Brazil’s federal police indicted 25 over diverted amendments

Businessman José Marcos de Moura, known as the “Garbage King”, and 24 other people were indicted on suspicion of fraud, corruption and money laundering in an investigation into diverted congressional funds. (Folha)

China’s slowdown extended into July

Industrial output and retail sales weakened after one of China’s lowest quarterly growth readings on record. The figures add pressure on Beijing to support activity in the world’s second-largest economy. (Guardian)

Virgin won access for cross-Channel trains

The rail regulator approved as many as 20 daily return services from London to Paris, Brussels and Amsterdam from 2030. The decision moves Virgin Trains closer to breaking Eurostar’s passenger monopoly through the Channel tunnel. (Guardian)

Heat and drought are cutting European harvests

Vegetable and grain growers face severe shortfalls after successive heatwaves. French production is down by about 25% for courgettes, 35% for lettuces, 40% for peas and 60% for broccoli, while some artichoke growers have lost their entire crop. (Guardian)

TECHNOLOGY

Meta faces a trial over children’s privacy

US states are trying to force an overhaul of Instagram and Facebook for younger users. A defeat could require fundamental changes to two of the world’s largest social platforms. (BBC)

YouTube will count a view as soon as playback starts

The change begins on 24 August and aligns long-form video with Instagram, TikTok and YouTube Shorts. Creators are likely to see totals rise faster under the new measurement. (The Verge)

Apple will change its tracking-consent prompts in Germany

Germany’s competition authority found that Apple’s prompt design favoured its own apps over third-party services. Apple agreed to revise the rules governing how apps ask permission to collect data across services. (The Verge, MacMagazine)

CULTURE

Hayden Panettiere died aged 36

The actor known for Heroes, Nashville and the Scream films died without a cause being disclosed. Her career began in childhood and stretched across television, film and music. (Guardian, BBC)

Amanda Abbington reached a settlement with the BBC

The actor received a five-figure sum after a review upheld some of her bullying allegations from Strictly Come Dancing. The BBC apologised following its examination of her complaints about professional partner Giovanni Pernice. (Guardian)

A rough animation became a Chinese box-office hit

Niu Lai, an 86-minute film about a calf and a skylark, climbed alongside The Odyssey and Spider-Man: Brand New Day in cinema revenue rankings. Online fascination with its crude, low-tech production turned the mother-and-son project into a sleeper success. (Guardian)

SPORTS

Arsenal opened with a 3–0 win over Manchester City

The Premier League champions coasted past City in Sunday’s Community Shield. The defeat gave Enzo Maresca a difficult first match after succeeding Pep Guardiola at the Etihad. (Guardian)

Premier League broadcasts will carry referee conversations

Sky Sports and TNT Sports can use audio between players and officials during live coverage for the first time this season. The change expands the head-mounted RefCam system introduced permanently last year. (Guardian)

LIFESTYLE

Older adults face dangerous heat at lower temperatures

People aged 60 and over can experience harmful heat stress at 1.5C of global warming above preindustrial levels, earlier than previous models suggested. The research points to greater risk as sustained extreme heat becomes more frequent. (Guardian)

British Gas sent £38,611 for a £300 claim

A solar-panel owner received the mistaken payment after submitting a quarterly feed-in tariff reading. The company then struggled to provide a way for the customer to return the extra money. (Guardian)
