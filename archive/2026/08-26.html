LEAD STORY

Dolly Parton dies aged 80

Dolly Parton, the country singer, songwriter, actor and philanthropist, has died aged 80 after cancer. Her nephew Brian Seaver announced the death on Tuesday. Parton wrote enduring songs including Jolene, 9 to 5 and I Will Always Love You, building a career that crossed country music, pop, film and television. (Guardian, BBC)

Her philanthropy included the Imagination Library, which has distributed hundreds of millions of books to children, and a $1m contribution to research that supported development of Moderna’s Covid-19 vaccine. Her catalogue and public life made her one of the most influential and widely loved figures in American popular culture. (Guardian, Guardian)

NEWS

UK households to receive national-emergency advice

The government is preparing a resilience campaign that will urge households to keep tinned food and bottled water for national emergencies. Cabinet Office guidance will cover extreme weather and threats from hostile states, with the aim of allowing emergency services to concentrate on the most vulnerable during a crisis. (Guardian)

Canada answers US tariffs with levies of up to 50%

Canada will impose retaliatory tariffs on American goods including steel, furniture, fresh tuna, cosmetics, dairy products and outdoor equipment. Some levies will reach 50%, matching US measures that took effect after trade talks collapsed last week. (Guardian, BBC)

CIA director makes unannounced Moscow visit

CIA director John Ratcliffe travelled to Moscow for an unannounced visit, his first known trip to Russia as head of the agency. Flight data showed an American military aircraft in the Russian capital and a US diplomatic motorcade was seen there; Washington has not publicly set out the agenda. (Guardian, BBC)

At least 47 killed in Haiti gang attack

Armed men killed at least 47 people and kidnapped more than 50 in Kenscoff, in the hills above Port-au-Prince. Homes were set alight during the weekend assault, and a gang leader threatened to kill the hostages if members of his group die during efforts to retake the community. (Guardian)

Brazil’s Supreme Court opens a second front in Bolsonaro film inquiry

Supreme Court justice Flávio Dino has authorised federal police to access evidence gathered by São Paulo investigators about Go Up Entertainment, the producer of Dark Horse, a film about Jair Bolsonaro. A related inquiry is already under justice André Mendonça, creating an overlap at the court as investigations increasingly shape the presidential campaign. (Meio, Folha)

Gold reaches a three-month high

Gold climbed to its highest level in more than three months as the war involving Iran and uncertainty over US fiscal policy sustained demand for safe-haven assets. Bitcoin rose above $80,000 for the first time since May during the same session. (Guardian)

Lego sales rise 21% to £4.8bn

Lego’s revenue reached 41.9bn Danish kroner in the first half of 2026, lifted by World Cup, Formula One, KPop Demon Hunters, Star Wars and botanical sets. Growth across the Americas, Europe and Asia-Pacific offset stalled sales in China. (Guardian)

TECHNOLOGY

Apple brings M6 to the Mac mini

Apple has introduced new Mac minis with M6 and M5 Pro chips and Mac Studios with M5 Max and M5 Ultra. The M6 is Apple’s first 2-nanometre processor and combines a 12-core CPU, 12-core GPU and dual 16-core Neural Engine, while the M5 Ultra becomes the company’s most powerful desktop chip. (MacStories, MacMagazine)

Instagram starts trimming Reels automatically

Instagram is rolling out First Draft, an iPhone feature that identifies highlights and automatically trims video clips into a starting edit for a Reel. Users can then adjust the cut and add the platform’s usual effects before publishing. (The Verge)

CULTURE

Notting Hill Carnival marks six decades

Notting Hill Carnival reaches its 60th year as one of London’s defining public celebrations. Created by the Caribbean community in west London, it grew through steelbands, masquerade and the arrival of sound-system culture into a two-day festival that transformed the streets of Ladbroke Grove. (Guardian)

How to Lose a Guy in 10 Days sequel enters development

Kate Hudson will produce a sequel to the 2003 romantic comedy, with the project still at an early stage. Hudson and Matthew McConaughey could return as Andie and Benjamin, 23 years after the original film’s release. (BBC)

SPORTS

Celtic surrender four-goal lead in Champions League exit

Celtic crashed out of the Champions League after a 5–1 defeat at LASK overturned their commanding advantage. Callum McGregor’s 21st-minute goal put the Scottish champions 4–0 ahead on aggregate, but five unanswered goals in Linz sent LASK through 5–4 and left Celtic outside the league stage for a second consecutive season. (Guardian)

LIFESTYLE

NHS to offer home cervical-screening kits

Millions of women in England who have missed cervical-screening appointments will be offered free home-testing kits by the NHS. About two-thirds attend screening when invited and as many as 4 million are not up to date; the programme forms part of the NHS target to eliminate cervical cancer by 2040. (Guardian)
