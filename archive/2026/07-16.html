LEAD STORY

Starmer bowed out as Burnham faced the tax test

Keir Starmer used his final prime minister’s questions to defend a two-year record on NHS waiting lists, child poverty and the economy, closing with tributes after a week that turned Labour toward Andy Burnham. The transfer of power now moves from Westminster ceremony to the first fiscal choices of the next government. (The Guardian, BBC)

Burnham entered the handover with the chancellor appointment unresolved and a wealth tax still on the table. The expected next prime minister signalled that some taxpayers may be asked to pay more, making the Treasury choice an early statement about how far his government will move on redistribution and public spending. (BBC, BBC)

NEWS

London mosque plot charge put terror policing back in focus

A 14-year-old boy from south London was charged with a terrorism offence linked to an alleged plan to target two mosques. Police said documents found at his home raised concern, and no other suspects are being sought. (The Guardian, BBC)

Farage’s £5m gift drew fresh scrutiny

Nigel Farage faced renewed questions over a £5m gift from crypto billionaire Christopher Harborne after senior Reform figures were told in March 2024 that he would need about £1m a year to cover lost earnings if he stood for parliament. The gift was made the following month and remained undeclared. (The Guardian)

France moved assisted dying through the Assembly

French MPs approved an assisted dying bill after years of argument. The measure would allow terminally ill adults who meet strict criteria to seek help to die, leaving the country closer to a major change in end-of-life law. (BBC)

Hong Kong bookshop raids ended in five arrests

Police in Hong Kong arrested five people after raids on independent bookshops. Authorities said the suspects were accused of selling books that incited hatred against officials, extending the pressure on the city’s remaining independent publishers and booksellers. (BBC)

Germany warned Washington over election influence

Friedrich Merz warned Donald Trump’s administration against interfering in German elections after the US state department announced grants of up to $3m for European groups working on sovereignty, migration, censorship and lawfare. (The Guardian)

Zelenskyy removed a reforming defence minister

Volodymyr Zelenskyy dismissed Mykhailo Fedorov as Ukraine’s defence minister during a wider government reshuffle. Fedorov had been credited with reducing corruption and modernising the ministry, and foreign partners and civil society figures had urged that he remain in post. (The Guardian)

Lula widened his lead over Flávio Bolsonaro

A Genial/Quaest poll put Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva eight points ahead of Flávio Bolsonaro in a second-round simulation, 45% to 37%. Flávio’s rejection rose to 57%, while Lula’s fell to 50%, tightening pressure on the right before the campaign formally begins. (Folha Poder, Canal Meio)

China growth slipped below target

China’s economy grew 4.3% in the three months to June, one of the weakest quarterly readings since official GDP reporting began in the early 1990s. The figure landed below Beijing’s 4.5% to 5% target and sharpened concern over a lopsided recovery. (The Guardian)

Buy now, pay later moved under new protections

Millions of UK shoppers gained new rights as rules for buy now, pay later credit took effect on Wednesday. The change brings a fast-growing consumer finance market closer to mainstream credit oversight. (The Guardian)

Brazil’s Senate passed a R$27bn fiscal challenge

The Brazilian Senate approved a spending package with an estimated R$27bn impact and support that included senators from Lula’s party. Finance minister Fernando Haddad may go to the supreme court if Congress does not identify a funding source. (Canal Meio)

TCU opened room for pay extras outside the cap

Brazil’s federal audit court voted eight to one to allow extra payments outside the constitutional ceiling for senior staff in the Chamber, Senate and the court itself. The decision followed pressure from Congress and created a precedent watched closely by the Lula government. (Folha Poder)

Britain’s biggest pub group faced a tenant probe

Stonegate Group, owner of Slug & Lettuce and Be At One, is under investigation over possible unfair treatment of landlords across more than 3,000 venues. A breach of the pubs code could bring a fine of up to 1% of revenue, or more than £16m. (The Guardian)

TECHNOLOGY

Apple Intelligence moved closer to China

Apple Intelligence appeared to clear a regulatory step in China after months of uncertainty. Approval from the relevant authority would give Apple a path to offer its AI features in one of its most important markets. (MacMagazine)

xAI took Grok abuse claims to court

xAI sued a South Carolina man over allegations that he used Grok to generate child sexual abuse deepfakes. The company said the user circumvented safeguards and altered nonconsensual images, turning model safety into a civil court test. (The Verge)

Roblox closed its avatar video chat experiment

Roblox is shutting down Roblox Connect, the video calling service it introduced in 2023. The feature let users speak through avatars that mirrored real-life movements inside shared virtual spaces. (The Verge)

US broadcast ownership rules faced a rollback

The Federal Communications Commission will vote on 6 August on whether to end the national cap that stops one company from owning stations reaching more than 39% of US television households. The move would reopen a consolidation fight across local broadcasting. (The Verge)

AppleCare Plus prices rose for new Mac and iPad plans

AppleCare Plus subscriptions for new Macs and iPads are set to become more expensive, rising by $0.50 a month or $5 a year for new sign-ups while existing subscribers keep current prices. (The Verge, MacMagazine)

CULTURE

Nolan’s Odyssey landed as a blockbuster epic

Christopher Nolan’s The Odyssey arrived with Matt Damon and Zendaya in a large-scale adaptation of Homer, the director’s first film since Oppenheimer. Early critical reaction treated it as a maximal cinema event built around war, return and moral damage. (The Guardian, BBC)

George Lucas put AI inside cinema’s future

George Lucas added his voice to film-makers accepting AI tools, arguing that the technology will become part of how movies are made. The intervention lands as studios, actors and technicians continue to fight over creative control and labour protections. (The Guardian)

David Bowie’s 1965 recordings headed for release

Unheard songs from David Bowie’s early Davy Jones period will be released in September on David Bowie: The Shel Talmy Recordings. Some tracks feature a young Jimmy Page before Led Zeppelin. (The Guardian)

UK fanzines found new life after punk

Music fanzines are enjoying a resurgence 50 years after punk titles such as Sniffin’ Glue, with creators using print to build fandoms away from algorithmic music culture. The revival turns small magazines back into objects of community and taste. (The Guardian)

Munch’s chocolate frieze returned with a sharper history

A new Oslo exhibition connects Edvard Munch’s 1922 Freia frieze with cocoa, labour politics and women’s emancipation. The factory canteen commission now sits inside a wider history of chocolate, workers and public art. (The Guardian)

SPORTS

Argentina set up a Spain final with a late England turnaround

Argentina beat England 2-1 in Atlanta after Anthony Gordon had put Thomas Tuchel’s side ahead early in the second half. Two late goals sent the holders into Sunday’s World Cup final against Spain in New Jersey and left England with a third-place match against France. (The Guardian, BBC)

Fifa faced a Falklands banner case

Argentina face possible disciplinary action after players celebrated the semi-final win with a banner asserting the country’s claim to the Falkland Islands. The political message followed a dramatic comeback in one of the tournament’s most watched fixtures. (The Guardian, BBC)

Spain’s control carried it through France

Spain reached the final after suffocating France with possession and collective control. Pedro Porro’s semi-final goal in Dallas gave Luis de la Fuente’s side a result built on discipline, passing rhythm and defensive calm. (The Guardian, The Guardian)

The final became a longer television event

ITV and BBC plan to screen Fifa’s full World Cup final half-time show, with broadcasters preparing for a 25- to 30-minute interval. The BBC will also show Saturday’s England-France third-place play-off and Sunday’s final live. (The Guardian, BBC)

Casino sponsorship rules threatened Premier League money

Unlicensed casinos could be barred from sponsoring UK sports teams from 2027 under plans aimed at protecting vulnerable people and reducing money-laundering risk. The proposal would affect Premier League clubs with gambling partners, including Everton. (The Guardian)

LIFESTYLE

Locksmith scams surged across the UK

Locksmith scams rose 147% between January and March compared with the same period last year, leaving locked-out customers exposed to emergency callout bills that can run into thousands of pounds. One London customer was charged £2,209 after being shut out with her baby. (The Guardian)

Heat pushed workplace limits onto the agenda

Sadiq Khan backed calls for a maximum workplace temperature as repeated heatwaves put pressure on schools, hospitals, transport and offices. Some workplaces have climbed above 40C, strengthening union demands for updated health and safety rules. (The Guardian)

Ultra-processed food moved deeper into heart risk

A Canadian modelling study suggested reducing ultra-processed foods could prevent thousands of heart disease deaths. Researchers placed ready meals, breakfast cereals, protein bars, fizzy drinks and fast food inside a preventable public-health burden. (The Guardian)

GLP-1 weight loss reached bridal fittings

Rapid weight loss linked to GLP-1 drugs is disrupting the wedding dress industry, with bridal stores training staff to ask about major weight changes before final alterations. A Zola survey found 10% of engaged couples were using the drugs. (The Guardian)
