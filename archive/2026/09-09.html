Brazil’s supreme court turns an investigation into an institutional crisis

Brazil awoke with the leadership of its federal police suspended, its supreme court split and the presidency challenging whether a single justice can remove an official whom the constitution leaves for the president to appoint. Justice André Mendonça ordered federal police director-general Andrei Rodrigues and intelligence director Leandro Almada out of their posts on Tuesday. His decision followed an internal police report that justice Alexandre de Moraes used to seek an investigation into Mendonça for possible abuse of authority, administrative misconduct and an impeachable offence. Those allegations concern Mendonça’s handling of investigations into Banco Master and fraud at the national social-security agency, INSS. (Folha, Meio)

The dispute grew from messages between former banker Daniel Vorcaro and Moraes shortly before Vorcaro was arrested. The messages indicated that the Banco Master owner sought information about the case, arranged meetings with the justice and asked whether he should leave Brazil. Mendonça requested a police report that placed Moraes inside the inquiry. Moraes then relied on another police document to ask that Mendonça be investigated. The police described that second report as lacking evidential value, while Mendonça treated it as proof that he and attorney-general Jorge Messias had been monitored for at least 30 days. Two members of the court are now contesting each other through documents produced by the same police force. (Folha, Folha)

The immediate response exposed how widely the shock has travelled. Federal police directors offered their posts to the interim chief in solidarity with Rodrigues. President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva called an emergency meeting with Messias and justice minister Wellington Lima e Silva. The government’s legal office asked court president Edson Fachin to suspend Mendonça’s order, arguing that it intrudes on a presidential power and risks disrupting the federal police. Fachin gave Mendonça 72 hours to answer. Lula plans to seek an institutional exit and avoid a personal attack on the justice, an effort to reduce the temperature while the government fights the decision through the court. (Folha, Folha, Folha)

Mendonça sent his ruling to the court’s second panel for confirmation. Luiz Fux and Nunes Marques joined him, creating a majority to maintain Rodrigues’s suspension, but Gilmar Mendes paused the proceeding by asking for more time. Fachin has considered taking control of the Banco Master and INSS cases to contain the conflict. Each procedural choice now carries political weight less than a month before the presidential election. Thirteen retired justices, among them former court presidents Celso de Mello, Rosa Weber and Joaquim Barbosa, have asked Fachin to convene an urgent public plenary session and a rigorous inquiry, warning that the disclosures are damaging public trust and democratic stability. Police independence, presidential authority and the court’s ability to oversee its own members have converged in one case. A stable answer will require the full institution to establish who may investigate a justice, who may command the police and how claims of surveillance are tested without turning them into weapons inside the court. (Folha, Folha, Folha)

News

Britain’s West Bank sanctions meet an immediate Israeli response

Britain has adopted the position that Israel’s occupation of the West Bank is unlawful in its entirety and will build a sanctions regime around that judgment. Foreign secretary Ed Miliband announced an import ban on goods from Israeli settlements, restrictions on construction, finance and advertising services that support them, and personal sanctions against people accused of promoting settler violence. Arms licences and other exports that materially assist the occupation will also be barred. Twelve countries, including France and Canada, are moving towards sanctions after a summer of diplomacy, while the British legislation is expected to take as long as nine months and could face a legal challenge. (Guardian)

Miliband said settler violence backed by Benjamin Netanyahu’s government amounts to the ethnic cleansing of Palestinians in parts of the West Bank, the strongest language a British government has used about Israeli conduct since the state was founded. The planned E1 development east of Jerusalem has made the issue urgent because it could split the West Bank and further erode the land available for a Palestinian state. Britain still awaits an international court’s judgment on whether Israel’s actions in Gaza constitute genocide, though Miliband said there was evidence of war crimes. The government is separately sanctioning Hezbollah’s financing arm and restoring economic measures against Iran. (Guardian, BBC)

Israel answered by closing the British consulate in Jerusalem and barring 12 British MPs and activists, including Jeremy Corbyn and Diane Abbott, from entering the country. Its foreign minister, Gideon Saar, accused the UK of working against Israel, and the US ambassador in Jerusalem raised the prospect of trade retaliation. Marco Rubio ruled out America following London’s policy but said Washington did not want the West Bank destabilised. The sanctions will now be judged by their reach through supply chains and services, their effect on Palestinians employed by settlement businesses, and whether the coalition stays together when diplomatic costs arrive. (Guardian, BBC)

Children’s phones will have to recognise explicit images

Apple and Google face a UK law requiring smartphones used by children to prevent nude images from being taken, sent or viewed. The protection would operate on the device and be enabled by default for under-18s; an adult would need to verify their age before switching it off. Ministers also want the restriction to follow children into apps, which will require additional legislation. The government moved towards compulsion after a three-month period of talks with the companies ended without agreement, although culture secretary Lisa Nandy left space for a voluntary deal while the bill is prepared. (Guardian, BBC, 9to5Mac)

The policy addresses a large source of abuse material: the Internet Watch Foundation recorded more than 140,000 self-generated images and videos in a year, one quarter of all the child sexual abuse material it found. Its technical path is likely to involve software examining an image on the phone before encryption or transmission. That keeps detection close to the device, while creating an infrastructure capable of scanning private content. Apple is preparing stronger communication-safety warnings; Google says it is working with legislators and child-safety experts. The bill’s durable challenge is to block grooming and coercion without letting a narrowly defined safety tool become a broad inspection system. (Guardian, BBC)

Police warnings arrived before the far-right port blockades

The government has told police chiefs to improve intelligence gathering after organised far-right groups assembled at Dover and Portsmouth with officers largely unprepared. Kent police had maintained that Saturday’s gathering came without warning, then acknowledged on Tuesday that non-emergency calls the previous evening had flagged a possible protest and were never fully assessed. Hundreds of masked men in black clothing used walkie-talkies, carried lawyers’ telephone numbers and understood public-order law well enough to remain just inside the boundaries officers set. A related crowd of about 200 people gathered at Portsmouth the next day, where vehicles may have been deliberately left to block roads and clashes injured police. Prime minister Andy Burnham met senior officers on Tuesday as the national coordination centre intensified its monitoring. (Guardian)

The organising moved through closed social networks and was designed to evade detection. That leaves police balancing the threshold for covert tactics against the speed with which a disguised crowd can obstruct critical infrastructure. Whitehall also wants to know why powers introduced last year to restrict face coverings at protests were not used. The consequences have moved beyond public order. Two RNLI crews carrying 120 people rescued from a large dinghy were denied access to Southampton and Portsmouth because suitable reception arrangements were unavailable, then landed at Eastney in front of protesters. Volunteers were labelled as traitors online, and activists circulated names, photographs and a claimed home address. The inquiry must now find people responsible for violence while protecting rescue workers whose legal duty is to save life at sea. (Guardian, BBC)

Economy

A costly gilt sale narrows the chancellor’s room

The Treasury paid 5.82% to borrow £4bn for 30 years on Tuesday, the highest rate at a long-dated government bond auction since the Debt Management Office was created in 1998. The number will travel directly into the budget arithmetic. When the Office for Budget Responsibility refreshes its forecast ahead of the 28 October budget, higher debt-interest costs are expected to consume at least half of the £24bn margin left in the spring plans. Chancellor John Healey entered the week promising to balance the books; investors have now raised the price of doing so before he has chosen between taxes, spending restraint and a smaller fiscal buffer. (Guardian)

The pressure is global, driven by concern about public debt and another rise in inflation as conflict in the Middle East lifts energy prices. Brent crude traded around $97 a barrel, with the Strait of Hormuz still largely closed to tankers and Ukraine attacking Russian refineries. Bank of England governor Andrew Bailey told MPs that energy had tilted inflation risks upwards and that typical UK mortgage rates were already about three quarters of a percentage point higher than when the conflict resumed, the largest increase in the G7 apart from a possible Japanese exception. The Bank meets next week. Financial conditions are tightening through markets even before its committee decides whether Bank rate should change. (Guardian)

Channel 4 prepares to lose a quarter of its staff

Channel 4 is preparing its largest round of redundancies, with roughly 300 to 325 roles expected to go from a workforce of 1,276. Chief executive Priya Dogra, who took charge in March, will outline a new strategy to staff today. The broadcaster cut 200 jobs two years ago, yet its wage bill has since risen from £108m to £122m. Three consecutive annual losses, including £10m last year, have reduced the room for another gradual adjustment. Cash reserves fell by £69m to £49m, their lowest level in more than two decades, and the state-owned but commercially funded company has arranged access to more of a £150m credit facility. (Guardian)

Advertising supplies about 90% of Channel 4’s revenue, leaving it exposed as viewing and marketing budgets move towards YouTube and other digital platforms. Dogra is expected to commission fewer programmes, give each chosen production more money and concentrate marketing behind a smaller number of hits. The intention is to protect as much of the £640m programming budget as possible, including the £480m spent on original British content. London roles are likely to absorb much of the reduction after Channel 4 completed a pledge to employ 600 people across the nations and regions last year. The plan can preserve spending on screen only by asking a much smaller organisation to choose, commission and promote it. (Guardian)

Technology

A mathematical landmark arrives with a dispute over its origins

OpenAI says a system more powerful than its newly released GPT-6 Astra has solved the Navier–Stokes problem, one of mathematics’s seven Millennium Prize Problems. The equations describe the motion of fluids such as air and water, and the unresolved question asks whether smooth solutions can always continue or sometimes develop a singularity. OpenAI’s proof takes the second route, constructing conditions under which speed becomes mathematically infinite. Around 10,000 agents worked concurrently for 88 hours, after which Astra spent about 17 hours checking the result. A valid proof would close a problem formalised nearly a century ago and carry a $1m prize, which the company says it will not claim. (Guardian, Verge)

Independent validation will take time, and a parallel research effort has made the provenance of the proof part of the story. New York University mathematician Tristan Buckmaster and Anthropic researcher Levent Alpöge announced progress on a related problem a day before OpenAI. They had developed drafts using both Codex and Claude, and Buckmaster questioned whether information from those sessions could have helped OpenAI reach a similar route. He stopped short of accusing the company of taking their work. OpenAI says nobody working on its proof accessed their private material and that the results differ, while acknowledging that it cannot completely exclude the possibility that de-identified product data improved its models. (Guardian, Verge)

The episode gives mathematicians two jobs. They must inspect a novel proof line by line, and they must work out what evidential standards apply when thousands of software agents explore a problem using models trained on an uncertain mixture of prior work. Attribution has always mattered in mathematics because intermediate ideas can be as valuable as the final theorem. Systems that remember, generalise and search at industrial scale make the boundary between assistance and contribution harder to see. Verification can establish whether the proof is correct. A credible record of data access, training cut-offs and independent development will be needed to establish how the result came to exist. (Guardian, Verge)

Meta gives its new agent a browser, a memory and a chaperone

Meta has launched Muse, a consumer agent that can shop online, send email, plan travel, complete forms and negotiate while its user does something else. A person gives it a goal through an iPhone or Android app, the web or WhatsApp; Muse continues working after the conversation closes and returns when a choice or approval is required. It is initially available in the US, free within weekly limits, with paid plans promised for heavier use and support for Meta’s glasses to follow. Users can choose the agent’s name and avatar, while its memory is designed to retain small preferences and offer suggestions later. (Verge, 9to5Mac)

Each agent runs in a separate virtual computer in the cloud and is designed to remain blind to stored passwords and payment details. A second agent called Sentinel monitors that machine and requires approval before Muse can reach the internet; 1Password, Shop Pay and a more tightly encrypted environment are planned. Users can ask Muse to forget information and opt out of having their interactions used for model training, though Meta has not made clear which setting is the default. The design acknowledges the central tension of a personal agent: it becomes more helpful as it learns more and gains greater authority. Meta’s history of privacy failures means those permission stops, audit trails and defaults will be tested as closely as the errands Muse completes. (Verge, 9to5Mac)

Nintendo returns to Ocarina of Time on its own calendar

Nintendo has placed a Switch 2 remake of The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time on 5 November, exactly two weeks before Grand Theft Auto VI. Most major publishers have pulled releases away from Rockstar’s date, producing a crowded September and an unusually empty November. Nintendo used a 30-minute presentation marking Zelda’s 40th anniversary to show a more realistic visual style, a freely controlled camera and extensive new footage. The original 1998 structure remains recognisable, down to deliberate loading transitions that the company says preserve its rhythm. Physical copies will cost $10 more than the digital edition, another choice players are already debating. (Verge, IGN, IGN)

The game has a separate audience from GTA VI because each is confined to different consoles, yet both will compete for attention, time and holiday spending. Zelda Notes, Nintendo’s companion app, will add a playable ocarina, in-game statistics and other tools. A glitter-finished Switch 2 special edition, orchestral concerts and the title of the live-action film completed the anniversary programme. The release date shows the value of a platform with its own hardware and enduring characters. Nintendo can schedule a major game around the needs of its players and machines while the rest of the industry treats Rockstar’s launch as a fixed point. (Verge, IGN)

Sports

City and Villa start quickly while Arsenal arrive late

Manchester City and Aston Villa opened the Champions League with away wins of very different shapes. City beat Porto 2–0 in heavy rain through two Erling Haaland headers, ending a run of six defeats in nine European away games and extending Enzo Maresca’s perfect start to four matches. Haaland now has 59 goals in 59 Champions League appearances and 167 for City, third on the club’s all-time list. Villa won 3–2 at Club Brugge after John McGinn, Emi Buendía and Nicolas Jackson scored before half-time. A penalty drew Brugge within one and forced a side still waiting for its first Premier League win to defend under sustained pressure. (Guardian, Guardian)

Real Madrid also began with three points, beating Inter 2–1 at the Bernabéu through Kylian Mbappé and Fede Valverde despite allowing 37 shots in a match held together by Thibaut Courtois. Arsenal play at Napoli tonight after an air-traffic control failure delayed their flight from England and forced Mikel Arteta to cancel his pre-match press conference. The team eventually travelled with Jurriën Timber in the squad for the first time since May; Cristhian Mosquera remains out. Arsenal won all eight league-phase matches last season and have a perfect Premier League record this term, while Napoli have begun poorly under Massimiliano Allegri and will be without Scott McTominay after treatment for an irregular heartbeat. The disruption changes the hours around the match, while Timber’s return gives Arsenal one more defensive option inside it. (Guardian, Guardian)
