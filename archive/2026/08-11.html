LEAD STORY

Ukrainian strike deep inside Russia killed at least 13

A Ukrainian drone strike hit the Taneco oil refinery in Nizhnekamsk, about 1,200km from the border, and killed at least 13 people, according to the city’s mayor. Smoke was seen rising from the industrial complex after the attack. (Guardian)

The operation would rank among Ukraine’s deadliest long-range strikes of the war. Kyiv has expanded attacks on refineries and other economic targets far from the frontline as it tries to restrict Russia’s capacity to finance and sustain the invasion. (Guardian)

NEWS

Typhoon Dolphin forced more than a million people from their homes

Eastern China moved more than a million people to safety as the storm made landfall and brought record rainfall to Shanghai. Wenzhou relocated more than 900,000 residents and opened 1,500 emergency shelters, while another 98,900 people left high-risk areas in Fujian. (Guardian)

Three-quarters of England entered drought

Four more regions were placed in drought, bringing restrictions on water use to 27 million people. The East Midlands, Lincolnshire and Northamptonshire, Kent and East Sussex, and Solent and the South Downs joined the list as a fifth heatwave approached Britain and Europe. (Guardian)

A record 230 people crossed the Channel in one dinghy

The vessel arrived in Britain early on Monday after smugglers assembled an unusually large group at sea. The crossing showed how networks in northern France are expanding the “taxi boat” tactic to evade tighter enforcement along the coast. (Guardian, BBC)

Russia was suspected in the Leipzig airport drone bomb

US intelligence assessed that Moscow was behind last week’s attack with an explosive-laden drone, according to American media. Germany’s government described the incident as a new level of danger but did not publicly identify a culprit. (Guardian)

Lula led the first round as the second-round race remained level

A BTG/Nexus poll put Brazil’s president on 40% in the first round and Flávio Bolsonaro on 35%. The projected runoff remained tied as the presidential campaigns moved towards their formal opening. (Folha, Meio)

Brazil prepared a federal law to regulate judges’ pay

Supreme court president Edson Fachin said a draft bill should be ready by the end of the year. The proposal would set federal rules for judicial remuneration amid pressure for greater transparency at the top of the courts. (Folha)

Harvey Nichols warned it could collapse within a year

The Knightsbridge-based department store needs new investment to survive beyond the next 12 months. Its Hong Kong owner put the chain up for sale in June, with Mike Ashley’s Frasers Group leading the bidding at about £40m. (Guardian)

Emergency rolling power cuts became easier to order

New rules allow Britain’s electricity system operator to impose rotating blackouts at short notice when supplies run low, without waiting for emergency government powers. The measure is designed as a last resort to prevent a wider collapse of the grid. (Guardian)

Thames Water paid its finance chief a £1m signing fee

The payment was made while the utility remained close to a government-handled administration. The environment department called the decision unacceptable after Thames disclosed it in a letter to MPs. (Guardian)

TECHNOLOGY

Meta set out its plan for personal superintelligence

Mark Zuckerberg published a 6,000-word programme covering data centres, regulation, cybersecurity, biological weapons, jobs and surveillance. Meta also released Muse Glimmer, an open-source model intended to compete with products from Anthropic and OpenAI. (Guardian, The Verge)

YouTube raised the threshold for creator payments

From 1 February 2027, channels seeking entry to the Partner Program will need 1,000 subscribers and either 8,000 qualified watch hours in a year or 20 million qualified Shorts views within 90 days. The current watch-time threshold is 4,000 hours. (The Verge)

A third of UK manufacturers faced a cyber-attack

Nearly 30% of manufacturers were hit directly or through their supply chains in the past year, while only half had a response plan. The survey followed the attack that halted production at JLR for weeks in 2025. (Guardian)

Apple’s support pages disclosed Qwen integration in China

An official macOS document described how Apple Intelligence would connect to Alibaba’s Qwen model for users in mainland China. The page appeared before Apple had publicly detailed the arrangement. (MacMagazine)

CULTURE

A Roman shipwreck emerged off Sicily with 500 amphorae

The 2,100-year-old vessel was found three miles from Mazara del Vallo at a depth of about 46 metres. Italy’s cultural heritage police investigated after fishers alerted authorities; the jars are thought to have carried wine. (Guardian)

Barack Obama turned formative books into a podcast

A Great Book with Barack Obama will open on Audible on 24 September with six episodes. The former president’s selections range from Toni Morrison’s Song of Solomon and James Baldwin to John le Carré’s Tinker Tailor Soldier Spy. (Guardian)

SPORTS

Arsenal sought defensive cover before the Premier League defence

The champions prepared a fresh bid for Aston Villa’s Ezri Konsa with William Saliba facing an extended absence and Jurriën Timber still well short of a return. The move followed a 3-2 friendly defeat by Borussia Dortmund marked by defensive mistakes. (Guardian)

The champions opened the season as favourites to retain the title

Arsenal entered the 2026-27 Premier League campaign after winning their first championship in more than two decades. The Guardian’s panel placed them first in its pre-season forecast as several rivals adjusted to managerial changes. (Guardian)

LIFESTYLE

Households were asked to cut electricity use during the eclipse

Octopus Energy urged customers to avoid washing machines and dishwashers between 6pm and 8pm on Wednesday. The moon is expected to block up to 95% of the sun over parts of Britain, sharply reducing solar generation during the evening peak. (Guardian)

Dogs’ brains distinguished different unhappy human faces

Brain scans found that dogs processed fearful faces differently from angry or sad expressions. The University of Vienna-led study went beyond earlier tests that compared only one positive and one negative emotion at a time. (Guardian)
