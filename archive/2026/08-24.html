LEAD STORY

Reform would put British-born applicants first for social housing

Reform UK will propose giving British-born people and married couples under 35 with children priority for social housing. Naturalised British citizens born overseas would wait longer under the plan, while workers and veterans would also receive preferential treatment. (Guardian, BBC)

The rules would require new legislation. Shelter warned that moving away from housing need could leave vulnerable families in temporary accommodation for longer. Labour said people on asylum, student and work visas are already ineligible for social housing; Reform says it would build 50,000 socially affordable homes a year. (Guardian, BBC)

NEWS

Iran-linked cyber-attack shuts British power plant for four days

A small UK power generator was taken offline for four days last month in a cyber-attack blamed on hackers linked to Iran. The government said the wider energy system was never at risk. The incident follows Britain’s decision to let the US use British bases for defensive operations against Tehran. (Guardian)

Minority ethnic victims account for 93% of London’s unsolved murders

Minority ethnic people made up 61.7% of London homicide victims between 2019 and 2023, and 92.9% of victims in cases that remain unsolved. Shootings represented 8.7% of all killings but 38% of the 41 unresolved cases. The Metropolitan police said lower trust among young Black men can make witnesses less likely to come forward. (Guardian)

Guinea dump collapse kills 30 after heavy rain

A landslide at a huge waste site in Conakry buried nearby homes, tents and shacks, killing 30 people. The collapse came less than a week after Guinea’s government announced plans to close and relocate the dump because of the danger it posed during the rainy season. (Guardian, BBC)

Labour groups prepare Gaza vote at Burnham’s first conference

Labour groups are preparing a conference motion calling on the government to recognise Israel’s actions in Gaza as genocide, impose a full arms embargo and ban trade that supports military action or settlements in occupied territory. Andy Burnham has apologised for Labour’s previous handling of Gaza but has left a genocide determination to international courts. (Guardian)

Brazil’s first presidential debate draws three candidates

Ronaldo Caiado, Renan Santos and Augusto Cury took part in Brazil’s first televised presidential debate of the 2026 campaign. Lula, Flávio Bolsonaro and Romeu Zema stayed away, producing the smallest field for an opening debate since broadcasters began staging them in 1989. A Datafolha poll found 56% would vote even if participation were voluntary, while 50% see a chance of foreign interference in the election. (Folha, Folha, Folha)

CrossCountry restores timetable after control-centre outage

CrossCountry expects to run a full service on Monday after a power cut at its Birmingham control centre forced the cancellation of almost all Sunday trains. Disrupted routes included Glasgow to Edinburgh, Manchester to Birmingham, Cheltenham to Cardiff and Newcastle to Edinburgh. (Guardian, BBC)

US and Canada trade new tariff blows

US tariffs of 50% have taken effect on $20bn (£14.6bn) of Canadian goods. Canada plans matching duties from 8 September on products including steel, dairy, appliances and electronics. A small-business survey estimates 40% of Canadian exporters will be directly hit and almost a third expect revenues to fall by at least half. (Guardian)

UK orders two reviews of franchise protections

Ministers have commissioned studies of Britain’s franchise market and regulatory models abroad as they consider new legal protections for franchisees. The work follows allegations involving former Vodafone partners and a settled High Court claim brought by 62 franchisees. Vodafone admitted no liability and rejects claims that partners faced undue pressure. (Guardian)

TECHNOLOGY

Australia bans Halloween game over drug-powered gameplay

Australia has refused classification to Halloween: The Game because smoking marijuana reveals Michael Myers’s location and gives players an advantage. Games that link illicit drug use to rewards cannot be sold under the country’s rules. The decision has drawn more than 500 complaints and the developer could seek reclassification after changing the mechanic. (Guardian)

CULTURE

Jorja Smith commits her third album to the dancefloor

Jorja Smith’s new album, What Are the Odds, is a 12-track blend of UK garage, funky house, 2-step and Afrobeats. Produced with P2J and featuring Wizkid and Devlin, the record marks the Walsall singer’s first full-length turn towards club music after two more introspective R&B albums. (Guardian)

A forgotten 1918 ballet receives its Proms premiere

Chineke! gave the belated premiere of Edmund Thornton Jenkins’s three-movement Ballet at the Royal Albert Hall. Jenkins wrote the work at the Royal Academy of Music in 1918; its incomplete manuscript was reconstructed by his great-nephew Tuffus Zimbabwe and conducted by Jonathon Heyward. (Guardian)

SPORTS

Premier League returns with two late finishes

Dominik Szoboszlai’s stoppage-time penalty rescued a 2-2 draw for Liverpool at Newcastle after Anthony Elanga had opened the scoring within five minutes. Manchester City also left it late: Marc Guéhi levelled against Bournemouth in the 85th minute and Josko Gvardiol struck in the 91st for a 2-1 win. (Guardian, BBC, Guardian)

LIFESTYLE

Hugo spritz overtakes Aperol in UK searches

UK searches for the elderflower-flavoured Hugo spritz have passed Aperol spritz for the first time this summer, while Ocado’s shandy sales are up 40% year on year. A traditional spritz follows a 3-2-1 ratio: three parts prosecco, two parts liqueur and one part soda water. (Guardian)

Cold soba makes a 15-minute summer dinner

Japan’s zaru soba pairs chilled buckwheat noodles with a savoury dipping sauce and can be ready in 15 minutes. Rinsing the noodles after cooking cools them quickly and removes surface starch; salads, tofu or seafood turn the simple dish into a fuller meal. (Guardian)
