LEAD STORY

Colombia earthquake left at least 240 dead and 3,000 missing

Rescue teams raced through collapsed buildings in western Colombia after the country’s deadliest earthquake this century. The 7.4-magnitude quake killed at least 240 people, left at least 3,000 missing and destroyed about 5,000 homes. (Guardian, BBC, Meio)

Firefighters pulled survivors from rubble in Cali nearly 24 hours after the shock. At least 40 buildings completely collapsed in the city, while hospitals in Pereira struggled with casualties and aftershocks continued across the region. (Guardian, BBC)

NEWS

Bashar al-Assad was sentenced to death in absentia

A Syrian court convicted the former president and his brother Maher of crimes against humanity and war crimes committed during the country’s 14-year civil war. Both fled to Moscow when rebel forces toppled the regime in December 2024; Russia has previously rejected extradition requests. (Guardian)

Police chiefs pressed Burnham over the release of PC Harper’s killers

Every chief constable in England and Wales joined a letter asking the prime minister to prevent two of PC Andrew Harper’s killers from receiving early release under an emergency prison-capacity scheme. Andy Burnham said he was confident their release could be stopped. (Guardian, BBC)

Councils gained new powers over vape and betting shops

Local authorities will be able to reject new vape stores and betting shops when they judge that town centres already have too many. Burnham said decades of decline had hollowed out high streets and left councils unable to shape the mix of businesses. (BBC, Guardian)

Wealthier areas were told to share asylum housing

Burnham said affluent communities should play their part in accommodating asylum seekers as protests continued over plans to use a former military base. The government is trying to spread placements more evenly while reducing reliance on hotels. (BBC)

Brazil’s congressional leaders moved closer to Lula’s campaign

Chamber president Hugo Motta and Senate president Davi Alcolumbre sought places alongside Lula at campaign events in their states. The approach strengthens the president’s re-election platform while giving both congressional leaders support for their own races. (Meio)

Brazilian parties tightened rules on candidates linked to gangs

Political parties introduced internal checks after prosecutors pressed them to keep candidates with ties to criminal organisations off the ballot. The measures arrive as Brazil’s 2026 election campaign moves into its formal phase. (Folha)

UK consumer confidence reached a near two-year high

Households felt more secure in their jobs and more able to spend on non-essentials in July, according to Barclays. The closing stages of the men’s World Cup and a rise in domestic holidays lifted summer spending. (Guardian)

Australian delivery drivers won a minimum hourly rate

Platform delivery workers will receive at least A$31.30 an hour under a national order taking effect on 17 August. The rules cover food, drink and grocery deliveries, require platforms to provide personal accident insurance and were jointly proposed by the transport union, Uber Eats and DoorDash. (Guardian)

TECHNOLOGY

ChatGPT and Gemini each passed one billion users

Gemini reached one billion monthly users and became Google’s fastest-growing product, joining ChatGPT above the same threshold. Google has now taken 14 products past one billion users. (The Verge, MacMagazine)

Spotify will label AI artists and limit their reach

The streaming service is creating an “AI persona” label for artist profiles and track listings. Accounts carrying the label will be excluded from algorithmic recommendations as Spotify moves against fake performers. (Guardian)

Meta glasses were banned from courts in England and Wales

Court security will confiscate the smart glasses on entry and return them when the wearer leaves. The devices can record video discreetly, while unauthorised images or recordings inside court buildings can amount to contempt of court. (Guardian)

Apple tested proof that an iPhone photo is genuine

Code in the fifth iOS 27 beta points to an Apple Reference Image system that would add provenance data when a photograph is captured. The metadata could help users distinguish original camera images from deepfakes. (The Verge)

CULTURE

Lucy Davis disclosed incurable stage-four breast cancer

The 53-year-old actor, best known as Dawn Tinsley in The Office, said the cancer had spread to bones including her spine, hip and ribs. She was diagnosed 18 months ago and said it was too late for chemotherapy. (Guardian, BBC)

A documentary revisited eBay’s harassment scandal

Whatever It Takes follows Ina and David Steiner, the married journalists behind EcommerceBytes, after criticism of eBay led to an aggressive stalking campaign. The film traces how a company culture built around loyalty turned on two independent publishers working from home. (Guardian)

SPORTS

Arsenal made Myles Lewis-Skelly available

The Premier League champions offered the 19-year-old left-back to Manchester United and alerted Chelsea to his availability. Arsenal are seeking funds after spending £75m on Bruno Guimarães, while Newcastle’s Lewis Hall remains United’s first choice. (Guardian)

LIFESTYLE

Britain prepared for its deepest solar eclipse since 1999

Up to 96% of the sun will be covered in parts of the UK on Wednesday, while totality crosses Greenland, Iceland, Spain, Russia and Portugal. Eclipse glasses must meet recognised safety standards; a pinhole projector offers a way to watch indirectly, and ordinary sunglasses do not protect the eyes. (Guardian, BBC)

Reading’s decline carries a cognitive cost

Studies associate sustained reading with benefits for memory, language and reasoning, even as phones and fragmented attention take more of the time once spent with books. The habit remains one of the simplest forms of regular mental exercise. (Guardian)
