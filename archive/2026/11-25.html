Burnham fast-tracks NDA ban and consults on staggered age of consent as Met review finds entrenched racism and PIP shake-up threatens hundreds of thousands

Prime Minister Andy Burnham declared a “watershed moment” against abuse by wealthy and famous men on Tuesday night, bringing forward legislation to January 2027 that will invalidate confidentiality clauses in non-disclosure agreements (NDAs) used to silence victims of crime, harassment, or discrimination. The ban extends beyond workplace contracts so that anyone who signed an NDA can speak freely to police, support services, or the media, a move praised by Can’t Buy My Silence founder Zelda Perkins. Ministers simultaneously launched a public consultation on a staggered age of consent tied to age gaps—following Russell Brand’s rape conviction and allegations that both Brand and David Walliams had sexual relationships in their 30s with 16-year-old girls—and on barring “third-party consent” claims after Philip Young, 50, advertised his drugged wife Joanne on swinging sites to men who argued in court that they believed she had consented. Earlier on Tuesday, former HarperCollins staff revealed the publisher hired law firm Allen & Overy in 2023 to investigate nearly a decade of complaints about Walliams’s conduct towards young women, closing the inquiry in early 2024 without interviewing him, paying a former employee a five-figure settlement under an NDA, and publishing five more of his books before dropping him in December 2025. (Guardian, Guardian, BBC, Guardian)

In policing, a 389-page review by Gillian Fairfield for Mayor of London Sadiq Khan concluded on Wednesday that the Metropolitan Police remain institutionally racist, misogynistic, and homophobic three years after Baroness Casey’s post-Sarah Everard report. Fairfield found pervasive defensiveness, female officers punished for reporting predatory seniors, and bereaved families gaslit while homicide exhibits and CCTV went missing, prompting Khan to demand a reset from Commissioner Sir Mark Rowley. Elsewhere, the Home Office unveiled the world’s first comprehensive licensing scheme for all knife sales—covering high-street shops, kitchen cutlery retailers, and UK intermediaries for overseas vendors, welcomed by Southport survivor Leanne Lucas—while Burnham appointed former Bishop of Liverpool James Jones to chair a Hillsborough-style panel probing the Ministry of Defence’s decades-long cover-up of Cold War blood tests on 22,000 nuclear test veterans across 45 trials between 1952 and 1967. (Guardian, Guardian, Guardian, BBC)

In welfare and the economy, Resolution Foundation analysis warned that up to one in six of England and Wales’s four million Personal Independence Payment (PIP) claimants could lose benefits under Social Security Minister Sir Stephen Timms’s proposal to replace the points test with proof of extra disability costs, as co-author Sharon Brennan conceded some claimants will receive less to curb a budget rising from £16 billion in 2019–20 to £41 billion by 2030. In the NHS, public service ombudsman Paula Sussex launched an investigation into England’s 205 trusts for repeatedly breaching their post-Mid Staffordshire statutory duty of candour by concealing fatal errors, exemplified by an avoidable death at Kettering General Hospital. Meanwhile, 20 million variable-tariff households braced for Ofgem to confirm a 26 per cent (£443) January energy price cap jump to £2,166, and £43.6 billion FTSE 100 catering giant Compass Group—employer of 55,000 UK staff across 2,000 schools and NHS hospitals—consulted shareholders on shifting its primary listing to New York by 2028 after North America hit 75 per cent of operating profit. (Guardian, Guardian, BBC, Guardian)

In environmental policy, ecologists warned that Chancellor John Healey’s budget exemption of brownfield plots up to 1.5 hectares (86 per cent of registered sites) from 10 per cent Biodiversity Net Gain rules will remove over 80 per cent of planning applications from nature obligations. The warning coincided with UK Health Security Agency data showing a record 5,258 summer heat deaths in England in 2026—surpassing 2022’s 2,985 peak across 41 heat-episode days—and £11 million in funding from Water Minister Emma Hardy for 12 coastal councils using laser drones and road relocations as 3,500 homes face erosion by 2055. Elsewhere, Norfolk police arrested two men for burglary at the Prince and Princess of Wales’s Anmer Hall retreat; a Los Angeles federal grand jury indicted Meghan’s estranged father Thomas Markle, 82, on two counts of possessing child sexual abuse material found on 35mm negatives in a storage unit; and the Natsal-4 survey in The Lancet showed British under-45s have sex twice a month (down from four times in 1990) while a third of under-25s have never had intercourse. (Guardian, Guardian, Guardian, Guardian, Guardian, BBC, Guardian, BBC)

Leaked Russian files expose 490,000 Ukraine war deaths and 349 killed in Mali as US readies fresh global tariffs and China purges Shanghai boss Chen Jining

An analysis by BBC Russian and Mediazona of 11 million Rosstat mortality records from 2021 to September 2026—hacked by Cyber Partisans BY and Silent Crow—confirmed on Wednesday that at least 490,334 Russian soldiers have died since the February 2022 invasion of Ukraine. The war caused 65 per cent of all deaths among Russian men aged 18 to 24 in 2025 and 59 per cent among those aged 25 to 34, with 20,000 troops killed taking 10 villages whose combined pre-war population was 12,000, including 1,122 Russian dead for Andriivka near Bakhmut (pre-war population 74). The same leaked files revealed 349 Russian military and mercenary deaths in Mali over three years—over 10 per cent of Moscow’s force backing Bamako’s junta—including 43 Africa Corps fighters killed on 25 April 2026 when JNIM and Tuareg rebels overran Kidal with car bombs and drones and downed a helicopter in Gao. (BBC, Guardian)

In Washington, the White House prepared to announce new import tariffs on at least 14 trading partners including China, the EU, and Japan on Wednesday under a Section 301 probe opened after the Supreme Court struck down Donald Trump’s April 2025 “Liberation Day” duties, testing a US–China truce expiring on 10 January. In Congress, 34-year-old Democrat Victoria Broderick unseated eight-term Republican Scott DesJarlais by under 400 votes in Tennessee’s redrawn 4th District—which Trump won by 20 points in 2024—expanding the Democrats’ House majority in the 120th Congress to a 30-year high of 259 seats. Meanwhile, Kilmar Ábrego García flew to Spain as a free man after his wrongful 2025 deportation to El Salvador’s CECOT prison; Trump awarded disbarred former lawyer Rudy Giuliani the Presidential Medal of Freedom; and the US Army prepared to detonate 68 drums holding 9,700 pounds of crystallized 60-year-old lead azide in Middletown, Iowa, under water tanks and foam after an appeals court denied a Sierra Club stay. (BBC, Guardian, BBC, Guardian, The Verge)

In China, Xinhua announced late on Tuesday that anti-graft investigators are probing Shanghai Communist Party chief Chen Jining, 62—an Imperial College London alumnus, former Tsinghua University president, environment minister, and Beijing mayor—making him the fourth member of the 2022 Politburo purged by Xi Jinping since 2025, after He Weidong, Zhang Youxia, and Ma Xingrui. Across the Taiwan Strait, BBC Verify uncovered a China-linked network (“noxparable”) behind over 500 AI deepfake videos with tens of millions of views ahead of Taiwan’s local elections, falsely claiming President Lai Ching-te holds a US green card to flee a war and fabricating drug-use clips of Taipei mayoral candidate Puma Shen. (Guardian, BBC)

Ahead of Spain’s Sunday general election, Socialist Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez warned in Tenerife against the right-wing wave of Trump, Jair Bolsonaro, and Javier Milei as polls put Alberto Núñez Feijóo’s People’s Party on course to govern with far-right Vox. Elsewhere, Vanuatu launched twin international legal and arbitration actions against France over the uninhabited Matthew and Hunter volcanic islands and their 350,000-square-kilometre Pacific economic zone; Colombian President Abelardo de la Espriella took command of a Cartagena Éxito supermarket siege where gunmen who botched an armoured-van heist seized 30 hostages before freeing 10; a report documented North Korean Christians spied on and jailed; and ex-Sydney police officer Beau Lamarre was convicted of murdering Jesse Baird and Luke Davies. Chamber passes Lula’s R$ 60bn fiscal package amid R$ 2bn health-budget deal as Alcolumbre threatens to annul exposed Senate ballots and Haddad weighs cabinet exit

Brazil’s Chamber of Deputies approved Planning Minister Bruno Moretti’s fiscal adjustment package by 353 votes to 128, with five abstentions, promising up to R$ 60 billion in 2027 savings through tax-break cuts and spending caps after rapporteur Mauro Benevides Filho (União Brasil-CE) preserved the Senate text. While PSOL voted against and PT deputy leader Lindbergh Farias abstained, seven deputies confirmed Speaker Hugo Motta (Republicanos-PB) oiled the vote and his February re-election bid by promising current and incoming deputies R$ 3 million each—R$ 2 billion in total—via opaque Health Ministry ordinances to municipalities. President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva then cleared Finance Minister Dario Durigan to draft a second bill capping mandatory spending growth at 2 per cent, as the PT prepared a new “Letter to the Brazilian People” and records showed 94 of the 594 lawmakers in the next Congress owe R$ 311 million in irregular federal active debt. (Meio, Meio, Folha, Folha, Folha)

At the Planalto, former Finance Minister Fernando Haddad left a Tuesday meeting with Lula convinced he will stay out of the fourth-term cabinet unless named chief of staff (Casa Civil), skipping Lula’s flight to São Paulo. Lula—who declared that he alone will pick every minister on his own timetable—scheduled Wednesday talks with former Justice Ministers Tarso Genro and Supreme Federal Court (STF) Justice Flávio Dino on the Justice portfolio while Marconi Perillo urged Vice-President Geraldo Alckmin to lead economic stabilisation and Roberto Freire backed a Cidadania–MDB–PSDB federation.
