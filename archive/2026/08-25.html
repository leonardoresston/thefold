LEAD STORY

US Supreme Court lets Trump pursue mail-voting restrictions

The US Supreme Court has allowed Donald Trump’s administration to proceed with an effort to restrict mail-in voting before November’s midterm elections. The justices voted 6–3 along ideological lines to lift a June injunction against the president’s executive order. (Guardian)

A second injunction remains in force, and further legal challenges are possible. The ruling therefore removes one barrier without settling whether the administration can implement the restrictions before voters go to the polls. (Guardian)

NEWS

Twelve arrested over A66 crash that killed seven

Nine men and three women, aged between 19 and 61, have been arrested as police investigate the events leading to a collision near Middlesbrough that killed two officers and five other people. PC Matthew Blades and PC Tom Clough died when a Volkswagen Passat travelling the wrong way on the A66 struck their marked armed-response vehicle; the five men in the Passat, aged 17 to 23, also died at the scene. (Guardian, BBC)

Global sea-surface temperature reaches record 21.1C

The average surface temperature of oceans outside the polar regions reached 21.1C on Saturday, the highest level recorded. More than 90% of the extra heat trapped by carbon dioxide from fossil-fuel use enters the oceans, intensifying marine heatwaves and pressure on ecosystems. (Guardian)

Pacific territories oppose US seabed lease plan

Officials in Guam and the Northern Mariana Islands are opposing a US proposal to auction exploration rights across 28m hectares of seabed near the Mariana Trench. The administration is seeking critical ocean minerals as strategic competition with China grows, while territorial leaders warn that deep-sea activity could damage marine ecosystems central to island life and food security. (Guardian)

Cash inquiry puts Lula’s campaign under pressure

A Brazilian federal police investigation says lobbyist Roberta Luchsinger handled significant sums in cash and used part of the money to pay a travel agency for a journey by Fábio Luís Lula da Silva, the president’s eldest son. Lula has urged allies to campaign in person as the inquiry becomes an issue in the presidential race. The allegations remain under investigation. (Folha, Folha)

Carter Center restores Brazil election mission

The Carter Center plans to send specialists to observe Brazil’s October election, reversing a decision communicated to the electoral court about 20 days earlier. The mission will add international scrutiny to a campaign already shaped by disputes over investigations, court decisions and confidence in the electoral system. (Folha)

Graduate vacancies fall to a ten-year low

UK employers advertised 8,383 graduate jobs in July, the lowest monthly total since Adzuna began tracking the market in 2016. Vacancies were down 45% from a year earlier, leaving the latest university cohort to compete for fewer entry-level roles. (Guardian)

Thames Water lenders propose new board

Creditors pursuing a £10bn rescue of Thames Water would appoint former Yorkshire Water chief Liz Barber and former Openreach chief Clive Selley as directors if they take control. The plan is intended to avert temporary nationalisation, while campaigners say a change of board would leave the company’s deeper financial and operational problems unresolved. (Guardian)

TECHNOLOGY

Robotaxi expansion meets stronger local resistance

Driverless-taxi operators including Waymo and Zoox are facing growing opposition as cities and states shape rules for commercial services. New York governor Kathy Hochul withdrew a proposal that would have permitted robotaxis outside New York City after resistance from taxi drivers, unions and legislators; commercial driverless service remains illegal across the state. (The Verge)

GrapheneOS prepares its first foldable phones

The privacy-focused Android project GrapheneOS plans to support Motorola foldables next year, extending beyond the Google Pixel devices on which it has traditionally run. Pixel 11 support is also planned, giving users more hardware choices for a de-Googled mobile operating system. (The Verge)

CULTURE

Kit Harington joins HBO’s Harry Potter series

Kit Harington will play Gilderoy Lockhart in HBO’s Harry Potter television adaptation. The role is his third collaboration with the network after Game of Thrones and Industry, and places him in the part played by Kenneth Branagh in the second film. (BBC)

SPORTS

Arsenal enters the Julián Álvarez transfer equation

Julián Álvarez faces a decision over his Atlético Madrid future before the transfer window closes on 1 September, with Arsenal among the possible destinations. The Argentina forward was jeered during Atlético’s 2–2 draw with Villarreal, intensifying a dispute over his wish to leave; no deal has been completed. (BBC, Guardian)

Chelsea opens with a 3–2 win at Fulham

Cole Palmer scored and created a goal for João Pedro as Chelsea beat Fulham 3–2 at Craven Cottage. The result gave Xabi Alonso a winning start in the Premier League and supplied an early test of Chelsea’s rebuilt side. (BBC)

LIFESTYLE

Wild beaver kits return to Norfolk after 500 years

Two wild beaver kits have been born at Pensthorpe nature reserve near Fakenham, the first free-living births recorded in Norfolk for more than five centuries. Their parents arrived at the reserve in December, and the newborns provide fresh evidence that beavers are re-establishing themselves in the English landscape. (Guardian)

Britain’s service charges spread beyond restaurants

Suggested tips and service charges are appearing more often in pubs, coffee shops and self-service checkouts, with 15% now common at some London restaurants. The expansion has renewed questions over when a charge is optional and how much reaches staff, particularly when customers are prompted to tip before receiving service. (Guardian)
