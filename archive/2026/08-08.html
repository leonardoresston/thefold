LEAD STORY

System failures left a sex offender free to murder two women

An Old Bailey jury found Simon Levy guilty of murdering Carmenza Valencia-Trujillo in March 2025 and Sheryl Wilkins five months later. Levy had already been established as a persistent sex offender, yet delays to sexual-assault charges and failures to present his past convictions in court left him free to attack again. (Guardian, BBC)

A senior Metropolitan police officer acknowledged that Wilkins could have been saved. The case exposed a chain of police and prosecution errors that allowed Levy to remain at liberty between the two killings. (BBC, Guardian)

NEWS

Spain imposed border checks on visitors from Italy

Spain announced temporary controls on arrivals from Italy as a dispute over migration through Ceuta intensified. Italy had introduced its own controls after about 78,000 people crossed from Morocco into the Spanish territory in north Africa. (BBC, Guardian)

Eight people died in a Thailand school shooting

A 14-year-old killed his grandparents before opening fire at Debsirin Nonthaburi school near Bangkok. Five people were shot dead at the school and the attacker then killed himself; more than 30 people were wounded and nine were in critical condition. (Guardian, BBC)

A court halted Trump’s $400m White House ballroom

A US federal appeals court ordered construction to stop for two weeks, allowing the administration to seek a Supreme Court ruling. The judges said Congress must decide whether the project should proceed after the historic East Wing was demolished to make room for it. (Guardian, BBC)

Mental health providers in England prepared service cuts

More than two-thirds of NHS providers surveyed expected services to be reduced or closed as trusts tried to repair their finances. More than half anticipated job losses, raising the prospect of longer waits for adults and children in a system already facing strong demand. (Guardian)

Thetford remained tense after attacks on asylum accommodation

Crowds in the Norfolk town tried to enter homes believed to be housing asylum seekers during several days of disorder. Residents described fear and frustration as local officials faced criticism for failing to engage with the community over the placements. (Guardian)

Brazil’s federal police will investigate R$55.4m in Pix amendments

Supreme court justice Flávio Dino ordered an investigation into suspected crimes in the execution of R$55.4m in direct-transfer budget amendments. The cases emerged from a federal audit and involve funds directed by vice-presidential candidate Alfredo Gaspar, lower-house speaker Hugo Motta and a former PT leader. (Folha)

Tarcísio’s sons entered a tax-credit consultancy business

The two sons of São Paulo governor Tarcísio de Freitas became partners in June with a businessman whose family works in tax-credit consulting and recovery. The relationship places the governor’s family alongside a sector that handles claims against public revenue. (Folha)

Ministers moved to contain a clash between the Supreme Court and police

Brazil’s justice minister joined officials from the attorney general’s office and the Supreme Court in talks aimed at reducing tension between justice André Mendonça and federal police chief Andrei Rodrigues. The intervention sought to prevent the dispute from disrupting federal investigations. (Meio)

UK house prices stalled in July

The average home cost £299,253, down £143 from June, according to Lloyds. Higher mortgage rates, uncertainty in the Middle East and stretched affordability kept buyers cautious and left prices broadly flat. (Guardian)

Boeing 737 Max operators were ordered to check for cracks

The US Federal Aviation Administration ordered fuselage inspections affecting about 471 aircraft. The directive followed reports of cracks in the same component on earlier Boeing models. (Guardian)

TECHNOLOGY

OpenAI paused work around a model with dangerous cyber capability

OpenAI halted internal activity involving an in-development model called Astra because it did not meet new security standards. The move followed disclosures that company models had accidentally hacked Hugging Face during testing. (The Verge)

The White House kept its AI safety framework private

The Trump administration finalised a voluntary process for testing advanced models for safety and cybersecurity risks after meeting OpenAI, Anthropic, Meta, Google, Nvidia and Microsoft. The framework’s details remain undisclosed, limiting outside scrutiny of the standards. (Guardian)

Microsoft Edge will shut out older ad blockers

Microsoft is ending support for Manifest V2 extensions, cutting off uBlock Origin and other older blockers as Chrome did earlier this year. The company said only 58 extensions with meaningful use remained on the older platform. (The Verge)

Brazil’s election placed big tech under new court rules

Technology platforms enter the 2026 campaign under new judicial requirements while US parent companies reduce election-specific safeguards and place greater weight on permissive speech policies. The combination will test enforcement and content moderation through the October vote. (Folha)

CULTURE

William Orbit died aged 69

The producer who helped shape Madonna’s Ray of Light and worked with Blur, All Saints, Pink and U2 died at 69. Orbit’s use of electronic and dance textures made him one of the defining British pop producers of his generation. (BBC, Guardian)

Sam Neill completed a role in The Legend of Zelda

The New Zealand actor finished filming the live-action video-game adaptation before his death from pneumonia last month at 78. The film completed production in April and is due for release next May; Neill’s role has not been announced. (Guardian)

The Edinburgh fringe defended Amanda Knox’s comedy show

Festival organisers resisted calls to cancel the sold-out Cartwheel after criticism from the family of Meredith Kercher, Knox’s murdered former flatmate. More than 9,000 people signed a petition seeking the show’s removal. (Guardian)

A Clara Peeters still life emerged from museum storage

Norway’s National Museum is bringing a long-unattributed painting by the Flemish baroque pioneer out of obscurity for a major exhibition of women artists. Curator Cynthia Osiecki found the work in storage in Oslo while studying Peeters in 2022. (Guardian)

SPORTS

Norway called for Infantino to resign from Fifa

The Norwegian football federation became the first national association to demand Gianni Infantino’s immediate departure after his failed attempt to sell private stakes in the World Cup. The aborted investment plan widened a governance crisis that continued to threaten his presidency. (Guardian, BBC)

LIFESTYLE

Adult obesity in England doubled since 1993

Around seven million more adults are living with obesity than would have been if the 1993 rate had remained unchanged. The rise has doubled the share of the adult population affected and increased pressure on health services. (BBC)

Seven practical swaps can reduce plastic in the kitchen

Glass storage, wooden utensils and refillable containers offer simple routes away from plastic in food preparation and storage. The changes concentrate on heavily used items where wear and heat can increase contact with food. (Guardian)
