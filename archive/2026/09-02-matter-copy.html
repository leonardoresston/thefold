LEAD STORY

Burnham sets out a wider role for the state

Andy Burnham used his first Commons appearance as prime minister to promise more devolution and public control of essential services. Six weeks into the job, he blamed Brexit and the Thatcher settlement for Britain’s weak growth and said the country had taken a series of wrong turns over four decades. (Guardian, BBC)

The prime minister left open the renationalisation of the water industry and another look at electoral reform in Labour’s next manifesto. He also signalled a possible commitment to defence spending of 3% of GDP by 2030 and confirmed that he would attend the Cop31 climate summit in Turkey. (Guardian)

NEWS

Russian strike kills six Ukrainian railway workers

A ballistic missile struck a locomotive depot during a large bombardment of Kyiv and its surrounding region, killing six employees of Ukraine’s state railway. At least 12 people died across the attacks and another 12 were wounded, including three children, as Russia intensified its campaign against the rail network. (Guardian)

Nepal flood toll passes 1,000

More than 1,000 people are confirmed dead and more than 4,000 remain missing after the Nepal–Tibet floods. Rescue operations are still trying to reach hydropower workers caught in tunnels and isolated sites; the missing include 36 British nationals. (Guardian, BBC)

British activist deported after West Bank abduction

Israel has deported 22-year-old British activist Finn Joughin after he was forcibly abducted by settlers in the occupied West Bank. The case comes amid a surge in settler violence against Palestinians and growing pressure on the British government to impose sanctions. (Guardian, BBC)

Brazil’s top courts confront election and banking crises

TSE justice Dias Toffoli suspended part of presidential candidate Renan Santos’s campaign after demanding explanations for digital advertising across 16 social-media profiles that were not declared to electoral authorities. The order restricts online campaigning, debate appearances and access to public campaign money, drawing objections from jurists and members of the court. (Meio)

In a separate case, a Federal Police report says supreme court justice Alexandre de Moraes edited a R$131m contract between Banco Master and his wife’s law firm before it was signed. Messages obtained from former banker Daniel Vorcaro indicate at least six meetings with Moraes, deepening scrutiny of the relationship. (Folha, Meio)

MORE TO KNOW

Bodycote accepts £1.65bn US private-equity bid

American buyout firm Veritas has agreed to pay 940p a share for the Macclesfield-based metallurgy specialist, valuing its equity at £1.65bn and the business at £1.85bn including debt. Bodycote shares closed above the offer at 955p as investors weighed the possibility of a renewed bid from CVC. (Guardian)

Scotland presses ahead with basic food price cap

John Swinney has kept a legally binding cap on the price of bread, milk and other essentials in the Scottish government’s programme. Farming and business groups oppose the plan, which the first minister presented as protection for households facing higher food costs. (Guardian)

Brazil targets R$18.6bn primary surplus

Brazil’s proposed 2027 budget projects a primary surplus of R$18.6bn. The plan arrives during an election campaign dominated by scrutiny of public spending and the distribution of congressional amendments. (Meio)

Anthropic cuts the cost of its new Claude models

Anthropic has launched Claude Fable 5.1 and Mythos 5.1 with changes aimed at price, data retention and safety controls. The company says Fable 5.1 is typically about 25% cheaper than its predecessor and can cost up to 45% less on complex agentic work. (The Verge, MacMagazine)

Perplexity splits AI work between Mac and cloud

Perplexity has introduced hybrid computing for its Personal Computer agent on Apple-silicon Macs. Sensitive tasks can run locally while heavier work moves to cloud models, extending the local-first approach already used by its Portable Computer product. (MacStories, MacMagazine)

Firefox adds an ad blocker on iPhone

Mozilla is rolling out an integrated advertising blocker in Firefox for iOS. The release brings blocking into the browser itself on a platform where extensions have historically offered fewer options than on desktop. (MacMagazine)

Ternus opens Apple tenure with launch preview

John Ternus used his first staff memo as Apple chief executive to preview a major product launch next week. His first day also brought further changes at the top, including Luca Maestri’s departure from leadership of corporate services. (The Verge, MacMagazine)

CULTURE & SPORT

Jury convicts Duane Davis of Tupac Shakur’s murder

A Las Vegas jury found former South Side Compton Crips leader Duane Keith Davis guilty of the 1996 murder of Tupac Shakur. Jurors deliberated for about three hours in a case built heavily on Davis’s own accounts of the killing, including his 2019 memoir. (Guardian)

Emily Wilson starts a new Odyssey translation

Emily Wilson will translate Homer’s Odyssey from scratch less than a decade after her acclaimed 2017 version made her the first woman to publish a complete English translation of the epic. The new project follows the release of Christopher Nolan’s film adaptation. (Guardian)

Britain loses one of its most prolific graffiti artists

Helch, whose towering tags became fixtures of high-rise Britain, has died. His work appeared across London and beyond, surviving redevelopment and even drawing the displeasure of Queen Elizabeth II after he painted a castle visible from Windsor. (Guardian)

Gabriel Jesus leaves Arsenal for Barcelona

Gabriel Jesus completed his move from Arsenal to Barcelona on the final day of the summer transfer window. The deal closes a significant part of Arsenal’s attacking reshuffle after the club’s perfect start to the Premier League season. (Guardian)
