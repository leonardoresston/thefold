LEAD STORY

Facial recognition is heading to Oxford Street

The Metropolitan police plans to deploy live facial recognition cameras on Oxford Street, Britain’s busiest shopping street. Sadiq Khan has indicated that the newly pedestrianised road will be included in a wider West End rollout expected by Christmas. (Guardian)

The cameras compare faces passing through a defined area with police watchlists and alert officers to possible matches. Civil liberties groups have challenged the accuracy and proportionality of the technology, while Britain still has no law written specifically to govern its use. (Guardian)

NEWS

Two West Bank families were trapped inside their homes

Settlers and soldiers cut water and electricity to two Palestinian homes in Qusra and blocked relatives from bringing food or medical care. One family, including two young children, had been confined since Sunday as the group outside sought control of the properties. (Guardian)

Simon Levy received a whole-life sentence

The 40-year-old serial sex offender will die in prison for murdering two women and raping a third. Police and prosecution failures after the first killing had left him free to attack again. (Guardian, BBC)

Karoline Leavitt will leave the White House

Donald Trump said his press secretary will step down at the end of August to spend more time with her family. Leavitt, 28, returned from maternity leave last month after giving birth to her daughter in May. (Guardian, BBC)

A survivor emerged 36 hours after Colombia’s earthquake

Emergency workers pulled a 32-year-old woman alive from beneath concrete slabs after a ten-hour rescue in Cali. Crews continued searching the wreckage left by the 7.4-magnitude earthquake. (BBC)

Lula and Alcolumbre broke a legislative logjam

Lula met Senate president Davi Alcolumbre at the Alvorada Palace and unlocked part of the electoral agenda tied to the president’s re-election plans. A proposal to end the six-day working week still has no date for a vote. (Folha)

Prosecutors challenged candidacies linked to organised crime

Electoral prosecutors in Rio de Janeiro are using family ties to accused criminals as grounds to bar candidates. They argue that political heirs can inherit and deploy the structures built by their sponsors. (Folha)

Heatwaves may have cost Britain £4.4bn

Repeated extreme heat through June and July may have cut UK economic output by £4.4bn. The Verdant thinktank estimates the annual cost could exceed £25bn by 2030 and is calling for cooler urban design and a maximum workplace temperature. (Guardian)

A ship paid about $4m to jump the Panama Canal queue

Commercial vessels face sharply higher transit costs as low water linked to El Niño reduces capacity and the Iran war disrupts global shipping. One container ship reportedly paid about $4m, or £3m, to move ahead of waiting traffic. (Guardian)

TECHNOLOGY

Google launched four Pixel 11 phones

The Pixel 11 range includes standard, Pro, Pro XL and Pro Fold models, led by a new Tensor G6 chip and expanded camera software. US prices start at $899 for the base phone, $1,099 for the Pro, $1,299 for the Pro XL and $1,899 for the foldable. (The Verge, MacMagazine)

Twitch offered an opt-out from Amazon AI training

Streamers can stop future Amazon generative-AI models from training on their broadcasts, clips, chats, images and channel text. The control does not reverse training already completed. (The Verge)

ICE sought $20m of electric-shock gloves

The US immigration agency plans to equip officers with gloves designed to deliver painful electric shocks and compel compliance. A Department of Homeland Security notice calls for delivery by March under a procurement worth as much as $20m. (The Verge, BBC)

A smart-feeder outage left pets waiting for meals

Petlibro’s cloud service failed, stopping some connected feeders from dispensing scheduled food and taking litter boxes and water fountains offline. Devices with schedules stored locally continued to work. (The Verge)

CULTURE

Countries at war can no longer host Eurovision

Broadcasters will be barred from hosting when their country is involved in an armed conflict or sensitive geopolitical situation. Bulgaria is due to stage the 2027 contest after Dara won in May. (Guardian, BBC)

EJ Swift won the Arthur C Clarke award

When There Are Wolves Again, a near-future novel about rewilding and climate activism in Britain, took the 2026 science-fiction prize. Swift was previously shortlisted in 2023. (Guardian)

Rod Stewart cancelled his remaining farewell dates

The 81-year-old singer cancelled the coming shows after the successful insertion of a coronary stent. He said he was already feeling better following the procedure. (Guardian)

SPORTS

PSG beat Aston Villa to lift the Super Cup

Paris Saint-Germain defeated Aston Villa 2–1 in Salzburg after goals from Khvicha Kvaratskhelia and Désiré Doué. Brian Madjo equalised before half-time and became the competition’s youngest scorer at 17. (Guardian)

LIFESTYLE

Millions watched Europe fall briefly dark

A total solar eclipse crossed parts of Spain, Iceland and Greenland, while more than 95% of the sun was covered in Cornwall. Temperatures dropped and birds fell quiet as the moon’s shadow moved across western Europe. (Guardian, BBC)

Reading produced Britain’s first home-grown chocolate bar since 1932

A master’s student harvested glasshouse cocoa and combined it with British-grown sugar and milk to make a bar entirely from UK ingredients. The experiment highlights the growing difficulty of cultivating cocoa in its traditional tropical regions as the climate changes. (Guardian, BBC)
