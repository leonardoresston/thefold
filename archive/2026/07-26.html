LEAD STORY

Wildfires forced a quarter-million people from France and Spain

Wildfires pushed France and Spain into a wider emergency on Saturday. Authorities said more than 250,000 people fled, including about 70,000 in central Spain and 197,000 in France, as strong winds drove blazes toward Bordeaux and smoke warnings reached Madrid. (Guardian, BBC)

The fire emergency cut straight through summer travel. Families visiting France had to turn holidays into evacuation journeys, while firefighters tried to hold lines across towns, resorts and rural communities under heavy heat and wind. (Guardian, BBC)

NEWS

Reform UK donations entered a police inquiry

Scotland Yard opened an investigation into two £250,000 donations to Reform UK made before the 2024 general election through Britain Means Business, a company controlled by deputy leader Richard Tice. The inquiry puts party finance back into Westminster's immediate pressure points. (Guardian, BBC)

Burnham put care, youth jobs and devolution first

Andy Burnham is preparing early moves on social care, youth unemployment and devolution after his first week in No 10. The package gives the new government a first domestic test across care staffing, regional power and the route into work for younger people. (BBC)

The UK's largest Muslim convention opened under heavier security

Jalsa Salana in Hampshire went ahead with several hundred extra security staff and closer coordination with police, emergency services and local authorities. The enhanced protection followed rising anti-Muslim hate crime and the early closure of another large Islamic gathering last month after arrests over a suspected far-right threat. (Guardian)

Southport families faced a prison-hospital turn

Axel Rudakubana, now 19, was transferred from prison to a psychiatric hospital after receiving a life sentence for the murders of Alice da Silva Aguiar, Bebe King and Elsie Dot Stancombe and the attempted murder of eight other children and two adults. A lawyer for the victims' families warned that the case was still causing fresh harm from custody. (Guardian)

Brazil's election fight pulled in Washington and Buenos Aires

Brazil denied visas to two US State Department officials expected in the country before the election calendar. The TSE later said the US embassy had requested contact with the court without setting out the agenda. (Folha, Folha)

The PL formalised Flávio Bolsonaro's presidential candidacy at Pacaembu, in São Paulo. Javier Milei's appearance at the convention became an institutional clash after the Argentine president attacked STF minister Alexandre de Moraes; STF president Edson Fachin rebuked the intervention hours later. (Folha, Folha, Folha)

PT launched Haddad for São Paulo governor

The PT confirmed Fernando Haddad as its candidate for São Paulo governor at a state convention with Lula and Geraldo Alckmin. The campaign opened with appeals to women voters and attacks on Tarcísio de Freitas and Flávio Bolsonaro, keeping São Paulo at the centre of the national race. (Folha, Folha)

US food-benefit cuts reached children

More than 1 million children in the United States lost access to Snap food benefits after the 2025 Trump spending bill. A Center on Budget and Policy Priorities analysis found that more than 4 million Americans were removed from the programme between July 2025 and March 2026. (Guardian)

India's education minister fell after student protests

Dharmendra Pradhan resigned after a week of mass demonstrations over an exam leak scandal. The departure gave student protesters a rare national victory and created a new test for Narendra Modi's government. (Guardian)

Japan's spending plan unsettled investors

Sanae Takaichi's government wants to inject ¥370tn, about £1.7tn, into 17 industrial sectors by 2040. The scale of the plan has left investors and members of her own party questioning how Japan will fund the programme without triggering a market shock. (Guardian)

Rosebank drilling hit a North Sea delay

Production at Britain's biggest undeveloped oil and gasfield could be delayed for months after equipment from a rig was accidentally dropped into the North Sea. The setback lands while Burnham's government weighs whether to grant the Rosebank licence under pressure from industry and climate campaigners. (Guardian)

UK savers found higher summer rates

Competition among banks pushed easy-access savings rates as high as 5%, fixed-rate bonds close to that level and regular savings accounts up to 8%. The improvement gives cash savers a clearer return after months of pressure from prices and bills. (Guardian)

TECHNOLOGY

Google signalled a Pixel 11 price rise

Google's devices chief Shakil Barkat all but confirmed that the next Pixel phone will cost more than the Pixel 10. The pressure follows RAM supply strain tied to AI data-centre demand and a broader run of hardware price increases across consumer technology. (The Verge)

Warner Bros sued Amazon over staff poaching

Warner Bros Discovery filed suit against Amazon, accusing the company of illegally poaching employees including Pia Barlow, a former senior vice-president for originals marketing. The case extends the streaming fight from libraries and subscribers into executive talent. (The Verge)

Alternative iPhone app stores reached Brazil quietly

Alternative app stores became available to iPhone users in Brazil after weeks of regulatory headlines, but the immediate practical change looked narrow. Apple's platform rules and developer incentives still leave the new market with limited everyday reach. (MacMagazine)

Teachers objected to a humanoid classroom pilot

A New York high school on the Allegany territory of the Seneca nation plans to introduce a humanoid machine and an AI teaching assistant. Teachers and local Native American residents criticised the pilot as inappropriate and culturally tone deaf. (Guardian)

CULTURE

The Odyssey became a box-office and bookshelf event

Christopher Nolan's adaptation took $264m, about £196m, over its opening weekend, sent translations of Homer's poem up bestseller lists and sold out London BFI Imax screenings deep into September. The film also pulled the trade in ancient artefacts into the spotlight after Zendaya wore earrings allegedly made from Iranian objects dating to the first millennium BC. (Guardian, Guardian)

The D4vd case moved through court evidence

Evidence presented in a Los Angeles courtroom indicated that singer D4vd, born David Anthony Burke, had impregnated Celeste Rivas Hernandez when she was 13 before the alleged fatal stabbing. Text messages also indicated that Rivas Hernandez later received an abortion. (Guardian)

Lou Koller died at 59

Lou Koller, singer and co-founder of New York hardcore band Sick of It All, died after cancer of the oesophagus. The band he helped form in 1986 became one of the defining names in New York hardcore. (Guardian)

Hiroshima's memory gained a new witness text

A newly surfaced memoir by Kiyoshi Tanimoto offered a detailed account of the day the atomic bomb fell on Hiroshima, including his journey across the devastated city to find his wife and child. The extract arrives as survivor testimony grows more fragile with time. (Guardian)

Manhunter returned in 4K

Michael Mann's 1986 thriller returned with a 4K restoration and director's cut, adding three minutes of footage before a US theatrical rollout. The anniversary release puts an early high point in Mann's crime cinema back on large screens. (Guardian)

SPORTS

The World Cup gave way to its postscript

The 2026 World Cup's closing memory reel stretched from Erling Haaland's mark on Norway to Scotland's travelling support in Boston, Curaçao's first tournament goal and Turkey's early exit. The tournament is already being fixed in public memory through moments beyond the final. (Guardian)

Arsenal explored Vinícius Júnior

Arsenal made an early inquiry over a possible move for Vinícius Júnior from Real Madrid. The Brazil forward has one year left on his Bernabéu contract and scored 22 goals for Real last season, plus four for Brazil at the World Cup. (Guardian)

Premier League transfer spending accelerated

The English transfer record has already been broken during a busy summer, with Elliot Anderson and Morgan Rogers among the major moves. Champions Arsenal added Christos Tzolis for £34m and still face decisions on wide players, Alex Scott and defensive cover. (Guardian)

LIFESTYLE

England's riverbanks remained out of reach

Almost three-quarters of England's riverbanks have no public access, according to data released by the Right to Roam campaign. The finding renews pressure for a right of access to water as hotter summers send more people toward rivers and banks. (Guardian)

AI helped reconnect a long-separated family

A family search spanning India, Canada and the UK turned on an AI-assisted match after Avtar Singh spent decades wondering what had happened to his mother and Nicci carried the story of a half-brother given away before she was born. The case shows the technology moving into intimate family history as well as work and search. (Guardian)

GLP-1 use moved into relationships

Millions of people are using GLP-1 weight-loss drugs, and doctors already warn patients about possible side-effects. The effects on romantic relationships are now becoming part of the medical and social conversation around the treatment boom. (Guardian)

Puffer jackets faced the sustainability test

Modern insulated jackets mix petroleum-based synthetics with animal and plant fibres, leaving buyers to weigh filler, durability and ethical trade-offs before buying new. The cold-weather staple is becoming another test of whether quality can reduce waste. (Guardian)

Holiday car hire still hid expensive extras

One in eight people who hired a car in the past two years ended up paying more than the first quote, according to Which? Unclear terms and add-ons are turning cheap-looking summer rentals into a familiar travel cost trap. (Guardian)
