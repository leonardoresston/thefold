LEAD STORY

US and Iran exchange fire in the Strait of Hormuz

The US and Iran have exchanged fire for the first time in a month. American missiles and drones struck Iranian rocket launchers on an island in the Strait of Hormuz after Washington said Tehran was preparing to deploy more mines; Iran retaliated against US bases in Jordan and the United Arab Emirates. (Guardian)

Donald Trump threatened further military action as the US tried to loosen Iran’s hold on shipping through the waterway. The strait carried a fifth of the world’s seaborne energy supply before the war, leaving each new confrontation exposed to global economic consequences. (Guardian)

NEWS

Nepal flood toll reaches 939 as rescuers seek trapped workers

Rescue teams are trying to reach more than 900 missing hydropower workers after flash floods swept through Nepal. Hundreds may be trapped in tunnels and underground areas at 12 projects; the disaster has killed 939 people in Nepal and another 16 in Tibet, with more than 4,400 missing across the border region. (Guardian, BBC)

Russia sends 800 faster drones into Ukraine

Russia fired about 800 Shahed drones into Ukraine over four days, subjecting Kyiv to hours of daytime air raids. The new jet-powered models cruise at 200mph to 250mph, roughly twice the speed of piston-engine versions and beyond the reach of Ukraine’s cheaper interceptors. (Guardian)

Augusto Cury jumps to third in Brazil’s presidential race

Augusto Cury reached 11% in the latest BTG/Nexus first-round poll, up nine percentage points from the previous survey. Lula led on 37% and Flávio Bolsonaro had 31%, while Cury pulled support from both and opened a clear gap over the remaining candidates. (Folha, Meio)

£110m diverted to speed up new prison cells

The government has fast-tracked £110m from existing budgets to accelerate construction of prison cells as England and Wales confront overcrowding. The funding follows a decision to narrow the early-release scheme due to begin in October. (BBC)

Bank of England warns AI shock could trigger downturn

The governor of the Bank of England has warned that volatility around artificial intelligence could cause a global economic downturn. Energy shocks from the US–Iran war have increased the risk surrounding the huge investment flowing into AI infrastructure. (BBC)

BT eyes £2bn copper windfall

BT is on course to make more than £2bn over the next decade by selling copper recovered as Britain’s phone network moves to full-fibre broadband. Prices have surged as datacentres, electronics and renewable energy projects compete for the metal. (Guardian)

TECHNOLOGY

AI spots heart disease in under two seconds

A new AI system can identify heart disease from a routine electrocardiogram in less than two seconds. Trained on millions of patient records, the tool extracts patterns beyond those normally visible to clinicians and could help move high-risk patients into treatment sooner. (Guardian)

Amazon accused of taking $20bn through ad surcharges

The US Federal Trade Commission and 22 states have sued Amazon over allegations that it secretly inflated advertising prices for years. Regulators say the charges generated $20bn and raised the cost of groceries and other essentials sold through the platform. (Guardian, The Verge)

Debian permits responsible use of AI-generated code

Debian developers have voted to allow AI tools in work on the Linux distribution’s code, maintenance and documentation. Contributions produced with generative systems remain subject to the project’s existing standards, with no special exemption or separate ban. (The Verge)

Apple leadership handover reaches the top

Tim Cook completed his final day as Apple chief executive after 15 years in charge, clearing the way for John Ternus to take over. Phil Schiller also stepped down from leadership of the App Store and Apple events while retaining his Apple Fellow title. (The Verge, MacMagazine, The Verge)

CULTURE

Met cancels John Galliano exhibition

New York’s Metropolitan Museum of Art has cancelled an exhibition honouring John Galliano after a backlash over the British designer’s 2011 conviction for antisemitic remarks. The show had been planned as the theme for the next Met Gala. (BBC, Guardian)

Dolly Parton laid to rest in Nashville

Dolly Parton has been buried beside her husband, Carl Thomas, at Woodlawn Memorial Park in Nashville. The private family ceremony followed the country singer’s death on 25 August at the age of 80. (Guardian)

British Museum gave Peter Thiel private tapestry viewing

The British Museum hosted Palantir founder Peter Thiel for a private viewing of the Bayeux tapestry on 18 August, before the exhibition opened to the public. Staff raised concerns because the fragile work has strict limits on how long it can be exposed to light. (Guardian)

SPORTS

Saka gives Arsenal victory at Aston Villa

Bukayo Saka scored the only goal as Arsenal beat Aston Villa 1–0 and extended their winning start to the Premier League season. Arsenal kept David Raya largely untroubled, while Villa opened a league campaign with two defeats and no goals for the first time since 2002. (Guardian)

Arsenal presses for Julián Álvarez before deadline

Arsenal is trying to persuade Julián Álvarez to leave Atlético Madrid after agreeing sales of Gabriel Martinelli, Gabriel Jesus and Fábio Vieira worth more than £70m combined. Álvarez favours Barcelona, though Atlético is refusing to sell him to a La Liga rival. (Guardian)

LIFESTYLE

Inflammation emerges as a target in mental health research

Researchers are examining links between persistent low-level inflammation and conditions including depression and anxiety. Some trials have found mood improvements from anti-inflammatory drugs, though the evidence does not support treating the immune response as a single cause or a universal route to therapy. (Guardian)

Small favours can become full-time family care

Health professionals are drawing attention to “caregiver creep”, the gradual expansion of occasional help for an ageing relative into continuous care. Recognising the change early can help families arrange practical support before exhaustion and isolation take hold. (Guardian)
