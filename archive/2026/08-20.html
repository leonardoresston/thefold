LEAD STORY

Zelenskyy fires senior aide as Ukraine’s political crisis deepens

Volodymyr Zelenskyy has dismissed presidential deputy chief of staff Iryna Mudra while officials investigate an alleged money-laundering scheme. Reports of searches at her home preceded the move; the presidential decree gave no reason for her removal. (Guardian)

The dismissal came one day after former defence minister Mykhailo Fedorov called for a wartime presidential election and accused the government of failing to control corruption. Fedorov’s removal in July prompted Ukraine’s largest street protests since Russia’s full-scale invasion. (Guardian)

NEWS

Israel opens criminal inquiry into Hind Rajab’s killing

Israel’s military has acknowledged that its troops fired on the car carrying five-year-old Hind Rajab and six relatives in Gaza in January 2024. It ordered a military-police investigation into their deaths and possible failures in coordinating the movement of the Palestine Red Crescent ambulance sent to rescue them. (Guardian)

Personalised vaccine kept melanoma from returning in trial

A personalised mRNA vaccine developed by Merck and Moderna prolonged the time that high-risk melanoma patients remained cancer-free when combined with Keytruda. The phase-three trial involved more than 1,000 people, though full results have yet to be published or peer-reviewed. (BBC)

Male prisons have fewer than 1,800 spaces left

More than 83,000 places in men’s prisons across England and Wales are occupied, leaving capacity at its lowest since March. The government plans to release about 5,000 inmates early from October after recalls for licence breaches and reoffending absorbed much of the space created by shorter sentences. (BBC)

Compulsory GCSE resits are harming teenagers’ wellbeing

UCL researchers found significantly lower mental wellbeing among students in England who missed grade 4 in English or maths, where resits are mandatory, than among comparable pupils in Wales and Northern Ireland. About 180,000 students are due to retake English and 175,000 maths this year. (Guardian)

Messages link Lulinha’s ally to a cannabis lobbying effort

Messages analysed by Brazil’s federal police show lobbyist Roberta Luchsinger claiming to speak for the president’s son and seeking a 20% fee on a medicinal-cannabis project offered to the government. A draft contract for a no-bid Health Ministry purchase reached Lula’s then chief of staff. (Meio, Folha)

The Milky Way’s fastest known star circles its black hole

Astronomers have detected S301 moving around Sagittarius A* at roughly 25,000km a second. Its tight 8.7-year orbit could allow scientists to measure the supermassive black hole’s spin within a decade and test how a rotating black hole warps spacetime. (Guardian)

Winter energy cap is forecast to reach a three-year high

Great Britain’s typical annual household bill is expected to rise 4% to £1,729 from October, according to Cornwall Insight. Higher gas prices would wipe out the average £45 saving from the government’s VAT cut on electricity. Ofgem sets the next cap on Wednesday. (Guardian)

Three companies face investigations over hidden fees

The Competition and Markets Authority has opened cases into Trainline, Virgin Atlantic and Red Driving School over prices that may exclude mandatory charges. The action forms part of a wider crackdown on illegal drip pricing. (Guardian)

TECHNOLOGY

Meta whistleblower tells jury the company knew children were at risk

Former safety engineer Arturo Béjar testified that Meta knew its products could expose children to sexual predators and violent material, and that warnings repeatedly reached senior executives. Twenty-nine US states accuse Meta of designing addictive products for young people; the company denies the allegations. (Guardian)

Unitree shares jump sixfold on their market debut

Shares in the world’s largest humanoid-robot maker rose more than 600% on their first day of trading in China. Unitree has built a global audience through videos of robots performing martial arts, running at high speed and dancing with pop stars. (Guardian)

Google gives Gemini a student hub

Gemini is adding a dedicated space for research notebooks, flashcards and practice quizzes, with support for graphs, images and test dates. Google is also offering students one free year of its AI Plus plan. (The Verge, MacMagazine)

CULTURE

Glória Menezes died aged 91

The actor who became one of Brazilian television’s defining stars has died at 91. Her six-decade career encompassed landmark telenovelas, cinema and theatre. (Meio)

Demon Hunter sues Netflix over KPop Demon Hunters

The Christian metal band says the name of Netflix’s hit film creates substantial confusion with the group it has used for 25 years. The lawsuit asks for damages and seeks to stop Netflix using the title on merchandise and future projects. (Guardian)

SPORTS

Arsenal agree £51m deal for Ezri Konsa

Arsenal have agreed to sign the England defender from Aston Villa for £51m. Villa also rejected an offer worth almost £40m from Al-Hilal for Ollie Watkins. (Guardian)

Celtic take a three-goal lead in Champions League playoff

Camilo Durán scored twice and Benjamin Nygren added another as Celtic beat LASK 3–0 in the first leg. The Scottish champions travel to Austria next week with a place in the Champions League group stage in sight. (Guardian, BBC)
