Nvidia board meets to decide Jensen Huang’s future as second affair allegation and espionage crisis wipe $600bn off chipmaker

Nvidia’s board of directors is convening to decide whether to dismiss co-founder and chief executive Jensen Huang after disclosures of a second extramarital affair—this time with a subordinate executive—sharply escalated a crisis triggered by his relationship with a suspected Chinese intelligence operative. The latest allegation centres on Kimberly Powell, Nvidia’s vice-president of healthcare, who reports directly to Huang and was allegedly filmed with him at a San Jose hotel during March’s GTC developer conference. On Sunday Huang sent a memo to staff admitting marital infidelity after revelations of his affair with Megaspeed executive Alice Huang, a Chinese businesswoman whom US intelligence believes helped facilitate shipments of advanced Nvidia chips to China. Although an independent board committee hired former acting Manhattan US attorney Joon Kim of Wachtell, Lipton, Rosen & Katz on Tuesday to lead an internal inquiry while stating that Huang would continue running the company without interruption, directors are now preparing to act before that probe concludes. (Guardian, Guardian, The Verge)

Shares in the $4tn (£3tn) chipmaker tumbled 8% in after-hours trading on Wednesday, erasing a daytime rebound lifted by strong results at AI cloud provider CoreWeave and bringing Nvidia’s market value loss since Friday to roughly $600bn. Under company bylaws, Huang, 63, who owns 3.6% of Nvidia and holds a personal fortune near $150bn, can be removed with or without cause by a simple board majority. His departure would leave an immediate vacuum at the centre of the global AI buildout: US tech giants plan to spend $750bn on AI infrastructure this year, much of it on Nvidia’s datacentre hardware, which grew 92% year on year in May to $75.2bn, and Huang oversees dozens of direct reports without an anointed successor. (Guardian, Guardian)

In Washington, Democratic Senators Elizabeth Warren and Chris Coons wrote to Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick, Secretary of State Marco Rubio and White House Chief of Staff Stephen Miller demanding the preservation of all records relating to Nvidia’s China export licences and Huang’s ties to Alice Huang. House Republican chairs John Moolenaar, Brian Mast and Rick Crawford warned that Huang faces subpoenas unless he agrees to a closed-door briefing on whether Beijing accessed privileged technology. The scandal has also complicated Donald Trump’s alliance with Huang, who lobbied for looser export rules, joined Trump’s delegation to meet Xi Jinping in Beijing in May and received the National Medal of Science in October; Trump said earlier this week that the FBI was investigating and that he believed Huang never knew Alice Huang was a spy. (Guardian)

Ministers threaten statutory crackdown on entertainment industry over Walliams and Brand as Milburn review targets under-25 sickness benefits

Culture Secretary Lisa Nandy will summon television, film and publishing executives to a roundtable next week to warn that ministers will legislate to compel participation in the Creative Industries Independent Standards Authority (CIISA) unless the sector strengthens safeguards for women, children and whistleblowers. The warning follows Monday’s ten-and-a-half-year rape sentence for Russell Brand and allegations that comedian and children’s author David Walliams groomed teenage fans as young as 15 and harassed junior staff. Equalities Minister Bridget Phillipson, Justice Secretary Alex Norris, Commons committee chair Olivia Bailey and campaigner Zelda Perkins called for tighter curbs on non-disclosure agreements and privacy injunctions covering freelancers, volunteers and fans. Walliams—who denies breaking the law or acting as a sexual predator while admitting he sometimes behaved thoughtlessly in casual relationships—has had his cameo cut from the Australian film adaptation of his 2019 book Fing!, dropped by Sky in the UK and Stan in Australia, while Thames Valley Police confirmed officers removed Brand from ex-girlfriend Jemima Goldsmith’s Oxfordshire home in July 2014 after a reported violent incident. (Guardian, BBC, Guardian, BBC)

In welfare policy, former Labour health secretary Alan Milburn will publish a review on Tuesday proposing that claimants under 25 be removed from the health element of universal credit and personal independence payment and placed on basic universal credit, alongside a targeted cost allowance, higher severe-disability payments and billions for job placements and wage subsidies. Backed by Prime Minister Andy Burnham to cut the nearly one million 18- to 24-year-olds out of education, work or training, the overhaul will be legislated in the new year with penalties for refusing support. Meanwhile, Lord Cameron warned that Burnham’s openness to rejoining the EU risked weakening Britain’s hand ahead of Thursday’s Dublin talks and next week’s Brussels summit; Reform UK general secretary Tom Waterhouse resigned after Hampshire council leader George Madgwick was charged with 12 counts of rape; Phillipson criticised Kemi Badenoch for dropping Tory support for banning conversion practices; and a Wigan inquiry heard Burnham warned before entering No 10 that lorries at Whistl’s Astley warehouse endangered schoolchildren. (BBC, BBC, Guardian, Guardian, Guardian)

Across policing, His Majesty’s Inspectorate of Constabulary warned that officers have treated biased retrospective facial recognition matches as evidence without corroboration, putting ethnic minorities at risk of wrongful arrest while forces unlawfully keep custody photos. On Wednesday, 1,000 schools in Scotland, 300 in Northern Ireland and 6,000 in Italy received hoax bomb emails, while L/Cpl Nicholas Horne was remanded for court martial charged with raping a servicewoman in Cyprus, and Prestwick DJ James Campbell, 32, was named as the Briton killed in Saturday’s Kraków shooting. (Guardian, Guardian, Guardian, Guardian)

Russia and Ukraine agree record prisoner swap in Abu Dhabi as Democrat Scott Colom wins Mississippi Senate upset and strike halts Spain

Russia and Ukraine have agreed to the largest prisoner exchange of the war—freeing more than 1,000 captives on each side—after US-brokered talks in Abu Dhabi led by Jared Kushner and Steve Witkoff, who meet Vladimir Putin in Moscow on Thursday before briefing Volodymyr Zelenskyy in Dublin. Speaking alongside Irish Taoiseach Micheál Martin, Zelenskyy saw a window for peace before 2027 if US envoys returned with specifics, while Ukrainian delegate Davyd Arakhamia noted progress on security guarantees. Russian negotiator Vladimir Medinsky insisted Moscow would accept peace only on its own terms, demanded Ukrainian-held Donetsk, dismissed European envoys as hotel tourists, and called for a new Yalta and Potsdam, as Russian strikes killed three people in Odesa and Ukrainian drones hit five fuel tanks at Russia’s Baltic port of Ust-Luga. (Guardian, Guardian, BBC)

In the United States, Democrat Scott Colom unseated Republican Senator Cindy Hyde-Smith in Mississippi after a week of counting across 82 counties, becoming the state’s first Black US senator since Reconstruction in 1875, its first popularly elected Black senator, and its first Democratic senator since 1982. A former district attorney whose federal judgeship nomination by Joe Biden was blocked by Hyde-Smith, Colom campaigned at barber shops, Waffle Houses and a Donald Trump rally in Corinth, turning cattle rancher Hyde-Smith’s remark that voters worried about beef prices had “so many proteins to choose from” into a viral burger advert and taco truck tour that lifted the Democratic Senate majority to at least 55 seats. Off Destin, Florida, the retired liner SS United States was sunk on Wednesday night to form the world’s largest artificial reef. (Guardian, Guardian, Guardian)

Across Spain, millions joined the country’s first general strike in 14 years on Wednesday over a housing crisis that pushed rents up 80% and home prices up 63% in a decade, putting an average 50-square-metre Madrid flat at €1,170 (£1,000) a month—nearly half the €2,460 average gross wage—following the eviction and death of 87-year-old tenant Maricarmen Abascal. Backed by tenants’ groups and the CCOO and UGT unions, the stoppage reached 90% in ports and car plants, blocked Barcelona’s AP-7 motorway and drew 120,000 marchers in Madrid before clashes in Barcelona. Socialist Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez, facing a 29 November snap election after rightwing parties defeated his housing bill, praised union gains, whereas opposition leader Alberto Núñez Feijóo and Madrid president Isabel Díaz Ayuso called the unions a “mafia”. (Guardian, BBC)

Climate Action Tracker warns of 2.7C warming overshoot and Gulf war oil slicks spread as Burnham hails £12bn Norfolk wind deal

The world faces an irrecoverable overshoot of the 1.5C Paris limit, with current policies projected to heat the planet by 2.7C by 2100—up from 2.6C last year—and full delivery of national pledges still yielding 1.9C, Climate Action Tracker warned on Thursday at Cop31 in Antalya. Although high fossil fuel prices from the Iran war and clean energy investment double that of fossil fuels are projected by Nature Climate Change to trim global emissions by 0.4% in 2026, developing nations face delays because major donors including France, Germany and Cop31 co-host Australia have yet to set post-2026 finance commitments toward the $300bn-a-year goal for 2035. Meanwhile, satellite analysis by Greenpeace and Pax mapped over 500 oil spills covering 20,000 square kilometres—nearly the size of Wales—across the Strait of Hormuz, Persian Gulf and Arabian Sea since the US-Iran conflict began on 28 February, after 133 tanker attacks and strikes on Lavan Island storage tanks. (Guardian, Guardian, Guardian, Meio)

Ahead of speaking at Cop31, Andy Burnham hailed a £12.8bn financing deal led by RWE and KKR to build the 2.8-gigawatt Vanguard West and East windfarms off Norfolk—powering three million homes by 2030 and supporting 2,300 jobs—alongside SSE’s Berwick Bank B in Scotland. Seeking to repair its green record after approving the Jackdaw gasfield and rejecting the £800m Botley West solar farm, the government committed a £1bn National Wealth Fund loan despite developers holding a 20-year contract at £91.20 per megawatt hour. Meanwhile, Labour’s climate caucus urged Deputy Prime Minister Louise Haigh against cutting the 2030 electric vehicle mandate from 80% to 60%–65%; Environment Agency data showed banned PFOS and PFOA chemicals in English rivers fell 30% and 29% over a decade; England and Scotland ordered flocks of 50 or more birds indoors against avian flu; and Argentina told the maritime tribunal in Hamburg ahead of a 28 November ruling that UK oil drilling at the Falklands’ Sea Lion field echoed 17th-century imperial claims. (Guardian, Guardian, Guardian, BBC, Guardian, Guardian)

Brazil negotiates R$80bn fiscal package touching health and education floors as Senate summons PF chief over Banco Master and Harvard returns skulls

Finance Minister Dario Durigan and Senate President Davi Alcolumbre are negotiating an R$80bn (£11bn) fiscal adjustment for 2027—0.5% of GDP—authorised by President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva before departing for Cop31 in Turkey and ASEAN talks in Manila provided campaign pledges remain untouched. Tabled this week as a complementary bill ahead of a wider 2027–30 package, the plan removes extraordinary oil and gas revenues from Brazil’s constitutional floors of 15% of net current revenue for health and 18% of tax revenue for education, while Central Bank chief Gabriel Galípolo warned against artificial rate caps. In Congress, lawmakers filed 179 amendments to exempt diet soda, pickup trucks, draught beer, speedboats, ride-hailing cars and communion wine from the “sin tax”, and business groups lobbied Alcolumbre over ending the 6x1 work week after Justice Flávio Dino confirmed the 40-hour limit starts in December 2027. After a study showed R$61bn in budget amendments failed to stop Centrão parties losing 20 Chamber seats to Jair Bolsonaro’s PL, União Brasil and PP backed Alcolumbre’s February re-election in exchange for ministries and burying a parliamentary inquiry into Banco Master, though Alcolumbre faced criticism over a R$6m Senate tender to lease 95 iPhone 18 Pro handsets and 330 other phones. (Meio, Meio, Folha, Folha, Folha)

The Banco Master crisis deepened on Wednesday as two Senate committees invited Justice Minister Wellington Lima e Silva, Federal Police chief Andrei Rodrigues and removed anti-corruption coordinators Daniel Mostardeiro Cola and Wedson Cajé Lopes to a joint hearing on 18 November. Although Supreme Court dean Gilmar Mendes defended Rodrigues after the PF chief ordered officers to strip value judgments from reports on jailed banker Daniel Vorcaro’s ties to six justices, officials view Rodrigues’s position as fragile ahead of creating a Public Security Ministry. Elsewhere, Dino ordered a PF inquiry into suspected foreign funding of Flávio Bolsonaro’s campaign via Meta and the US Rockbridge Network; a Quaest poll found 62% of voters feel unrepresented by any party; São Paulo Governor Tarcísio de Freitas named five former Bolsonaro officials including Ricardo Salles to his cabinet; and Brazil repatriated two 19th-century skulls of enslaved Afro-Brazilians from Harvard’s Peabody Museum—including a suspected rebel from Salvador’s 1835 Malê uprising and a young woman exhumed in Rio during Louis Agassiz’s expedition—for burial. (Folha, Folha, Folha, Folha, Folha, Guardian)

MPs accuse Poundland owner of asset-stripping ahead of High Court rescue hearing as UK diesel stays near £2 and Nissan cuts petrol output

Poundland’s management goes to the High Court on Thursday seeking a ten-working-day extension to creditor protection for the 600-store chain and its 12,000 staff after US distressed-investment firm Gordon Brothers released £19m from £50m in frozen company cash to restock for Christmas. After grilling Gordon Brothers European head Mark Newton-Jones on Wednesday, Commons business committee chair Liam Byrne accused the firm—which bought Poundland from Pepco for £1 in June 2025—of acting like a “wolf on Wall Street, on the British High Street” by extracting millions while starving the retailer of its own cash and rejecting three whole-company rescue bids from buyers including Martin Property Group and Xyra Group to profit from selling store parcels to Tesco, B&M and Poundstretcher, despite chief executive Barry Williams delivering an £18.1m annual profit and an 11.4% sales rise. (Guardian, BBC)

In transport and manufacturing, UK unleaded petrol has fallen 6p from October’s peak to 168p a litre—with the AA forecasting another 10p drop after wholesale costs dipped below 62p—yet diesel remains at 197p as Federal Reserve governor Christopher Waller told a London conference that Strait of Hormuz war-risk insurance, ship-to-ship transfers and tight refining capacity are adding $20 to $30 a barrel to oil. High pump prices helped lift UK used electric car sales by 56% year on year in October to a 10% market share on Autotrader, while Nissan cut its annual global sales forecast to between 2.97 million and 3 million vehicles and scaled back petrol production after China’s EV price war cut its nine-month Chinese sales by 32%, with chief executive Ivan Espinosa urging Brussels to include Sunderland-built cars in “Made in Europe” rules. (Guardian, Guardian)

Brussels proposes Digital Fairness Act targeting loot boxes and dark patterns as researchers flag unpatched iOS flaw and DeepMind unveils AlphaGenome 2

The European Commission unveiled its draft Digital Fairness Act on Wednesday, proposing fines of up to 6% of global turnover to ban manipulative dark patterns, addictive “rabbit-hole” social media feeds, relentless notifications, undisclosed influencer promotions, hidden pricing and video game loot boxes or virtual currencies sold to children—measures EU Commissioner Michael McGrath estimated will save consumers €13.7bn ($15.1bn) annually. In mobile security, crypto exchange Binance urged iPhone users to delete sideloaded apps after researchers @0xjohnny and @forcequitOS published a proof of concept for worse_query, an unpatched flaw in iOS 26.7.1 through iOS 27.3 beta 1 that bypasses the iOS sandbox so malicious apps can read local files from other apps, including crypto keys and WhatsApp data. Meanwhile, Apple met Thai Digital Economy Minister Chaichanok Chidchob after mercenary spyware alerts across 99 countries hit Thai politicians, and Cupertino police arrested Apple employee Eric Chen Su on vehicular manslaughter charges after a 150km/h crash beside Apple Park killed a nanny and a two-year-old child. (The Verge, 9to5Mac, MacMagazine, 9to5Mac, MacMagazine)

Across Apple’s ecosystem, iOS 27.3 beta adds a Global Privacy Control toggle in Safari to reject cookie banners alongside vertical tabs, iOS 27 brings Apple Intelligence video summaries and search to HomeKit Secure Video on 2TB-plus iCloud+ tiers, CNET’s 32-phone battery test crowned the iPhone 18 Pro Max and foldable iPhone Duo ahead of the Pixel 11 Pro XL, smoke tests by Geekerwan showed the touchscreen PROLED XDR MacBook Pro vents heat through its hinge in clamshell mode, and Chinese supplier Quick Intelligent Equipment pointed to camera-equipped AirPods and Apple smart glasses. In AI, Google brought hands-free Gemini Live to Apple CarPlay and unveiled DeepMind’s AlphaGenome 2—trained on 100 million single-cell profiles across 68 tissues to double detection of disease-linked gene variants—while Anthropic launched Claude Meetings, Whiteboards and Sheets, OpenAI released ChatGPT Finances for macOS, Brazil barred unsupervised school AI up to fifth grade, and United Airlines blocked third-party bots such as OpenAI’s Dots and Meta’s Muse from booking flights because agents cannot legally certify baggage safety. CD Projekt Red uses AI to restore stroke-hit Polish Geralt actor’s voice for Witcher 3 expansion as Nintendo defends cutting Mario cameos from Ocarina of Time

CD Projekt Red confirmed it is using Respeecher synthetic voice technology—with the full consent of Polish actor Jacek Rozenek—to restore Rozenek’s 2014 vocal tone as Geralt of Rivia in Songs of the Past, a new expansion for The Witcher 3: Wild Hunt arriving 11 years after the original game. After a 2019 stroke temporarily left Rozenek unable to speak and left his recovered voice deeper and easily fatigued, audio engineers trained a model on his 2014 lines so his newly recorded dialogue matches the original era, while localization producer Mikołaj Szwed said Rozenek’s natural older voice will be used for an aged Geralt in The Witcher 4. Elsewhere, Nintendo announced its Switch 2 remake of The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time has sold 5.2 million copies since 5 November—with players using its microphone ocarina mode to summon Epona by mimicking Family Guy’s Lois Griffin—as producers Eiji Aonuma and Hidemaro Fujibayashi defended cutting the 1998 original’s Hyrule Castle Mario portraits to preserve immersion, and insiders reported Skydance’s Marvel 1943: Rise of Hydra is nearing completion after cuts to length and interactivity. Gilded Roman bronze leg goes on display in Edinburgh as councils keep 95% of public art in storage and Coachella unveils 2027 lineup

A gilded bronze leg dug out of a Dumfriesshire bog in 1820 and kept for a century in the Duke of Buccleuch’s charter room goes on display on Saturday in Roman Scotland: Life on the Edge of Empire at the National Museum of Scotland in Edinburgh. Once dismissed as loot hacked from York by northern tribes, the “Mislington leg” is now identified by curator Dr Fraser Hunter as part of an equestrian statue of Emperor Antoninus Pius erected near Hawick around AD140, shown alongside two painted Mithras altars and a cavalry horse skeleton from Inveresk. Meanwhile, freedom-of-information requests across 225 English councils revealed local authorities hold more than 500,000 artworks—including works by David Hockney, Tracey Emin, LS Lowry and Antony Gormley—yet display only 26,856 (5%), with Rugby Borough Council the sole authority with over 100 works exhibiting all 257 while Kent and Derbyshire sold hundreds after shutting storage services. (Guardian, BBC)

In music and film, Coachella announced its April 2027 lineup in Indio, California, with Dua Lipa becoming the first British female solo headliner alongside SZA and Tame Impala, while Madonna returns to headline the dance tent 21 years after her 2006 appearance following her chart-topping album Confessions II, joined by Phoebe Bridgers, Robyn, Jorja Smith, Underworld and Lambrini Girls, and Paul McCartney and Ringo Starr set a January 2027 VetsAid reunion in Las Vegas. Tributes were also paid to founding Television guitarist Richard Lloyd, whose interplay with Tom Verlaine at CBGB and on 1977’s Marquee Moon shaped post-punk before his death from cancer aged 75, and former Texas teacher Irma P Hall, who won a Cannes jury prize in the Coen brothers’ The Ladykillers and an NAACP Image Award in Soul Food before dying aged 91. (Guardian, Meio, Guardian, Guardian)

Manchester United agree Pochettino framework ahead of Carrick decision as Kane breaks England cap record and IRS receives Infantino complaint

Manchester United have agreed a contract framework with United States coach Mauricio Pochettino to replace Michael Carrick, whose dismissal after one win in ten Premier League matches—leaving United 18th, their lowest position at this stage since 1986-87—follows Wednesday’s Monaco talks between Sir Jim Ratcliffe, chief executive Omar Berrada and football director Jason Wilcox and a board vote with the Glazer family. Pochettino, 54, is set to join on a fourth approach after United pay his $10m (£7.5m) US Soccer release clause ahead of visiting Liverpool on 22 November, when Marcus Rashford targets a return from injury. England manager Thomas Tuchel confirmed ahead of Thursday’s Nations League match against Croatia at Wembley that his contract through Euro 2028 contains no release clause, citing “unfinished business” after June’s World Cup semi-final defeat by Argentina as Dan Burn, Jarell Quansah and Arsenal’s Declan Rice start alongside captain Harry Kane, who receives a presentation from Peter Shilton on winning his record 126th men’s cap two weeks after claiming the Ballon d’Or. (Guardian, BBC, Guardian, Guardian)

In football governance, Washington lawyer Abed Ayoub filed a US Internal Revenue Service complaint alleging an employee paid by a Fifa-linked entity worked solely as a private housekeeper for Fifa president Gianni Infantino at his Miami home—a claim Fifa denied, saying Infantino pays domestic staff personally—as incoming House Judiciary chair Jamie Raskin scrutinises Fifa’s tax-exempt status and former president Sepp Blatter called Infantino a “dictator” in Der Spiegel for awarding Donald Trump a Fifa peace prize before the Iran war. Meanwhile, US Soccer withdrew from next week’s Under-17 World Cup in Qatar over Gulf conflict risks; Fifa confirmed the top four seeds (Spain, the US, Germany and England) will be kept apart until the semi-finals at the 2027 Women’s World Cup in Brazil while approving the 2031 US-led and 2035 UK bids; and tributes were paid to Football365 co-founder, former NME editor and TalkSport host Danny Kelly, who died of oesophageal cancer aged 69.
