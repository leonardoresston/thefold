Burnham prepared a cabinet for Monday’s handover

Ukraine’s defence shake-up reached the front line

Ukrainian soldiers criticised Volodymyr Zelenskyy’s removal of defence minister Mykhailo Fedorov after protests in Kyiv. The dismissal exposed wider frustration over command, equipment and wartime decision-making as the government tries to keep public unity around the war effort. (BBC, The Guardian)

US strikes on Iran entered a seventh night

The US launched another night of strikes on Iran as the Hormuz conflict escalated. Central Command said the attacks were aimed at degrading Iranian military capabilities, leaving the strait at the centre of a widening confrontation over energy routes and regional power. (The Guardian)

Russia tightened pressure on anti-war critics

Russian authorities barred opposition figure Boris Nadezhdin from running for parliament and held blogger Ilya Remeslo in custody. The cases extended the clampdown on dissent as Moscow narrows the space for public criticism of the war. (BBC)

Jeffrey Donaldson moved to appeal convictions

Former DUP leader Jeffrey Donaldson lodged appeal documents after his conviction for rape and other sexual offences against two children. The case now moves to Belfast’s court of appeal. (The Guardian, BBC)

Southern Water was fined over sewage dumping

Southern Water was fined £7m for illegally dumping sewage off the Kent coast. The penalty added another enforcement case to the long-running fight over water companies, coastal pollution and regulator pressure. (The Guardian)

Trump’s election speech drew a fact-check fight

Donald Trump used a primetime address to revive claims about Chinese interference in US elections. Critics warned the speech could prepare the ground for challenges to the midterms, while the available intelligence record did not show Beijing changed the 2020 result. (The Guardian, BBC, The Guardian)

Republicans threatened sanctions against Canada over wildfire smoke drifting across the US, even as fires burned on both sides of the border. The smoke brought dangerous air quality to millions of people and moved climate stress into a fresh political dispute. (The Guardian, The Guardian)

The Jackdaw gasfield faced a jobs test

Documents showed the Jackdaw gasfield would create only 27 direct full-time jobs, sharpening the argument over North Sea drilling as Burnham prepares climate and industrial decisions. The numbers put economic claims around new fossil-fuel projects under closer scrutiny. (The Guardian)

UK lenders loosened the first-home route

Banks and building societies expanded mortgages that require small deposits or none at all, giving some first-time buyers a route around the savings hurdle. The shift sits beside a weaker pensions picture, with almost half of working-age adults in the UK saving nothing into a pension. (The Guardian, The Guardian)

Brazil’s Pix amendments audit found widespread failures

Brazil’s federal audit court found irregularities in 82% of the Pix parliamentary amendments it examined, including overbilling, suspected procurement fraud, unsupported payments and transparency failures. The findings added fiscal pressure as Congress argues over budget rules and infrastructure spending. (Folha, Folha)

Apple passed Nvidia to regain the title of the world’s most valuable company, with its value reaching about $4.9tn after a run of record highs. The rally landed while Apple and the US Department of Justice were said to be exploring a settlement in the antitrust case. (The Guardian, MacMagazine, MacMagazine)

AWS customers saw trillion-dollar bills

Amazon Web Services customers received bills running as high as $1.5tn after a global glitch. The error turned a cloud-billing failure into a reminder of how much critical accounting now depends on automated infrastructure behaving exactly as expected. (The Guardian)

New malware hit games and Macs

A Florida man was arrested over the alleged theft of more than $200,000 in crypto through malware hidden in a Steam game. Security researchers also identified ClickLock Stealer, a macOS malware strain using fake CAPTCHA screens to steal passwords. (The Verge, MacMagazine)

Apple raised Apple Music prices, taking the US individual plan to $11.99 a month, the family plan to $19.99 and the student plan to $6.99. Apple One also became more expensive in Brazil and the US. (The Verge, MacMagazine)
