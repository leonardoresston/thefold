LEAD STORY

Five governments condemn Israel’s West Bank settlement plan

The UK, France, Germany, Italy and Canada have condemned Israel’s plan to expand settlements in the E1 area of the occupied West Bank. The project would place a large new settlement block outside Jerusalem and further weaken the prospect of a viable Palestinian state. (BBC, Guardian)

Foreign secretary Ed Miliband summoned Israel’s chargé d’affaires in protest. The E1 development risks severing the West Bank from East Jerusalem, while the British government said all settlements breach international law and damage a two-state solution. (Guardian, BBC)

NEWS

Ben-Gvir displays construction site for Palestinian gallows

Israel’s national security minister Itamar Ben-Gvir has posted a video from a construction site he says will become a hanging facility for Palestinians convicted of terrorism in military courts. The death-penalty law passed this year applies to Palestinians under the military system while excluding Jewish extremists accused of similar offences. (Guardian)

620,000 pupils face English or maths resits

About 620,000 year 11 pupils in England failed to reach grade 4 in English or maths and now face resits. Only one in seven older students retaking maths passed this summer, alongside one in six in English. The government plans a new level-one stepping-stone qualification for those receiving grade 2 or below. (Guardian)

Brazilian police identify Lulinha as indirect beneficiary

Brazil’s federal police say Fábio Luís Lula da Silva, the president’s son, indirectly benefited from a lobbyist’s attempts to secure government access for a medicinal-cannabis business. Lula met his son’s lawyer and asked that Lulinha answer the accusations as the investigation into suspected influence-peddling advances. (Folha, Meio)

Trump urges states to offer separate MMR vaccines

Donald Trump has signed an executive order advising US states to offer separate measles, mumps and rubella injections. The combined MMR vaccine is safe and highly effective, completing the schedule in two visits; separate shots can require six and risk reducing uptake. (Guardian)

Equalities minister warns racism and sexism are becoming normal

Bridget Phillipson says commonplace misogyny and racism are drawing young men towards violent online movements. The equalities minister accused politicians of helping to fuel hatred and warned that police responses to violence against women and girls remain inadequate. (Guardian)

French interceptions slow small-boat departures

French authorities have stopped hundreds of attempted Channel crossings this summer after adopting new interception tactics, according to the Home Office. The government says the change is making French operations more effective against departures bound for the UK. (BBC)

Evergrande founder is jailed for life

Hui Ka Yan has received a life sentence in China after pleading guilty to eight charges including fundraising fraud, bribery and misuse of funds. All his personal property will be confiscated; Evergrande Group was fined 8.8bn yuan after defaulting on most of its $300bn in liabilities. (Guardian)

US bond sell-off pushes borrowing costs higher worldwide

Government borrowing costs across the UK, Europe and Japan have climbed to their highest levels in decades as investors sell US bonds. Fears over American public finances and inflation from the war with Iran are feeding the move, with higher yields flowing into mortgages, business loans and government budgets. (Guardian)

TECHNOLOGY

Apple Music will label AI-made tracks

Apple Music plans to introduce labels for music created with artificial intelligence later this year. The service informed industry partners of the change as streaming platforms face a growing volume of synthetic tracks and pressure to tell listeners how recordings were made. (MacStories, MacMagazine)

Google Discover adds prompt-based feed controls

Google will let users describe what they want to see in Discover and use AI to tune the feed around that request. The feature is due to reach the Google app in the coming days and will remember preferences for later visits. (The Verge)

Framework investigates BIOS update that bricks laptops

Owners of Framework Laptop 13 machines with AMD Ryzen 7040-series mainboards say BIOS version 3.20 has left systems unusable on both Windows and Linux. Framework says it is addressing the problem, though the update remained available from its website at the time of publication. (The Verge)

CULTURE

Barbican’s conservatory sends 500 plants into new homes

Five hundred people collected cuttings from the Barbican conservatory before its three-year refurbishment. Two more adoption events will each distribute another 500 plants while the glasshouse closes for work within the centre’s £231m renewal programme; it is due to reopen in 2030. (Guardian)

Weezer turns its 20th album into a study of survival

Weezer’s conceptual 20th album examines the work of sustaining a band after three decades. Rivers Cuomo built the opening song, CEO, around a deliberate echo of the group’s 1994 debut single while using streaming data and spreadsheets to test what still connects with listeners. (Guardian)

SPORTS

Arsenal open their title defence against Coventry

The Premier League champions begin the new season at home to Coventry tonight after ending a 22-year wait for the trophy. Mikel Arteta is now the division’s longest-serving manager, while nine other clubs enter the campaign with new men in charge. (Guardian)

Maracanã will stage Women’s World Cup opener and final

Brazil will open the 2027 Women’s World Cup at the Maracanã on 24 June, with the final returning there on 25 July. The 32-team tournament has 64 matches; Arena Itaquera in São Paulo will host 10, more than any other venue. (Guardian)

LIFESTYLE

Heatwaves force British allotments to change their crops

Repeated heatwaves and drought have left growers doing more watering for smaller harvests, with potatoes, peas and brassicas struggling across England. Hotter conditions have also brought early tomatoes, larger sweet potatoes and successful watermelons near Bristol, encouraging gardeners to rethink future planting. (Guardian)

Fashion retailers cut back plus-size ranges

H&M offered no new women’s dresses in sizes 3XL or 4XL for spring and summer this year, after those sizes made up 2% of new stock in 2025. Mango also reduced sizes 16 to 20 sharply, even as secondhand demand for large and plus-size clothing continued to grow. (Guardian)
