LEAD STORY

Trump widened the tariff fight to Brazil and Brussels

Donald Trump threatened new tariffs on the European Union after Brussels fined Google €890m and targeted several US technology groups. Hours earlier, a forced-labour tariff took effect against more than 80 countries, with Brazilian products hit by an additional 12.5 percentage points and some exports facing a total US tariff load of 37.5%. (Guardian, Meio, MacMagazine)

The measures joined three disputes at once: American anger over European tech regulation, pressure on global supply chains and a direct blow to Brazilian exporters. Apple suppliers also entered the week with a wider US import schedule that could add cost pressure to iPhone production. (Meio, MacMagazine)

NEWS

Wildfires put France and Spain into emergency mode

More than 200,000 people fled or were ordered to stay indoors as major fires swept through Spain and south-west France. Spain declared a national emergency, while French authorities ordered the Cap Ferret peninsula to be evacuated by land and sea after flames burned through more than 19,000 hectares. (Guardian)

The ICC removed Karim Khan

International criminal court member states voted to remove prosecutor Karim Khan after findings linked to allegations of sexual misconduct. Khan denies the allegations, but the vote ended a two-year crisis at the court and marked an unprecedented removal from the prosecutor's office. (Guardian, BBC)

Care workers may escape tougher UK visa rules

Hundreds of thousands of care workers already in Britain could be exempted from tougher right-to-remain rules as Andy Burnham prepares a National Care Service push. The proposal would ease pressure on a sector short of staff before the new government sets out its wider immigration plan. (Guardian)

A border bomb case reached court

Isobella Perrie Sullivan, a 25-year-old law student from County Kildare, was charged with possession of explosives after an intelligence-led operation near the UK-Irish border intercepted what police described as a military-grade bomb. She was remanded in custody after a special sitting of Trim district court. (Guardian, BBC)

Brazil's presidential race stayed tight

Datafolha put Lula at 48% and Flávio Bolsonaro at 43% in a second-round presidential scenario, with little movement from June. Lula's government rating also held steady after the US tariff shock, with 38% calling the administration bad or terrible, 32% good or great and 28% regular. (Folha, Folha)

Brazil's STJ inquiry reached a second minister

Cristiano Zanin authorised a federal police investigation into STJ minister Marco Buzzi in the inquiry into alleged sales of court decisions. The case has now put another senior judge under formal scrutiny in a scandal already weighing on Brazil's higher courts. (Folha)

Flávio Bolsonaro entered convention week exposed

The PL moved toward formalising Flávio Bolsonaro's presidential candidacy without declared backing from other parties or a running mate. Javier Milei's planned arrival in São Paulo gives the campaign a regional-right show of support while the domestic coalition remains unsettled. (Folha, Folha)

Water bosses' pay rose through the bonus ban

Water company chiefs' total pay rose over the past year despite a government bonus ban and continuing anger over sewage, bills and investment. Anglian Water chief executive Mark Thurston received £1.9m, including a £500,000 retention payment. (Guardian)

Cafes were left outside rates relief

Independent cafes and other alcohol-free venues criticised the new business-rates cut for favouring pubs and bars. The dispute leaves high-street support split between hospitality businesses that sell alcohol and community spaces that face the same rent, wage and energy pressures. (Guardian)

Holiday travel put EES back on the agenda

About 2.2 million British holidaymakers were expected to travel abroad between Friday and Monday as Liberal Democrats urged Burnham to ask EU leaders to suspend the entry-exit system. The call turns a summer travel weekend into another test of post-Brexit border capacity. (Guardian)

TECHNOLOGY

Anthropic moved Claude Opus to version 5

Anthropic released Claude Opus 5, pitching the model as close to the capabilities of Claude Fable 5 across many domains while keeping it below Fable's price point. The launch arrived after a run of US government and OpenAI security arguments that kept frontier-model oversight in the foreground. (The Verge, MacMagazine)

Qualcomm warned customers of double-digit price rises

Qualcomm told customers it plans to raise prices by a double-digit percentage on products shipped after 1 September. The company blamed higher supplier costs, a warning that points toward broader hardware inflation across phones, PCs and connected devices. (The Verge)

Meta's glasses problem moved from privacy to product limits

Meta paused a plan to rate-limit a smart-glasses feature after backlash, while the same device line continued to face harassment and moderation concerns around public filming. The glasses are now carrying both the subscription fight and the surveillance fight. (The Verge, The Verge)

Roku raised streaming-device prices

Roku increased prices across its streaming hardware, with the cheapest HD Streaming Stick moving from $29.99 to $39.99 and some devices rising by as much as $50. The change makes living-room hardware another visible point of consumer tech inflation. (The Verge)

Nothing confirmed layoffs

Nothing acknowledged a reorganisation and staff cuts while rejecting claims that it was exiting 12 markets. The phone maker also disputed weak-sales claims around the Phone 4B, leaving the company fighting both a headcount story and a demand story. (The Verge)

CULTURE

Glasgow School of Art's restoration faced a funding cliff

The Mackintosh building at Glasgow School of Art may be left with facade retention or managed decay unless a major patron emerges. The fire-damaged landmark has already been destroyed twice, and projected restoration costs are now threatening the chance of a full return. (Guardian)

Tribute acts became venue economics

Tribute acts are now a serious source of income for struggling UK live venues, with Replika festival drawing 108 acts and a Guinness world record for the format. The boom is also forcing a sharper question over space for original artists on small stages. (Guardian)

Paramount paused the Warner Bros deal

Paramount Skydance agreed to pause its $110bn Warner Bros Discovery acquisition while a challenge from 12 US states plays out. The hold keeps one of Hollywood's largest consolidation plans in court before it can reshape studios, streaming and news assets. (Guardian)

Chuck Russell died at 74

Chuck Russell, the director behind The Mask, A Nightmare on Elm Street 3, The Blob and The Scorpion King, died at his home in San Diego. His films helped move Jim Carrey, Cameron Diaz and Dwayne Johnson into wider Hollywood visibility. (Guardian)

SPORTS

Spain kept the World Cup final row alive

Spain coach Luis de la Fuente criticised Argentina players' conduct after the World Cup final, with FIFA's investigation still unresolved. The post-final case leaves disciplinary pressure hanging over a tournament that had already ended with Spain as champions. (Guardian)

Germany gave Jürgen Klopp the national job

Jürgen Klopp began as Germany head coach by promising to change the team and warning that he would quit if his family were hounded by the media. The appointment gives Germany a new cycle after its latest World Cup disappointment. (Guardian, BBC)

Premier League rebuilds moved through coaching and transfers

Enzo Maresca began shaping the post-Guardiola era at Manchester City, while Liverpool's Andoni Iraola put new staff on set-piece work and Newcastle completed a move for Monaco midfielder Aladji Bamba. The first post-World Cup Premier League reset is already being written through benches as much as squads. (Guardian, Guardian, Guardian)

LIFESTYLE

Disabled passengers put airline access under scrutiny

Wheelchair users described air travel as increasingly humiliating, with accounts of abuse, inaccessible toilets and pressure to accept unsafe or degrading arrangements. The complaints turn summer travel into a test of whether airlines and airports can treat disabled passengers as ordinary travellers. (Guardian)

Secondhand fashion became expensive fashion

Thrifting has moved from subculture to a resale market projected around $350bn, helped by Gen Z sellers and platforms such as Depop. The shift is making used clothing harder to separate from the same pricing pressures and trend cycles that shape new fashion. (Guardian)

Food nutrients put soil back in the conversation

Declines in the nutrient content of crops are pushing more attention toward regenerative agriculture, which focuses on rebuilding soil health, biodiversity and natural ecosystems. The argument connects what happens in fields with what ends up in everyday diets. (Guardian)

Heat advice moved from weather to bedrooms

Another English heat alert and a wave of consumer cooling advice put sleep, fans and ventilation back into daily planning. The practical question for hot homes is now how to keep rooms usable without defaulting to heavy air-conditioning costs. (Guardian, Guardian)
