LEAD STORY

Canada answers new US tariffs dollar for dollar

The United States has imposed 50% tariffs on about $20bn (£14.6bn) of Canadian goods after trade talks collapsed at the last minute. Prime minister Mark Carney announced matching retaliation, covering products from beer and hockey equipment to paper goods and clothing. (Guardian, BBC)

The new US duties apply to about 5% of Canada’s annual exports across the border. Washington and Ottawa traded $880bn in goods and services last year, but no further talks are planned and Canada is preparing additional support for affected workers and businesses. (Guardian)

NEWS

Kyiv runs out of Patriot interceptors as strikes intensify

Ukraine has exhausted the US-made Patriot interceptors needed to stop Russian ballistic missiles over Kyiv. Sixteen people were killed in the latest wave of attacks, while UN figures recorded 437 civilian deaths and 2,610 injuries across Ukraine in July, the highest monthly death toll since May 2022. (Guardian)

Seven die in wrong-way crash on the A66

Police constables Matthew Blades and Tom Clough were among seven people killed when their marked vehicle collided with a Volkswagen Passat travelling the wrong way on the A66 near Middlesbrough. The police watchdog has opened an inquiry into the early-morning crash. (Guardian, BBC)

Girl killed in sword attack at Swedish school

A 17-year-old girl has died after an 18-year-old man attacked students with a sword during classes in Fagersta, central Sweden. Three other people were wounded, including boys aged 12 and 17 who suffered minor injuries. (Guardian)

Former diplomats call for trade and arms action over Palestine

More than 100 former British and French diplomats have urged their governments to suspend arms transfers and trade agreements with Israel, guarantee unrestricted aid access to Gaza and ban commerce with settlements. Their joint letter accuses Israel of ethnic cleansing and warns that a Palestinian state is being erased. (Guardian)

Libido-suppressing drugs could reach thousands of prisoners

Voluntary medication to reduce the sex drive of male offenders is expanding to 20 prisons in England and Wales. About 3,750 current prisoners could be considered for treatment, with wider use possible from December 2028. (Guardian)

Brazilian presidential race divides the largest states

Flávio Bolsonaro leads Lula by 47% to 42% in a possible second round in São Paulo, Brazil’s largest electoral college, while Minas Gerais is tied. Lula’s positive approval stands at 27% in São Paulo, Rio de Janeiro and the Federal District, rising to 46% in Pernambuco. (Folha, Folha)

Yanomami malnutrition deaths fall by three-quarters

Deaths from malnutrition in Brazil’s Yanomami Indigenous territory fell 75.7% in the first half of 2026 compared with the same period in 2023, when the government declared a public-health emergency in the region. (Folha)

Discarded batteries cost recycling centres £1bn a year

Lithium batteries thrown into household waste are causing more than 10 fires a week at UK recycling centres. The fires, often linked to batteries in disposable vapes and small electronics, now cost the waste industry about £1bn a year and repeatedly put workers and firefighters at risk. (Guardian)

Premium bond prize fund rate rises to 4.35%

NS&I will raise the annual premium bond prize fund rate from 3.8% to 4.35% for September’s draw. The odds for each £1 bond improve from 22,000-to-one to 21,000-to-one, with about 308,000 extra prizes and a total pot of £497m. (Guardian)

TECHNOLOGY

Meta faces potential $200bn bill in children’s safety trial

California and 28 other US states have accused Meta of designing addictive products and collecting data from under-13s without parental permission. Damages could reach $200bn, and the states also want permanent changes to Facebook and Instagram. Meta denies the allegations. (Guardian)

Amazon lifts device prices by as much as 60%

Amazon has raised prices across its Echo, Kindle, Fire TV and Eero ranges, citing higher memory and storage-component costs. The Echo Dot has climbed from $49.99 to $79.99, leaving the cheapest devices with some of the steepest increases. (The Verge)

CULTURE

Abbey Road’s ordinary crossing still carries the Beatles’ magic

Iain Macmillan photographed the Beatles walking across Abbey Road on 8 August 1969, turning a north London street into one of music’s most recognisable images. The cover’s plain setting, precise lines and individual body language have sustained decades of copies, parodies and pilgrimages. (Guardian)

Éric Rohmer’s lost first novel returns in English

Élisabeth, written in 1946 before Maurice Schérer adopted the name Éric Rohmer, has been republished in an English translation by Aaron Kerner. The story of young lovers in prewar France quickly disappeared after its original release and now provides an early map of the film-maker’s recurring concerns. (Guardian)

LIFESTYLE

Plug-in solar panels reach Britain this week

Households in Great Britain will be able to install plug-in solar systems from 27 August after wiring rules were changed to permit devices producing up to 800 watts. Basic kits cost about £400 and could save between £40 and £110 a year, depending on sunlight and how much power is used during the day. (Guardian)
