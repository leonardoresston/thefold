News

Burnham faces climate revolt over Jackdaw gasfield approval as Covid inquiry condemns care home failures

Energy Secretary Miatta Fahnbulleh and Chancellor John Healey travel to Aberdeen on Wednesday to grant final development consent for the Jackdaw gasfield in the North Sea, ending more than a year of legal and environmental deliberations. Operated jointly by Shell and Norwegian energy group Equinor, Jackdaw is one of the largest undeveloped fields in the declining basin and will represent 6% of UK North Sea gas output once production begins. Prime Minister Andy Burnham’s government has pitched the decision around energy security and jobs, even though Labour’s 2024 manifesto pledged no new oil and gas licences because further drilling would fail to lower household bills. Because Jackdaw was first approved under the Conservatives in 2022 before court challenges over downstream emissions stalled work, ministers argue it honours an existing licence while leaving the larger Rosebank oilfield undecided. Climate groups and Labour MPs condemned the approval, with Uplift director Tessa Khan noting Jackdaw will supply barely 2% of UK gas demand over 10 years while operating largely remotely. (Guardian, BBC)

The UK Covid-19 public inquiry meanwhile published its sixth report, delivering a damning 287-page verdict on adult social care during the pandemic. Inquiry chair Baroness Heather Hallett concluded that government failures allowed the virus to spread “like wildfire” across care homes, where more than 43,000 residents died between March 2020 and July 2022. Hallett dismissed former health secretary Matt Hancock’s claim to have thrown a “protective ring” around care homes as unsupported by evidence, finding the sector was treated as an afterthought while ministers prioritised freeing NHS hospital beds. Around 30,000 hospital patients were discharged into English care homes between mid-March and mid-April 2020 without routine testing or isolation advice. Care staff described improvising personal protective equipment from clinical bin bags, plastic festival ponchos and painters’ overalls, while blanket bans on family visits left tens of thousands of frail and dementia-stricken residents in prolonged isolation. (Guardian, BBC, BBC)

In Westminster, undercover footage recorded in July by investigative group Verbatim heaped pressure on Reform UK over £72m donated last week by crypto billionaires Christopher Harborne and Ben Delo. In the video, Cambridge professor James Orr—Nigel Farage’s head of policy until his suspension last month—warned that the government’s proposed £100,000 annual foreign-donor cap, retrospective to March 2026 and requiring a year of UK residency, could make donations from Thailand-based Harborne or Hong Kong-based Delo illegal, three months before each gave £36m. Labour suspended a second MP in a week after Perran Moon (Camborne and Redruth) referred himself over a £50,000 pandemic Bounce Back Loan taken by his former firm Autoswot before it ceased trading in late 2022. Elsewhere, former Ofsted chief Sir David Bell’s review found three in five Jewish students in England experienced prejudice and recommended 27 measures treating antisemitism as a distinct category of harm, while counter-terrorism police re-arrested a 24-year-old British man and a 25-year-old dual UK-Iranian national under the National Security Act over September’s incursion near RAF Fairford. (Guardian, BBC, Guardian, BBC, Guardian)

Italy deploys 500 soldiers to Milan after cost-of-living riots as RAF Typhoons defend Saudi airspace

Italian Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni ordered 500 soldiers into Milan on Tuesday and unveiled emergency security legislation after weekend protests over surging energy, fuel and food prices turned violent across northern Italy. Police in Milan used teargas and water cannon around Piazza del Duomo to disperse crowds that smashed bank windows and looted shops, detaining 271 youths and arresting 32 suspects, including 23 minors, alongside clashes in Turin, Bologna, Brescia and Bergamo. Meloni’s decree makes participating in a violent protest a specific offence and imposes civil fines on parents for looting by underage children, as luxury brands Chanel, Tiffany and Dior cleared display windows in Milan’s Galleria Vittorio Emanuele II ahead of Saturday marches. In France, where nearly 2,400 protesters were arrested last weekend, Interior Minister Laurent Nuñez confirmed army troops will guard static buildings this Saturday to free police for riot control, while Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu met five union confederations and extended 11pm–6am curfews in central Paris and regional cities. (Guardian)

In the Middle East, Prime Minister Andy Burnham authorised his most significant military deployment since taking office, sending Royal Air Force Typhoon fighter jets to fly defensive patrols over Saudi Arabia against drones and missiles launched by Yemen’s Iran-aligned Houthis. Defence Secretary Wes Streeting stressed that British aircraft will protect Saudi cities, airports and oil facilities without conducting offensive strikes inside Yemen. The deployment follows a Houthi missile attack on Riyadh’s King Khalid International Airport that killed 13 people, bringing Saudi fatalities to 19 since cross-border strikes resumed in September. In Yemen’s Shabwa province, officials confirmed the death toll from Monday’s Houthi ballistic missile strike on the al-Alam training camp—used by UAE-backed Southern Giants Brigades forces allied with Yemen’s Presidential Leadership Council—had risen from 53 to 73 soldiers. (Guardian)

In Oslo, Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy met US envoy Steve Witkoff, Norwegian Prime Minister Jonas Gahr Støre and Finnish President Alexander Stubb, with Jared Kushner joining by video. Zelenskyy confirmed Washington has resumed sharing satellite early-warning intelligence on incoming Russian ballistic missiles after a five-day pause that curtailed air-raid warnings, while pressing US Secretary of State Marco Rubio to lift Pentagon-backed curbs on SpaceX’s Starshield network ahead of Witkoff’s Wednesday talks with Vladimir Putin in Moscow. In the US, former New York mayor Michael Bloomberg injected $80m into Democratic midterm campaigns—including $24m via Everytown for Gun Safety and $16m to the House Majority PAC—against $400m committed by Donald Trump and $366m spent by Elon Musk for Republicans, while Trump and Vice President JD Vance handed conservative YouTuber Nick Shirley $10,000 to launch a $25m federal fraud tip bounty. (Guardian, BBC, Guardian, Verge)

Lula brings forward Bolsa Família payments and files banking waiver as Brazil’s runoff enters its final days

Five days before Brazil’s 25 October presidential runoff, President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva’s government compressed October’s Bolsa Família disbursement schedule so all beneficiaries receive their payments—including September’s 15.04% increase—by Wednesday rather than 30 October. Senator Flávio Bolsonaro scrambled to delete 2014 social media posts in which, as a Rio state deputy, he called the number of Bolsa Família recipients a badge of national shame, while his economic coordinator unsettled centrist allies by floating a transition rule to move formal workers off the benefit. Seeking to neutralise opposition corruption claims before Friday’s final TV Globo debate, Lula filed a signed waiver with the Superior Electoral Court (TSE) lifting banking, tax and phone secrecy for himself, First Lady Janja da Silva and his son Fábio Luís (“Lulinha”), conditional on Bolsonaro releasing records for himself, his family and five companies—including a British Virgin Islands offshore firm—linked to lawyer Willer Tomaz. (Meio, Folha, Folha, Folha)

In Congress, Senate government leader Randolfe Rodrigues filed an ethics complaint accusing Bolsonaro of requesting R$134m from former Banco Master owner Daniel Vorcaro, while PT Chamber leader Pedro Uczai proposed a joint parliamentary inquiry (CPI) alongside Supreme Court probes into Willer Tomaz, former Rio governor Cláudio Castro and deputy Alexandre Ramagem; prosecutors also opened an inquiry into whether Federal Police chief Andrei Rodrigues leaked confidential INSS case data regarding Lula’s brother, Frei Chico. On the campaign trail, Bolsonaro held a closed session in Alphaville with 300 digital influencers coached by Pablo Marçal, won Paraná governor Ratinho Junior’s endorsement, and leaned on Assemblies of God pastors and São Paulo governor Tarcísio de Freitas—who has attended 12 Bolsonaro rallies against five official duties since 5 October—while Argentine President Javier Milei attacked Lula online. Lula rallied over 600 organised pop fan clubs in São Paulo, watched Bolsonaro’s Sunday debate stunt of writing “corrupto” on his palm become a nationwide meme, and secured Chamber Speaker Hugo Motta’s conditional backing for a weekend free-transit constitutional amendment provided parliamentary budget earmarks remain intact, as Meta’s Oversight Board criticised recurring election moderation failures. (Folha, Folha, Folha, Folha, Folha, Guardian, Guardian)

Google Cloud power fault disrupts UK banks and courts as Flutter cuts 1,400 jobs and Ryanair warns on fares

A power failure inside Google Cloud’s europe-west2-b London data centre zone on Tuesday morning caused cascading outages across British financial and public services. HM Courts and Tribunals Service lost its Cloud Video Platform across England and Wales, delaying remote bail and sentencing hearings for remanded prisoners, while the NHS app, Royal Mail tracking, and mobile apps at Lloyds, Halifax and HSBC suffered hours of disruption. Although Google restored most cloud services between 1pm and 3pm, fintech firm Revolut remained severely impaired into the evening: only six of its 27 platform functions worked normally at 5pm amid failed card payments, ATM withdrawals and transfers, prompting Financial Conduct Authority and Bank of England inquiries into financial reliance on cloud hyperscalers. (Guardian, Guardian)

Employers across banking, gambling and aviation also announced restructurings ahead of Chancellor John Healey’s 28 October budget. NatWest ordered 13,000 remote-first employees—previously required in an office twice a month—to attend at least two days a week from February 2027 across all 40,000 UK staff at NatWest, RBS, Ulster Bank and Coutts. Paddy Power and Sky Bet owner Flutter Entertainment announced plans to cut up to 1,400 roles—over 10% of its 8,960 UK and Ireland staff in Leeds, Dublin and Sunderland, plus hubs in Porto and Cluj-Napoca—blaming last year’s budget rises in remote gaming duty (21% to 40%) and online betting duty (15% to 25%) as Healey weighs higher slot-machine taxes. Meanwhile, Ryanair chief executive Michael O’Leary warned European air fares could climb up to 20% in 2027 as Middle East conflict doubles spot jet fuel above the $85-a-barrel level at which Ryanair hedged 15% of its 2027 fuel, while cutting 20 Brussels routes over Belgium’s boarding tax rise from €2 to €7. (Guardian, Guardian, Guardian)

Technology

Tesla and SpaceX form committees to explore a merger as SpaceX revenue surges to $14.6bn

Elon Musk disclosed during SpaceX’s third-quarter earnings call—its second report since June’s initial public offering—that Tesla and SpaceX have formed special committees of independent directors at shareholders’ request to evaluate a corporate merger, though Tesla’s bylaws require 50% of all outstanding shares to approve a related-party deal. SpaceX’s third-quarter revenue jumped 161% year-on-year to $14.6bn, led by its Super Intelligence (SI) division—formerly xAI—whose revenue climbed 830% to $7.6bn ($431m operating income) following its $60bn acquisition of AI coding platform Cursor, Grok enterprise sales and cloud contracts. Starlink generated $5.2bn in revenue and $2.4bn in operating profit across 13.6 million subscribers, while space launches brought in $1.8bn at a $7m operating loss amid heavy spending on orbital data centres and reusable rockets. (Verge, Verge)

Musk added that SpaceX will attempt next month to catch both the upper-stage Starship spacecraft and its Super Heavy booster using launch-tower mechanical arms in Texas for the first complete recovery of an orbital rocket after April’s Indian Ocean splashdown. Elsewhere in consumer technology, OpenAI introduced PIN-protected Locked Chats in ChatGPT across web, iOS and Android to keep sensitive chats out of search and memory; Google brought Gemini’s Personal Intelligence to Android Auto alongside Waze conversational reporting; Oura added Cardio Effort and automatic VO2 Max tracking for Oura Ring 3 and 4 users; and Snap hired actor Robert Downey Jr to promote its consumer Specs augmented-reality glasses. (Verge, Verge, Verge, Verge)

UK regulators challenge Apple over digital wallets as iPhone Duo orders are capped and Perplexity goes offline

Britain’s Competition and Markets Authority (CMA) opened a consultation until 17 November on conduct rules forcing Apple to lower fees and remove technical barriers on third-party access to the iPhone’s NFC contactless chip. CMA chief executive Sarah Cardell noted contactless payments represent 39% of UK transactions, up from 3% a decade ago, with 60% of British adults using mobile wallets. Meanwhile, Apple capped online purchases of its foldable iPhone Duo at two per customer in the UK, US, Japan, Spain and Brazil to curb scalping while moving some US and Japanese deliveries forward from December and January to 23 October and mid-November. Apple also committed up to $100m over three years to Pennsylvania titanium producer Perryman Company and expanded its partnership with Delaware’s Qnity Electronics for the iPhone 18 Pro’s variable-aperture camera among nearly 100 US-made components, while supply-chain filings showed Samsung plans six foldable models in 2027. (MacMagazine, 9to5Mac, 9to5Mac, MacMagazine, 9to5Mac, 9to5Mac)

On the desktop, Perplexity added a local-first mode to Perplexity Computer on Apple Silicon Macs (36GB unified memory recommended), allowing users to run open-weights Qwen 3.6 or PPLX Pro models 100% offline with local terminal, file and browser control. Apple seeded the visionOS 26.2 developer beta featuring WebP and AVIF support, multi-user enterprise tools and spatial audio previews alongside second release candidates for iOS 26.1 and macOS Tahoe 26.1, while Brazil’s Anatel opened a 60-day consultation on mandatory emergency cell broadcasts and certified a wave of tri-band Wi-Fi 7 routers. (MacStories, MacMagazine, 9to5Mac, MacMagazine)

Culture

Broadcaster axes linear TV channels in £500m cost drive as Liverpool plans a vast Beatles museum and Netflix viewing dips

Director general Matt Brittin and news director Rachel Jupp unveiled sweeping broadcast cuts on Tuesday as the corporation seeks to close a £500m funding gap, of which £200m has been found so far. Under the plan, linear television channels BBC Three and BBC Scotland will move online-only to iPlayer in spring 2027, Radio 4 Extra will close with its archive shifting to Sounds, and 100 news posts will be cut to save £5.5m, drawing union and political criticism in Scotland. In Liverpool, Apple Corps announced with Sir Paul McCartney, Ringo Starr and Prime Minister Andy Burnham that it will take ownership of The Beatles Story museum and relocate it in 2028 to the vacant 100,000 sq ft St John’s Market Hall—quadrupling its footprint—while turning the 450ft St Johns Beacon tower into a Yellow Submarine attraction and funding grassroots music via the new Apple Corps Liverpool Trust. (Guardian, BBC, Guardian, BBC)

In streaming and film, Netflix reported third-quarter revenue up 12% year-on-year to $12.9bn, outlined plans to expand its ad tier to 15 more countries in 2027, and named former Disney executive Peter Rice chief content officer from 26 October after cutting 1,000 jobs, though average monthly viewing per subscriber fell from 66 hours in early 2023 to 49 hours. Comedy creators Mathew Baynton, Simon Farnaby, Martha Howe-Douglas, Jim Howick, Laurence Rickard and Ben Willbond confirmed they are reuniting with Charlotte Ritchie and Kiell Smith-Bynoe for a feature film, Ghosts: The Possession of Button House. Meanwhile, Hollywood unions debated two indie films using generative AI: the western As Deep as the Grave, featuring an estate-approved digital recreation of a young Val Kilmer, and Misaligned, which casts synthetic AI actress Tilly Norwood. (Verge, Guardian, Guardian)

Ghent unveils carbon-absorbing brick museum extension as AI conquers the Times cryptic crossword and Rockstar films in Miami

London architects Carmody Groarke completed a €20m (£17m) mass-timber extension for Design Museum Gent in Belgium clad in 82,000 low-carbon white bricks made by compressing crushed local concrete, brick rubble and hydrated lime without firing, allowing the facade to absorb carbon dioxide as it cures. In publishing, Poet Laureate Simon Armitage released a four-beat alliterative verse translation of the 4,000-year-old Mesopotamian epic Gilgamesh with Faber. British puzzle culture also reached a milestone after Darmok, an AI solver created by University of Texas computer scientist Dr Sid Chatterjee, beat human champion Liam Hughes by three minutes at the Times cryptic crossword championship, solving all four finals puzzles cleanly. (Guardian, Guardian, Guardian)

Ahead of next month’s launch of Grand Theft Auto VI, Rockstar Games closed downtown Miami streets with helicopters and camera cars to film custom Grotti and Emerus supercars bearing Vice City plates, while US federal label filings revealed a partnership with Florida’s Tripping Animals Brewing to sell real-life cans of in-game beers Pißwasser, Patriot Beer, Logger and No Mames Vice City. Elsewhere in gaming, Electronic Arts confirmed a new Fight Night boxing game is in development, and Aspyr Media set a 10 November launch for Shrek 2 Remastered with a trailer parodying GTA VI. (IGN, IGN, IGN, IGN)

Sports

Liverpool rally from two goals down to beat Villarreal 3–2 as Foden scores a hat-trick for Manchester City

Andoni Iraola’s Liverpool maintained their 100% Champions League record on Tuesday night by rallying from two goals down to beat Villarreal 3–2 at Anfield. Early errors by Virgil van Dijk and a high line allowed Georges Mikautadze and Sergi Cardona to put Iñigo Pérez’s visitors 2–0 up inside half an hour, before Alexis Mac Allister headed in a Kostas Tsimikas corner and 18-year-old winger Rio Ngumoha crossed for on-loan defender Ronald Araújo to level before half-time. Ngumoha then set up Mac Allister’s second goal after the break and substitute Florian Wirtz scored his first goal of the season before Tani Oluwaseyi’s stoppage-time volley. (Guardian, BBC)

At the Etihad Stadium, Phil Foden scored a hat-trick across right-wing and false-nine roles—following Saturday’s goal that ended a drought dating back to mid-December—as Enzo Maresca’s Manchester City rested Erling Haaland and beat AEK Athens 5–1 with additional goals from Elliot Anderson and Rayan Cherki. Elsewhere in Europe, holders Paris Saint-Germain defeated Barcelona 4–2 at a curfew-bound Parc des Princes through Désiré Doué and Vitinha, while Scotland midfielder Scott McTominay came off the bench on his return from September heart surgery to score twice in Massimiliano Allegri’s Napoli 4–3 win over Bodø/Glimt. (Guardian, BBC, Guardian, Guardian)

Arsenal travel to Bayern Munich for Champions League test as regulators take over Manchester City accounting inquiry

Arsenal face Bayern Munich at the Allianz Arena on Wednesday evening having won 10 of their 11 matches across all competitions this season, with goalkeeper David Raya and manager Mikel Arteta brushing off rival complaints about their physical set-piece routines and game management after Sunday’s 1–0 victory at Nottingham Forest. Speaking in Munich, Raya said the squad remained driven by the memory of last May’s Champions League final defeat on penalties against Paris Saint-Germain and relished measuring their progress against the German champions, whose head coach Vincent Kompany admitted a viral infection affecting his legs could keep him off the touchline while French winger Michael Olise serves a suspension. Manchester United head coach Michael Carrick, whose side sit 13th in the Premier League with a single league win ahead of Wednesday’s Champions League trip to Italian debutants Como, insisted he retains full board backing and dismissed predecessor Erik ten Hag’s serialised book claims that Old Trafford suffered from a toxic dressing-room culture after Cristiano Ronaldo refused to come on as a substitute against Tottenham in October 2022. (Guardian, Guardian, BBC)

Off the pitch, Britain’s corporate accounting watchdog confirmed it has stepped directly into the financial misconduct case surrounding Manchester City. Appearances before the Commons business and trade select committee revealed that on 30 September the Financial Reporting Council (FRC) assumed control of regulatory investigations from the Institute of Chartered Accountants in England and Wales into both the club’s historical financial statements and its long-standing auditing firm, BDO. The intervention follows an independent commission ruling earlier this year that Manchester City artificially inflated their declared revenue and financial position by more than £900m across nine seasons between 2009–10 and 2017–18 by disguising equity funding as commercial sponsorship—a verdict and sporting sanction that the Etihad hierarchy strongly deny and are currently appealing before an independent panel. (BBC)
