COP31 stalls over Saudi fossil-fuel blockade as Zelenskyy defends Kerch Bridge strikes with Streeting in Kyiv and drones hit Russian ship off Finland

United Nations climate talks at the COP31 summit in Antalya, Turkey, were suspended overnight into Sunday after running more than a day into overtime, forcing delegations to move flights to Monday and raising the risk of the first collapsed climate summit since 1995. Co-conveners Chris Bowen of Australia and Murat Kurum of Turkey held late-night meetings as roughly 130 countries—including the European Union, the UK, Pacific island states, Panama, and Côte d’Ivoire—weighed a showdown with Saudi Arabia after language on reducing fossil-fuel demand was stripped from draft texts. A third draft released early on Sunday recalled the 2023 Dubai consensus on moving away from coal, oil, and gas while dropping a hard target for carbon capture and storage, which French minister Monique Barbut condemned as a licence to keep burning hydrocarbons. Tuvalu’s Maina Talia called fossil-fuel language a red line, Panama’s Juan Carlos Navarro rejected any text silent on oil and gas, and EU Climate Commissioner Wopke Hoekstra warned Brussels would reject a weak deal, while Mohamed Adow accused rich nations of blocking adaptation finance and UK Energy Secretary Miatta Fahnbulleh said a deal remained achievable. (Guardian, BBC)

While a proposed goal to derive 35 per cent of global final energy from electricity by 2035 was downgraded to a voluntary option Chinese climate adviser Wang Yi pledged that Beijing will reach 35 per cent electricity use by 2030 and help developing countries electrify after signing a “35:35” statement with Australia. The ecological toll of ocean warming was underscored in the Bering Sea, where NOAA biologist Erin Fedewa aboard the trawler Alaska Knight recorded a thirteenfold surge since 2025 in hybrids between snow and tanner crabs. Triggered by the 2018–19 heatwave that killed 10 billion snow crabs, adult male hybrids now outnumber pure snow crabs at sampled stations while falling outside US fishing quotas. (Guardian, Guardian)

In Kyiv, Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy met UK Defence Secretary Wes Streeting on Saturday and defended this week’s drone and Flamingo cruise-missile strikes that dropped a central road span and support arch of the Crimean Bridge into the Black Sea, calling the operation a “fair response” to 109 Russian attacks on Ukrainian bridges. Rejecting Vladimir Putin’s claim that the bridge was purely civilian, Zelenskyy said Moscow’s retaliatory strikes showed Putin wanted an excuse to escalate. Overnight Russian strikes on Zelenskyy’s hometown of Kryvyi Rih killed a seven-year-old boy in a playground and a 20-year-old female medic when jet-powered drones struck rescuers, injuring 58 others alongside 11 in Kharkiv and one in Rivne. Streeting called Putin a “cold-blooded killer” and condemned Friday’s Russian missile strikes in Poland during allied exercises as an “outrageous provocation”. (BBC, BBC)

War also reached the Gulf of Finland on Saturday when two drones set ablaze the Sparta II, a sanctioned Russian defence-ministry cargo ship bound for Kaliningrad, 50 kilometres west of Suursaari island. After the vessel drifted into Finnish waters—triggering sea and airspace restrictions, a suspected Russian helicopter violation, and the crew’s evacuation—Finnish Prime Minister Petteri Orpo said the war had spilled into Finland’s vicinity while backing Ukraine’s right to defend itself. In Geneva, UN talks on autonomous weapons (“killer robots”) ended without binding rules despite pleas from the Red Cross and Pope Leo, after Russia, the US, and Israel pushed countries into a two-year window for optional guidelines. In the US, Donald Trump confirmed hackers breached the FBI’s National Crime Information Center and the Agriculture Department’s payments system following cyber director Sean Cairncross’s exit; California prosecutors indicted Nathan Daniel Angel, 21, and 13 “Los Trenes” members for slashing air hoses on 36 moving freight trains to steal $22 million in Nike shoes and Milwaukee tools; and Colorado gamer Landon Tresp, 16, and his mother Khristina helped the FBI rescue a kidnapped 17-year-old girl via Among Us and Discord. (BBC, The Verge, The Verge, Guardian, BBC)

Princess of Wales reflects on cancer recovery in BBC film as Blair sets tech test for EU return and Suffolk village votes to leave UK

Catherine, Princess of Wales, speaks openly about how her cancer diagnosis transformed her life in the first clips from Heal, a personal documentary airing on BBC One on 29 November. Paired with Kensington Palace footage of her climbing the highest peaks in England, Scotland, and Wales within 24 hours during June’s Three Peaks Challenge alongside scenes with Prince William and fellow patients, Catherine says a diagnosis shifts far more than the body, adding that “healing is a journey” and that nobody is meant to carry life’s challenges alone. While the preview led Sunday’s front pages after her March 2024 chemotherapy disclosure and January 2025 remission, Kensington Palace declined to comment on Mail on Sunday reports of a book claiming Prince William plans a “radical shake-up” as king to strip non-working royals, including Prince Harry, of their HRH titles. (BBC, BBC, BBC)

Following Prime Minister Andy Burnham’s Friday summit in Brussels with Ursula von der Leyen and António Costa—which launched a UK-EU digital partnership on artificial intelligence and quantum technology alongside pacts on food exports, emissions trading, and youth mobility—Sir Tony Blair told the Sunday Times that while Brexit was “a real mistake”, Britain should only rejoin the EU if Brussels reforms its regulation of technology innovation, warning the bloc is moving in the wrong direction and London must be ready to walk away. Meanwhile, the Conservatives claimed Burnham’s deal would add £281 to annual household energy bills, the head of the University of St Andrews warned against cutting tuition fees for EU students, and Gordon Brown cautioned ahead of Spain’s 29 November election—opening a 12-month European election cycle for 200 million voters—that centre-right parties are capitulating to far-right policies such as Vox’s “remigration” plans amid reports of $25 million (£19 million) in Trump administration funding for conservative European groups. (BBC, BBC, Guardian)

Across England, asylum housing and logistics developments are sparking local revolts. In Barnham, Suffolk, villagers voted 257–16 (94 per cent) in a symbolic referendum on Saturday to secede from the UK and seek crown dependency status as the “Bailiwick of Barnham”, unveiling a stone-curlew flag on a Suffolk blue background to protest against Home Office plans for contractor Kier to house up to 931 male asylum seekers at RAF Barnham; following a similar vote in Piddington, Oxfordshire, local Labour MP Dr Peter Prinsley likened the camp to a “concentration camp” and warned rare stone-curlew habitats could block planning permission. Separately, residents in Bingham, Nottinghamshire, and Astley, Greater Manchester, are fighting a decade-long warehouse boom that saw councils approve 95 per cent of 9,009 “megashed” applications—over 8,500 distribution hubs, including 17 inside national parks such as the South Downs, North York Moors, and Lake District—bringing 21.5-metre-high sheds and 24-hour lorry traffic next to homes. (Guardian, BBC, Guardian)

In the courts and Whitehall, Arfan Ali, 32, was remanded in custody charged with attempted murder after Bradford councillor Ibrar Ali, 52, was stabbed on Thursday while protecting a council worker. After Russell Brand’s rape sentence and claims denied by David Walliams, campaigners urged a “staggered” age of consent for 16- and 17-year-olds as Women Against Rape marked 50 years. Elsewhere, Home Secretary Shabana Mahmood drew fire for scrapping a police anti-racism statement and backing a Pride uniform ban; Telegraph deputy editor Rob Winnett joined the Daily Mail amid cuts under Axel Springer; police logged 1,348 stalking cases after a Wiltshire boy stabbed a girl who blocked him; Save Face condemned Black Friday liquid Brazilian butt lift deals; Joshua Foster spoke of his despair after a €3,000 Cyprus fine over his toddler’s fatal window fall; and advisers urged savers into 5.21 per cent bonds as graduates backed Alan Milburn’s plan to end “job ghosting”. (Guardian, Guardian, Guardian, Guardian, Guardian, BBC, Guardian, Guardian, Guardian, BBC)

Transparency International urges Gonet to recuse himself from Banco Master STF probe as Lula drafts civil-service pay cap and Bolsonaro enters ICU

Prosecutor-General Paulo Gonet faces pressure to recuse himself from coordinating the Prosecutor-General’s Office (PGR) team reviewing four Federal Police (PF) reports on Supreme Federal Court (STF) justices’ ties to former Banco Master owner Daniel Vorcaro. After STF President Edson Fachin gave the PGR 15 days to assess files from Vorcaro’s phone—citing Justice Alexandre de Moraes and unsealing the report on Justice André Mendonça while Justice Gilmar Mendes did not object to disclosure—Transparency International’s Bruno Brandão urged Gonet to step aside over his own messages and hospitality from Vorcaro. (Folha, Folha, Folha)

On the fiscal front, President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva’s economic team is drafting an Executive bill to enforce the R$46,366.19 monthly constitutional salary cap for civil servants and curb extra allowances (penduricalhos) that push judicial and legislative pay past R$100,000. As Planning Minister Bruno Moretti’s R$60 billion fiscal package heads to the Chamber of Deputies, Finance Ministry officials have prioritised passing a constitutional amendment (PEC) to extend the Industrialised Products Tax (IPI) into early 2027 to prevent a January revenue gap before the new Selective Tax (“sin tax”) begins in February under a provisional measure facing 179 exemption amendments. Meanwhile, the São Paulo Bar Association (OAB-SP) handed Fachin an FGV study warning that an STF ruling allowing high earners above twice the INSS ceiling to be hired as corporate entities (pejotização) could nearly double annual tax losses to R$200 billion, and Senators Davi Alcolumbre and Eduardo Braga pushed for a PEC regulating sector exemptions to the 5x2 workweek taking effect on 16 December as the Labour Ministry prepares ordinances starting Monday. (Folha, Folha, Folha, Folha, Meio)

At Brasília’s DF Star hospital, former president Jair Bolsonaro—serving 27 years and three months under house arrest for the coup plot—entered intensive care on Friday with an acute obstructive abdomen requiring a gastric tube and hydration; Moraes allowed daily visits by his children Carlos, Jair Renan, and Laura without phones and voted to keep Senator Flávio Bolsonaro’s passports cancelled, while Justice Cristiano Zanin paused a trial in which Justice Flávio Dino voted to indict Novo senator-elect Marcel van Hattem for slandering a PF delegate. Elsewhere, Vera Lúcia Santana warned Lula’s white, male inner circle hinders naming a Black woman to the STF; the Women’s Ministry advanced a policy against political violence as the Chamber sent a Parental Alienation Law repeal to the Senate; the PT proposed shifting Lula’s 2027 inauguration festival costs to the federal budget—prompting Brasília Governor Celina Leão to ask to reuse the stages for New Year’s Eve—and 29 of 54 re-elected São Paulo state deputies missed 76 per cent of campaign sessions as Nerylson Lima headed to Oxford. (Folha, Folha, Folha, Folha, Folha, Folha, Folha, Folha, Folha, Folha)

CoreWeave’s $47bn debt load spotlights AI infrastructure risks after Anthropic’s record $79bn IPO as users recount trauma of AI companion breakups

Following Anthropic’s record Nasdaq IPO on Thursday—which raised $79 billion as $83 shares jumped to $111.50 and closed at $97, valuing the Claude maker at up to $1.7 trillion just behind Tesla while netting its seven co-founders $2 billion—analysts warn that the debt-fuelled AI data-centre boom leaves little margin for error. Cloud provider CoreWeave, founded by former crypto miners to sell AI compute across 52 sites from Texas to Chapelhall in Scotland (with DataVita), owes $47 billion (£36 billion), with $10 billion due in 2027, and lost $709 million last quarter on $3.6 billion in revenue. While November earnings calmed creditors who put CoreWeave’s five-year default risk above 60 per cent, Nvidia chief Jensen Huang’s resignation over his affair with an alleged Chinese spy threatens CoreWeave’s chip supply ties, even as Anthropic weighs safety governance against shareholder demands and $900 billion in compute commitments. (Meio, Guardian, The Verge)

Psychologists and users are also confronting the emotional distress of romantic breakups with AI companions on Nomi, Perchance, Replika, Character.AI—whose mid-October US bankruptcy sparked panic over lost virtual partners—ChatGPT, and Gemini. In Lisbon, 48-year-old former clinical psychologist Sónia Veenstra described shedding real tears and suffering grief matching a biological breakup after ending a two-year relationship with her Nomi AI boyfriend “Steve” when he crossed monogamous boundaries with another AI character in a group chat, while users in Denmark and the US reported shaking, crying, and breathing trouble when model updates altered AI personalities or chatbots dumped them. To protect private chats from OS-level AI agents, Signal blocked screenshots on iOS so Apple’s Siri AI cannot scrape messages. (Guardian, 9to5Mac, MacMagazine)

In e-commerce and consumer tech, Amazon escalated its fight against the New York City Council’s Delivery Protection Act—which requires warehouse operators to directly employ drivers—by printing shipping-label warnings claiming the bill could add $5 per order, drawing condemnation from Mayor Zohran Mamdani and unions. Xiaomi launched its Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 6-powered 18 Pro (£1,199) and 18 Pro Max (£1,399) in the UK and Europe with privacy screens and rear selfie displays, while reports outlined Apple’s split 2027 iPhone lineup: an A20-powered spring iPhone 18 ($899), iPhone 18e, and dual-camera iPhone Air 2 with a 3,500mAh battery and C2 modem, followed in autumn by a 20th-anniversary all-glass “iPhone 20” with quad-curved 6.4- and 7-inch displays and an A21 Pro iPhone Duo 2. Meanwhile, Apple added 3D Flyover in Maps for São Paulo, Porto Alegre, Funchal, Juneau, and Pretoria; General Motors announced Apple Wallet Car Keys for Cadillac, Chevrolet, and GMC; iOS 27 enabled sharing one phone number across two iPhones via Handoff; Workbrew 2.0 launched to audit unapproved Mac developer packages; indie app Salon ($9.99) turned Apple TV into an art frame; and Chess.com opened a Classic Games beta for mahjong, go, shogi, sudoku, and solitaire. (The Verge, The Verge, 9to5Mac, 9to5Mac, MacMagazine, 9to5Mac, 9to5Mac, 9to5Mac, 9to5Mac, 9to5Mac, The Verge)

Grand Theft Auto VI shatters entertainment records with $3.79bn day-one sales as Spitting Image co-creator Peter Fluck dies aged 85

Rockstar Games and Take-Two Interactive announced that Grand Theft Auto VI generated $3.79 billion (£2.88 billion) in global retail sales in its first 24 hours on Thursday—driven by 32.5 million pre-release sales across PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X/S—making it the biggest entertainment launch in history. The day-one figure is nearly five times Grand Theft Auto V’s $800 million opening in 2013 and beats the $2.9 billion lifetime box-office run of Avengers: Endgame, while Visibrain reported more social media posts than this year’s World Cup and Comcast logged the busiest internet traffic day in its 63-year history, peaking 31 per cent above average. As Rockstar directors Steven Messinger and John Macpherson detailed how Florida Keys research gave Leonida’s non-player characters autonomous routines and over 100 interiors—and a Neuralink patient drove boats, flew planes, and fought shootouts as Jason Duval using his brain implant—players also hit snags: PS5 users reported crashes with error code CE-108255-1, Standard Edition buyers were locked out of Vice City shops reserved for the $99.99 Ultimate Edition, a UK indie shop sold just five physical copies, and Nicalis patched out undisclosed Suno AI music from a GTA-themed daily run in The Binding of Isaac: Repentance+ after creator Edmund McMillen disavowed the tracks amid a Steam review-bombing. (Guardian, IGN, IGN, IGN, IGN, IGN, IGN, BBC, IGN)

British satire mourned the death at home in Cornwall aged 85 of caricaturist and puppeteer Peter Fluck, who co-created ITV’s landmark puppet show Spitting Image with Cambridge School of Art friend Roger Law. Forming “Luck and Flaw” after Rupert Murdoch’s Fleet Street takeovers squeezed out newspaper cartoonists, the pair modelled clay caricatures into life-sized foam-rubber puppets that skewered Margaret Thatcher’s cabinet, Ronald Reagan, celebrities, and the royals across 18 seasons from 1984 to 1996, winning a BAFTA and two Emmys and drawing up to 18 million viewers. (Guardian, BBC)

Elsewhere in British culture, Lancashire Police seized a mobile phone after unusual noises disrupted Saturday’s live Strictly Come Dancing broadcast from Blackpool Tower Ballroom while Motsi Mabuse judged Delta Goodrem and Kai Widdrington, prompting an apology from host Emma Willis and an iPlayer edit. The BBC also startled the Radio Times by announcing that festive specials including Mortimer & Whitehouse: Gone Christmas Fishing, The Repair Shop at Christmas, and Death in Paradise will land on iPlayer on 27 November—up to a month before TV broadcast—while keeping the Strictly Christmas special, Amandaland, and Call the Midwife prequel Sisters in Arms as linear premieres. Meanwhile, Channel 4 aired Dominic Savage’s improvised drama I Am Helen, starring Nicola Coughlan and Joe Cole in a story about non-consensual choking during sex weeks after the UK outlawed strangulation porn; critics faulted Nathan Fielder’s three-hour A24 film You Can See Everything—shot at Theranos fraudster Elizabeth Holmes’s home before her 11-year prison term—for trading courtroom facts for eye-gazing “vibes checks”; John Mulaney and Jane Wickline’s viral SNL sketch Sister drew seven million views and praise from Melinda French Gates and Senator Kirsten Gillibrand for highlighting daughters’ unpaid elder-care burden; Fontaines D.C. earned rave reviews at Liverpool’s M&S Bank Arena showcasing Dopamine Chamber; and 70 British Library guards protested plans to outsource security and turn three reading rooms into commercial spaces. (Guardian, Guardian, BBC, Guardian, Guardian, Guardian, Guardian)

Madueke rescues 2–2 draw for Arsenal at Newcastle as Raya suffers shoulder injury, Leeds humiliate Alonso’s Chelsea 4–1 and Kane faces months out

Noni Madueke’s late counter-attacking goal salvaged a 2–2 draw for Premier League leaders Arsenal against Matthias Jaissle’s Newcastle United at St James’ Park on Saturday evening, keeping Mikel Arteta’s champions six points clear at the top but costing them goalkeeper David Raya to a left shoulder injury. Arsenal dominated the first half—where former Newcastle captain Bruno Guimarães was booed and Jaissle dropped or benched £180 million of summer signings—and led when Kai Havertz robbed Lewis Hall and fed Eberechi Eze to arrow home his first league goal since April. Jaissle turned the game at half-time by introducing Tino Livramento, Jacob Ramsey, and Anthony Elanga: a heavy touch by Martin Ødegaard let William Osula set up Harvey Barnes to equalise, and Dan Burn headed down a Hall corner for Malick Thiaw to put Newcastle 2–1 up. Raya limped off in the 70th minute after hurting his shoulder against Osula—leaving £72 million deputy Kepa Arrizabalaga and Illan Meslier as cover for a keeper with a league-low seven goals conceded—before Ødegaard released Madueke to dummy two defenders and beat Lukas Hornicek via a deflection as Newcastle chair Yasir al-Rumayyan watched on, ending Arsenal’s 21-game league run without a draw. (Guardian, BBC)

At Stamford Bridge, Daniel Farke’s Leeds United routed Chelsea 4–1 for their first win at the stadium since December 1999 to climb to sixth, deepening the crisis around Xabi Alonso as eighth-placed Chelsea suffered their fifth defeat in 11 league games—more losses than 18th-placed Manchester United ahead of Mauricio Pochettino’s debut at Liverpool on Sunday—and lost home league games to Leeds and Tottenham in the same season for the first time in 67 years. After debutant Emmanuel Emegha put Chelsea ahead and Moisés Caicedo went off injured, two Noah Okafor goals, a Dominic Calvert-Lewin penalty, and a Brenden Aaronson strike sparked walkouts, boos, and a clash between Cole Palmer and Chelsea “cultural architect” Willie Isa. Off the pitch, Elon Musk’s lawyer Alex Spiro—who also represents FIFA investor Joshua Kushner against UEFA—joined NFL great Tom Brady in talks to buy minority stakes in Chelsea owner Clearlake Capital after its £1 billion buyout of Todd Boehly and Mark Walter. (Guardian, BBC, Guardian, Guardian)

Elsewhere, Enzo Maresca’s third-placed Manchester City were booed after a 1–1 draw with Fulham extended their winless run to four games and saw them concede in nine straight matches: ex-City winger Oscar Bobb set up Rodrigo Muniz before Elliot Anderson levelled and VAR disallowed an Erling Haaland effort for a foul on Bernd Leno, ending City’s English-record 20-game winning streak against Fulham since 2011. Second-placed Brighton—level with City ahead of Sunday’s trip to Hull behind Paul Barber’s analytics model and £3.5 million midfielder Yasin Ayari—sit above Bournemouth, who won a club-record ninth straight match as Evanilson and Justin Kluivert beat Nottingham Forest 2–0. At Tottenham, 18-year-old Luca Williams-Barnett became Spurs’ youngest Premier League scorer since Gareth Bale in 2007 and assisted Dominic Solanke in a 2–1 win over Ipswich Town, drawing comparisons from Roberto De Zerbi to Lamine Yamal. At the bottom, Thomas Frank lost his Crystal Palace debut 3–2 at Frank Lampard’s Coventry City via a late ricochet for Palace’s fifth straight loss, while 19th-placed Sunderland held Aston Villa 0–0 as Chemsdine Talbi hit the post. (Guardian, Guardian, Guardian, Guardian, Guardian, Guardian)

In Europe, Bayern Munich confirmed England captain and Ballon d’Or winner Harry Kane faces “the next few months” out with a torn left ankle ligament suffered against Cologne’s Joël Schmied in Friday’s 1–1 draw. Paris Saint-Germain also lost France forward Désiré Doué to a serious right knee injury after he scored in a 3–2 win at Nice ahead of Wednesday’s Champions League clash with Roma—who moved second in Serie A behind Lazio as Donyell Malen scored four at Parma—adding to Khvicha Kvaratskhelia’s ACL tear as Ferran Torres sealed PSG’s 12th straight win, while Carlos Espí’s late goal gave Real Madrid a 2–1 win over Celta Vigo to join Barcelona on 33 points despite Kylian Mbappé’s third missed penalty in six attempts. (Guardian, Guardian)
