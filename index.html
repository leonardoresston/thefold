News

COP31 climate summit collapses without a deal in Antalya as Ukrainian sea drones sink Russian warship in Libya and strikes kill 13 near Kyiv

United Nations climate talks at the COP31 summit in Antalya, Turkey, broke down on Sunday afternoon after running more than 40 hours past their Friday deadline, becoming the longest UN climate conference on record and the first since Copenhagen in 2009 to fail to adopt a central political deal. Turkish COP President Murat Kurum and Australian negotiations president Chris Bowen abandoned their proposed “Antalya pact” cover decision after nearly 40 nations registered objections on the plenary floor. While a Saudi-led bloc refused any text endorsing a transition away from fossil fuels, a coalition of over 130 countries—led by the European Union, the UK, Norway, Panama, the Marshall Islands, and least-developed states—rejected a diluted text that omitted the words “fossil fuels”, promoted carbon capture, and spoke only of an “irreversible transition towards lower greenhouse gas emissions”. Speaking for the EU to applause, Irish Environment Minister Darragh O’Brien said the text failed to respond to science and that Brussels would never endorse greenwashing. (Guardian, BBC)

At his final COP before stepping down as UN Secretary-General, António Guterres expressed deep disappointment and accused fossil-fuel interests of copying Big Tobacco’s playbook by distorting science and blocking policy while piling up profits. Australian Prime Minister Anthony Albanese cited difficult geopolitical times, whereas Climate Analytics chief Bill Hare praised governments for rejecting a hollow text and argued Australia should never again co-chair talks while exporting fossil fuels. Simultaneously, campaigners condemned wealthy nations for blocking adaptation finance: Climate Action Network director Tasneem Essop called Antalya the worst COP ever, and Power Shift Africa’s Mohamed Adow faulted Europe for demanding fossil-fuel roadmaps while withholding funds and expanding domestic oil and gas. Delegates passed only minor side measures, including a just-transition mechanism that Indigenous leader Edson Krenak and former Colombian minister Irene Vélez Torres called inadequate. (Guardian, Guardian, BBC)

Russia’s war against Ukraine expanded into the central Mediterranean at dawn on Sunday when suspected Ukrainian sea drones struck and sank the Merkury, a Russian Navy Project 20380 Steregushchiy-class corvette refuelling at Benghazi naval base in eastern Libya, over 1,000 miles from southern Ukraine. Authorities aligned with General Khalifa Haftar said four sea drones were intercepted before two hit the warship, with Planet Labs and Vantor satellite imagery showing the vessel capsized and sunk; hours later, another sea drone struck the Russia-linked cargo ship Ascalon off Benghazi, prompting the UN mission in Libya (UNSMIL) to warn against Libya becoming a foreign battleground. In Ukraine’s Kyiv region, rescuers in Dymer dug for 14 hours on Sunday to pull a seven-year-old boy and a 79-year-old woman alive from a bombed residential building where overnight Russian strikes killed nine people, while separate attacks killed one person in Bila Tserkva and three at a grocery store in Vyshneve. (Guardian, BBC, BBC)

In the Middle East, an Oxfam and Conflict and Environment Observatory report found that Israel’s campaign in Gaza—which has killed over 74,000 Palestinians and wounded 175,000—has left a “toxic wasteland” of heavy metals, asbestos, munitions residue, and raw sewage that Oxfam chief Amitabh Behar termed environmental war crimes. In the West Bank, Israeli opposition leader Gadi Eisenkot—tasked with forming a government after October’s inconclusive election denied Benjamin Netanyahu a majority—condemned settler arson attacks on four Palestinian villages including Kafr Malik as “Jewish terrorism” stoked by ministers Itamar Ben-Gvir and Bezalel Smotrich. In the US, Democrat Chuck Hubbard unseated 21-year Republican incumbent Virginia Foxx by 330 votes after a North Carolina recount, giving Democrats at least 258 House seats to Republicans’ 173—the largest majority since 1992—and vowing to probe Donald Trump’s business conflicts as Trump hosted his third $TRUMP meme-coin gala in Virginia, where over half of leaderboard wallets linked to foreign exchanges. Meanwhile, Seattle-Tacoma airport saw protests after masked ICE agents detained father Ricardo Larrondo Mendoza and daughter Sofia Hernandez Mejia on an Alaska Airlines jet bridge, and the US Army invoked emergency rules to bypass a court stay and detonate unstable explosives in Middletown, Iowa. (Guardian, Guardian, BBC, Guardian, Guardian, Guardian, Guardian)

Thirty-one NHS trusts warn of unpalatable cuts as Timms calls for PIP overhaul and Burnham defends EU reset before MPs

Thirty-one NHS hospital trusts in England have warned in their risk registers that NHS England savings demands threaten patient care and normal operations. Twenty-seven cited lack of money as their top threat, and six—London’s King’s College Hospital (£85 million savings target) and St George’s, Epsom and St Helier (£91 million), plus North Midlands, Gloucestershire, Doncaster and Bassetlaw, and Birmingham and Solihull mental health trusts—gave finances the maximum risk score of 25 as trusts seek up to £106 million each amid drug inflation, strikes, heatwaves, and IT rollouts. East Kent cancer patients waited up to a month for pre-chemotherapy appointments after bank-nurse pay cuts; York and Scarborough shut a 28-bed Bridlington step-down ward and weighed cutting urgent treatment hours; Harrogate ended 15 years of rural out-of-hours GP cover; and Sherwood Forest moved to close King’s Mill Hospital’s hydrotherapy pool. To ease clinical workloads, the NHS launched the EDITH trial across 30 sites starting at Cambridge University Hospitals, inviting 700,000 women aged 50 to 71 to test artificial intelligence replacing one of two human mammogram readers. (Guardian, BBC)

Disability Minister Sir Stephen Timms confirmed that his review of Personal Independence Payment (PIP), due on Tuesday, will recommend a “fundamental overhaul” of assessments while keeping the benefit for all adult ages. Co-chaired with disability advocates Clenton Farquharson and Sharon Brennan, the review deems the 13-year-old setup unfit for purpose amid rising mental health and neurodivergent claims and proposes a fast track for lifelong, degenerative, or terminal conditions. Retaining universal eligibility contrasts with Alan Milburn’s NEETs report last week, which proposed scrapping PIP for under-25s to save £3.6 billion, handing Prime Minister Andy Burnham a delicate choice after 120 Labour MPs rebelled against £5 billion in welfare cuts in 2025. On Monday, Burnham will tell MPs that Friday’s Brussels deal—ending most food, plant, and animal border checks from 1 March 2028 alongside carbon-market and youth-mobility pacts—is this parliament’s biggest growth measure, worth 0.5 per cent of GDP (£17 billion). While Scottish seed potato exporter Jamieson Brothers welcomed reopening a £112 million trade, Tory leader Kemi Badenoch will tell the CBI that Burnham botched the talks even as polling shows 512 of 632 British seats favour rejoining the EU and Theresa May urged politicians on BBC One to move on from Brexit. (Guardian, BBC, BBC, Guardian, Guardian)

Burnham also faces a winter squeeze ahead of Wednesday’s Ofgem decision, with Cornwall Insight forecasting a 26 per cent (£443) rise in January’s typical annual energy price cap to £2,166 as Middle East turmoil and 3.6 per cent inflation hit northern towns hardest. Centrica chief Chris O’Shea warned that £5 billion in household energy arrears will climb as non-payment adds £1.60 a week to paying customers’ bills, while Poundland—with 600 stores and 11,000 staff—has days to find a buyer after owner Gordon Brothers froze £50 million despite an £18.1 million profit turnaround. Elsewhere, TaxWatch FOI data showed UK taxpayers’ offshore tax-haven funds topped £1 trillion after a 10 per cent rise as HMRC assigned compliance managers to 200 billionaires; police evacuated 12,000 fans from Manchester’s Co-op Live after a suspected tear-gas release at Don Toliver’s concert left 50 treated; RAF chief Sir Harv Smyth said the 2035 Tempest jet could be Britain’s last crewed fighter as autonomous jets fly next year; a probe found police and medics twice released paranoid schizophrenic Alexander Lewis-Ranwell before he killed three Exeter men in their 80s; Laura Brand vowed on YouTube to obey prison rules; and historians showed four 19th-century Bank of England directors profited from post-abolition Cuban slavery. (BBC, Guardian, BBC, Guardian, BBC, BBC, Guardian, Guardian, Guardian)

Federal Police reports deepen Brazil’s Supreme Court rift over Banco Master as Amazon highway BR-319 paving advances for 2027

The transfer of four Federal Police (PF) reports mentioning Supreme Federal Court (STF) justices to Prosecutor-General Paulo Gonet has opened an internal clash over who will oversee the Banco Master investigation. The inquiry into former banker Daniel Vorcaro’s ties to senior magistrates is on its third rapporteur since November 2025: after Justice Dias Toffoli recused himself and Justice André Mendonça ran the case from February to 12 September, STF President Edson Fachin temporarily took control. Mendonça’s allies view the decision by Fachin and PF director-general Andrei Rodrigues—whom Mendonça had previously ordered removed—to send a report citing Mendonça to the Prosecutor-General’s Office rather than archiving it alongside six other thin files as a move to oust him and stall operations. Conversely, justices close to Alexandre de Moraes argue Mendonça’s selective disclosures and personal ties make his position untenable, urging that the case move from the Second Panel to the full 11-justice plenary. (Folha)

In the Amazon, northern lawmakers emboldened by October’s election victories are moving to advance the paving of 400 kilometres of the 900-kilometre BR-319 highway linking Manaus and Porto Velho in 2027, threatening one of the rainforest’s best-preserved regions where illegal side roads (ramais) fuel land grabbing, logging, and wildcat mining. Senator Omar Aziz (PSD-AM), elected governor of Amazonas in October, met his state’s congressional caucus on 10 November alongside re-elected Senator Eduardo Braga (MDB-AM)—the newly named general rapporteur for the 2027 federal budget—to route parliamentary amendments through Congress’s infrastructure committees while pushing legislation to relax environmental licensing rules. (Folha)

In Brasília, as President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva forms his fourth-term cabinet, Workers’ Party (PT) senators pressed party chair Edinho Silva to keep the Health and Education ministries out of Centrão hands while competing with the MDB, Progressistas (PP), and Presidential Secretariat Minister Guilherme Boulos—who plans to join the PT in early 2027—for the Cities Ministry overseeing Minha Casa, Minha Vida. A survey by political scientist Sérgio Praça of Brazil’s 38,500 federal commissioned posts found that 1 in 10 appointees (4,149 officials) belongs to a party, with Centrão and right-wing parties holding 46.5 per cent of partisan regional posts outside Brasília—including Finance Ministry, AGU, Funasa, and DNIT branches—against 51 per cent for the left. Meanwhile, former president Jair Bolsonaro remained stable in intensive care at DF Star hospital on Sunday without a discharge date, fasting on nutritional support for a near-total small-intestine blockage that cardiologist Brasil Caiado warned may require surgery; the Brazilian Distilled Spirits Association backed the government’s Selective Tax provisional measure while seeking 2028 rate predictability; Deputy Mendonça Filho delayed his report on lowering the criminal responsibility age to 16 until December; Senators Leany Lemos and Eliziane Gama pushed a resolution punishing gender and racial political violence with mandate loss; and economist Samuel Pessôa examined why Brazil’s interest rates stay high. (Folha, Folha, Folha, Folha, Folha, Folha, Meio)

US intelligence chief and Microsoft warn of further tech outages after Delta hub glitch as AI reskinning and Black Friday clone scams surge

US Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton and Microsoft President Brad Smith warned on Sunday that travellers and businesses face months of further technological disruptions after a wave of major outages. On Sunday morning, Delta Air Lines flights at Hartsfield-Jackson Atlanta International Airport suffered widespread delays when automated software calculating aircraft weight, fuel distribution, and centre of gravity failed, forcing crews onto manual workarounds. The glitch followed a multi-day Microsoft Outlook outage, disruptions at JPMorgan Chase, Apple, and the National Security Agency, a cyber-attack on 178 organisations, and the longest US aviation shutdown since 9/11 on 12 November, which cancelled over 18,000 flights after a newly replaced router misfired in El Dorado, Arkansas, during contractor L3Harris’s 24-year project to replace FAA copper lines with fibre-optic cables. Clayton told CNN to expect “bumps in the road” during a step change in compute, while Smith told CBS’s Face the Nation that upgrading global infrastructure will take months as artificial intelligence helps both defenders and hackers find code flaws. (The Verge, The Verge, Guardian)

While the AI boom has revived downtown San Francisco—filling offices around OpenAI and newly listed Anthropic, lifting rents, and spurring services such as Lauren Goldboss’s startup dog-walking firm Paw Perk even as the IMF warns of a stock correction and Nvidia navigates Jensen Huang’s exit with quarterly profits doubling to $65 billion—cheap generative tools are driving digital theft and retail fraud. Performers including UK guitarist Sophie Lloyd and Norwegian creator Kjetil Krogstad revealed that third-party accounts are “reskinning” their exact videos, movements, and music onto hypersexualised AI avatars such as “Mila Vasari” to harvest millions of views and funnel subscribers to adult site Fanvue. Simultaneously, National Trading Standards, Norton, and Guardio warned ahead of Black Friday on 27 November that fraudsters are deploying AI-cloned brand storefronts offering 80 per cent discounts while “poisoning” search results and chatbots such as ChatGPT into recommending scam links. (BBC, BBC, Guardian)

In corporate and hardware news, The Hague’s District Court banned Chinese automaker BYD from selling its seven-seat Ti7 plug-in hybrid SUV across the EU for infringing Jaguar Land Rover’s Defender design—though the ruling lacks jurisdiction in the UK, where the £47,995 Ti7 undercuts the Defender by £20,000 ahead of January deliveries—while Indian Youth Congress activists stormed Meta’s New Delhi offices over blocked opposition Instagram accounts. Within Apple’s lineup, Bloomberg’s Mark Gurman reported that the February–March 2027 iOS and macOS 27.4 cycle will bring a second-generation MacBook Neo with an A19 Pro chip, 12GB of RAM, and new colours alongside an A19 budget iPad, iPhone 18, and iPhone 18e. Apple also detailed iOS 27’s Connectivity Assist for blending cellular data with weak Wi-Fi, while code leaks showed Apple tested a right-edge Lock Screen complication column for two circular widgets on the foldable iPhone Duo alongside independent screen orientation locks, external display case adjustments, rear-camera selfies, and Duo FaceTime. Elsewhere, Reto unveiled the $34.99 Kodak SlideTag E Ink photo keychain and Find My tracker, and Drew Michael’s off-Broadway show Love Me used Bluetooth Auracast headphones to simulate hearing loss live. (Guardian, The Verge, 9to5Mac, 9to5Mac, 9to5Mac, MacMagazine, MacMagazine, The Verge, The Verge)

Jilly Cooper accused of extensive plagiarism in 1978 novel Prudence as Rockstar explains GTA VI cuts and Nan Goldin dazzles at the Hayward

Late novelist Dame Jilly Cooper—who died last October having sold over 11 million books in the UK, while Disney+ airs the second series of her Rutshire adaptation Rivals—faces accusations of “extensive and deliberate plagiarism” in her 1978 romance Prudence. An Observer analysis identified at least 35 passages lifted from Cyra McFadden’s 1977 Marin County satire The Serial alongside borrowings from Josephine Tey’s The Franchise Affair, including near-verbatim dialogue for Cooper’s American feminist character Berenice; McFadden’s daughter Caroline McFadden called the copying unconscionable and is exploring legal action. In London, Nan Goldin’s retrospective You Never Did Anything Wrong opened to five-star acclaim at the Hayward Gallery, pairing her raw photographs of lovers and opioid addiction with Caravaggio, Correggio, and Canova in the bronze-yurt installation Stendhal Syndrome and an animal-gaze film named after a pet-cemetery headstone, while 186 Gwydir Street in Cambridge—where Victorian working-class “art-workman” David Parr spent 40 years painting William Morris-inspired interiors by candlelight—won a blue plaque for unveiling in February 2027. (Guardian, Guardian, Guardian)

After Grand Theft Auto VI’s record $3.79 billion opening day, Rockstar North co-head Rob Nelson revealed that darts and bowling were cut because even Rockstar lacked “unlimited money or unlimited time” to match the physical weight of Leonida’s pool tables and gym workouts. Narrative vice-president Rupert Humphries and writers Michael Wiafe and Elizabeth Gauvey-Kern explained that protagonist Jason Duval’s stoic demeanour drew on Steve McQueen in Sam Peckinpah’s The Getaway and interviews with US Army Rangers, while personal cars act as mobile bases storing two-longarm gun loadouts, robbery stash bags, and five trunk outfits to evade police descriptions. Inside Jason’s JoySystem console, an internal team built three complete games—led by the four-hour isometric zombie roguelike deckbuilder Street Trash 3: Dezombifier alongside Time Jackers Rewind and the Jonathan Blow and Hideo Kojima parody Tenebrae Lux—while players visited a memorial wall north of Hamlet where QA developer Matt Green honoured his late mother Bev Green alongside production designer Terrance Radcliff’s father Elhuntry and daughter Jade. (IGN, IGN, IGN, IGN, IGN)

Elsewhere in culture, Steve Coogan and Rob Brydon reunited with Michael Winterbottom after six years for The Trip to the Northern Lights across Sweden and Norway to the Arctic Circle, trading Melvyn Bragg impressions at Valldal’s Juvet Landskapshotell, while prankster Oobah Butler launched HBO Max’s Scams for a Better Tomorrow by marketing “ethically extracted” children’s-tear serum with ex-Theranos creative chief Patrick O’Neill, selling $500 wildfire-tainted Napa wine, running a human-staffed AI dating app, and sending fake AI rapper Nepo viral. In New York, Pitchfork founder Ryan Schreiber previewed his memoir Weird Era, discussing the site’s 1995 Minneapolis launch, his regret over Liz Phair’s 0.0 review, and his 2015 sale to Condé Nast and Anna Wintour before quitting in 2018 and coming out as gay. Meanwhile, Errollyn Wallen premiered her nativity community opera A Christmas Miracle with 150 performers at Suffolk’s Orford Church; Interpol outshone co-headliners Bloc Party in Cardiff; Miley Cyrus married drummer Maxx Morando in Palm Springs; and Australian broadcaster Ian “Molly” Meldrum died in Melbourne aged 83. (Guardian, Guardian, Guardian, Guardian, Guardian, BBC, BBC)

Sports

Liverpool rout 10-man Manchester United 4–0 on Pochettino’s debut as McBurnie stuns Brighton, Arsenal await Raya scan and UK secures 2035 Women’s World Cup

Mauricio Pochettino suffered the joint-heaviest debut defeat in Manchester United’s history on Sunday as Andoni Iraola’s Liverpool routed his 10-man side 4–0 at Anfield, matching Lal Hilditch’s 4–0 loss to Bolton Wanderers in October 1926 and leaving United 19th in the Premier League with eight points from 11 games—their worst start since 1972–73 and the first time they have sat in the bottom two after 10 matches since November 1986. Pochettino’s decision to drop Harry Maguire for Matthijs de Ligt’s first league start in 12 months backfired in the eighth minute when De Ligt misjudged a Ronald Araújo pass; Alexander Isak raced through and was fouled outside the box by 19-year-old Leny Yoro, who received a straight red card. Dominik Szoboszlai curled the free-kick past a disorganised wall and Senne Lammens, and three minutes later 18-year-old Rio Ngumoha beat Diogo Dalot to set up Florian Wirtz for 2–0 amid mocking Kop chants. Ngumoha—the fixture’s youngest Premier League scorer—and Víctor Muñoz completed the rout, taking United to 34 goals conceded in all competitions, the most in Europe’s top five leagues, as Pochettino protested Yoro’s dismissal and a denied Bryan Mbeumo penalty claim while captain Bruno Fernandes confirmed contract talks are on hold. (Guardian, Guardian, Guardian, Guardian, BBC)

Earlier on Sunday, second-placed Brighton & Hove Albion missed the chance to cut Arsenal’s lead at the top of the Premier League to three points when Oli McBurnie scored twice in stoppage time to give promoted Hull City a 2–0 win at the MKM Stadium. Fabian Hürzeler’s side dominated for 88 minutes but were repeatedly thwarted by Greek goalkeeper Konstantinos Tzolakis, who saved at close range from Diego Gómez and Yasin Ayari before Brighton’s João Costinha received a second yellow card in the 89th minute for fouling Sorba Thomas. McBurnie bundled home from close range in the 91st minute and tapped into an empty net on a counter-attack two minutes later to preserve Sergej Jakirović’s unbeaten home record and secure Hull’s first league win since August. Meanwhile, leaders Arsenal—six points clear of Brighton and Manchester City after Saturday’s 2–2 draw at Newcastle United—awaited Sunday MRI scan results on goalkeeper David Raya, who left St James’ Park in tears after hurting his shoulder colliding with William Osula having kept seven clean sheets in 10 league games. (Guardian, Guardian)

In international football, FIFA is set to confirm on Monday at a virtual meeting in Zurich that the United Kingdom will host the 2035 Women’s World Cup, bringing a football World Cup back to British soil for the first time since 1966 after England, Scotland, Wales, and Northern Ireland stood as the sole bidders. The four home associations’ “All Together” bid proposes a 48-team, 104-match tournament over 39 days across 19 stadiums—14 in England including Everton’s Hill Dickinson Stadium, two in Scotland, two in Wales, and one in Northern Ireland—and scored 4.3 out of 5 in FIFA’s technical evaluation, though inspectors noted medium contractual risk over UK government qualifications on security guarantees and FIFA’s demand for tax-free status. Football Association chief executive Mark Bullingham said staging the 2035 tournament across all four nations would build on the surge in women’s and girls’ participation since the Lionesses’ Euro 2022 victory and follow the UK and Ireland’s joint hosting of the men’s European Championship in 2028. (BBC)
