Xi, Trump and Putin hold historic Shenzhen trilateral as Ukraine strikes Kerch Bridge again and Musk disavows MAGA

Xi Jinping, Donald Trump, and Vladimir Putin sit down in Shenzhen today for the first trilateral meeting ever held between the presidents of China, the United States, and Russia, a gathering that has drawn anxious comparisons across Europe to the 1945 Yalta conference. Xi set the stage on Wednesday by breaking decades of Chinese protocol to greet Trump on the airport tarmac with an embrace—prompting Trump to call his host an “incredible leader”—before standing between his American and Russian counterparts for the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation (APEC) group photograph after Canadian and Australian delegates shelved threatened boycotts. In his keynote address, Xi cast Beijing as the defender of open supply chains against rampant protectionism even as Washington restricted Chinese firms from renting US artificial intelligence chips overseas, while Putin dismissed Western powers as “former hegemons” and declared Europe to be in a “humiliating decline” ahead of today’s talks on Ukraine, the US war in Iran, nuclear arms, and trade. (Guardian, Guardian, BBC)

Hours before the Shenzhen talks began, Ukrainian forces struck the Kerch Bridge linking Russia to occupied Crimea for the third time this week. Moscow-installed governor Sergei Aksyonov halted all remaining traffic early on Thursday to assess fresh damage to the crossing, where a seven-stage Ukrainian drone and Flamingo cruise-missile attack had already dropped a 227-metre road span into the Black Sea on Tuesday, while Russian drones killed three civilians in Odesa. After visiting wounded veterans in Lviv, Prince Harry told the Telegraph that Europe had been in an undeclared war with Moscow for a decade and said he would rejoin the British Army if needed. Speaking by video from China to the New York Times DealBook Summit, SpaceX chief executive Elon Musk accused Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy of lying about Pentagon-controlled Starshield disruptions and misusing civilian Starlink terminals for offensive drone strikes, saying he refused to make “kill decisions”. In the same interview, Musk declared “I’m not MAGA”, backed split party control in Washington, and admitted he felt “pretty foolish” after spending a record $446 million (£339 million) to back Republicans in last week’s midterms only for Democrats to sweep Congress. (Guardian, BBC, BBC, The Verge, Guardian, BBC)

In the Middle East, Israeli tanks crossed the “yellow line” along central Gaza’s Salah al-Din road into Nuseirat on Wednesday in the military’s first ground incursion into Hamas-controlled territory since October’s ceasefire, displacing families from tent camps a day after the US-backed Board of Peace accused both sides of truce breaches. The raid came as opposition leader Gadi Eisenkot worked to form an Israeli government without caretaker prime minister Benjamin Netanyahu, while Attorney General Gali Baharav-Miara froze building tenders for the E1 West Bank settlement days after UK Foreign Secretary Ed Miliband sanctioned eight Israeli construction firms. Elsewhere, the International Court of Justice rules today in The Gambia’s seven-year genocide case against Myanmar over the 2017 Rohingya massacres—potentially the first conviction of a state under the 1948 Genocide Convention—and a Miami federal jury convicted pastor Christian Emmanuel Sanon over the 2021 assassination of Haitian President Jovenel Moïse. At COP31, Panamanian Environment Minister Juan Carlos Navarro warned that the world was “held hostage by the petroleum mafia” as 30 nations backed a separate fossil fuel treaty, while the UN Population Fund found that one in four women globally—and 45 per cent in west and central Africa—cannot refuse sex within marriage. (Guardian, BBC, Guardian, BBC, Guardian, Guardian, Guardian)

Brussels presses Burnham for Brexit clarity as Iran war drives UK inflation to 3.6% and energy bills toward £2,166

European Union capitals have warned Downing Street that they will shut the door to further long-term negotiations after Friday’s Brussels summit until Prime Minister Andy Burnham decides whether Britain wants to reverse Brexit. Diplomats said member states had tired of piecemeal “reset” summits and wanted a durable cross-party British consensus before investing further effort. While Friday’s joint statement will advance talks on the EU single energy market, irregular migration, and defence, a leaked draft shows friction over Brussels’s demand that London align with EU tariffs on Chinese electric vehicles to qualify for Made in Europe industrial rules. Burnham plans to launch a “national conversation” on Europe next week, even as pro-rejoin MPs worry they have been outflanked by the launch on X of Stay Out—a resurrected Leave campaign claiming rejoining would cost £750 million a week—and after a bruising Prime Minister’s Questions clash with Kemi Badenoch further dented Burnham’s promise of consensual politics. (Guardian, Guardian, Guardian)

At home, oil and gas price shocks from the war in Iran pushed UK consumer price inflation from 3.5 per cent in September to a one-year high of 3.6 per cent in October. Office for National Statistics data showed annual motor fuel inflation jumping 33.2 per cent—the steepest rise since July 2022—as petrol reached a four-year high of 174.7p a litre and diesel hit 199.5p, the highest price since records began in 1996. Ahead of next Wednesday’s Ofgem announcement, consultancy Cornwall Insight forecast that typical dual-fuel energy bills in Great Britain will surge 26 per cent in January to £2,166 a year, adding £443 to household costs and dwarfing Chancellor John Healey’s £100 winter payment for 20 million homes. In Parliament today, Housing Minister Matthew Pennycook introduces the Commonhold and Leasehold Reform Bill to ban virtually all new leasehold flats in England and Wales in favour of resident-owned commonhold and cap existing annual ground rents at £250 from 2028 before reducing them to a peppercorn rate after 40 years. (Guardian, Guardian, Guardian, BBC)

Two prominent British legal cases also saw major disclosures today. The Marine Accident Investigation Branch’s final report into the August 2024 sinking of the 56-metre superyacht Bayesian off Porticello, Sicily—which killed software tycoon Mike Lynch, his 18-year-old daughter Hannah, Jonathan and Judy Bloomer, Chris and Neda Morvillo, and chef Recaldo Thomas—concluded that an unforecast 70-knot (80mph) thunderstorm downburst knocked the yacht flat in under 15 seconds and sank it in 18 minutes, and that onboard stability documents never warned the crew of its vulnerability at anchor with sails down and the centreboard raised. Separately, an investigation revealed that paediatric endocrinologist Professor Peter Hindmarsh—the prosecution’s key expert witness arguing that nurse Lucy Letby poisoned babies with insulin—was barred from clinical practice by University College London Hospitals Trust in March 2023, five months before the verdicts, over fears his insulin advice posed an unacceptable risk to child patients. Neither Letby’s defence nor the jury was told of his exclusion or an April 2023 tribunal supervision order. (Guardian, BBC, Guardian)

Elsewhere in Whitehall and the courts, Google refused a Prison Service request to remove a YouTube video in which Laura Brand read a prison message from her husband Russell Brand protesting his innocence after his ten-and-a-half-year sentence for rape and sexual assault. Home Secretary Shabana Mahmood backed banning uniformed police from marching in Pride parades under her new impartiality standard, arguing that gender-identity debates made uniformed participation look political—remarks City of London Police Deputy Commissioner Nik Adams warned made LGBTQ+ people feel less safe. In Birmingham, High Court Judge Emma Kelly granted an interim injunction barring anti-migrant group Raise the Colours from hanging flags on council lampposts, hours before a Stafford jury convicted 36-year-old group member Billy Allison of murdering Matthew O’Gara with an unprovoked punch in Lichfield. Meanwhile, a judge rejected McDonald’s bid to strike out harassment claims by hundreds of workers alleging managers failed to protect teenage staff. (BBC, Guardian, Guardian, Guardian, Guardian, BBC)

Federal Police hand Banco Master files on Supreme Court justices to Fachin as Brazil’s Senate stalls on fiscal package

Federal Police (PF) Director-General Andrei Rodrigues delivered the confidential reports detailing links between former Banco Master owner Daniel Vorcaro and Supreme Federal Court (STF) justices to Court President Edson Fachin on Wednesday evening. Fachin immediately forwarded the sealed files to the Prosecutor-General’s Office (PGR) with a 15-day review deadline—an awkward step given that Prosecutor-General Paulo Gonet appears in Vorcaro’s messages and was photographed smoking cigars and drinking whisky with the banker in London in April 2024, though the Federal Public Ministry council voted 5–4 in September against investigating him. Testifying before a joint congressional hearing, Rodrigues defended removing the delegates who wrote the reports and opening a leak probe, arguing they overstepped STF limits by investigating justices without authorisation and risked nullifying the case. Hours earlier, leaked audio from Vorcaro’s phone revealed his lawyer Walfrido Warde boasting of inside information on Rodrigues’s actions in a São Paulo PF inquiry into alleged Gafisa and Banco Master market manipulation—a claim Rodrigues denied. (Folha, Folha, Folha, Meio)

In Congress, Senate President Davi Alcolumbre and rapporteur Eduardo Braga postponed the final vote on four amendments to Planning Minister Bruno Moretti’s R$46 billion-to-R$56 billion fiscal bill after coalition absences threatened a defeat over corporate tax-credit caps. Business groups also warned that trimming the Workers’ Food Programme (PAT) corporate tax deduction from 3.6 per cent to 3.2 per cent—a R$300 million clawback—could prompt employers to drop meal vouchers used by 22 million workers. After low turnout at Tuesday’s protests with the National Union of Students, PSOL leaders Juliano Medeiros, Manuela D’Ávila, and Pastor Henrique Vieira ruled out further rallies and reined in radical critics such as incoming deputy Jones Manoel, while the Human Rights Ministry cancelled a R$722,000 staff massage tender. Meanwhile, Workers’ Party chief Edinho Silva opened fourth-term cabinet talks offering União Brasil Regional Development and Tourism while shifting Communications and Science and Technology to the Progressistas (PP), which demanded bigger budgets. (Meio, Meio, Folha, Folha, Folha, Folha)

Speaking at the Buenos Aires Forum alongside Justice Gilmar Mendes, Chamber Speaker Hugo Motta confirmed he and Alcolumbre had asked Justice Alexandre de Moraes to schedule the constitutional actions on the “Dosimetry Law” cutting sentences for Jair Bolsonaro and the 8 January 2023 coup convicts to “turn this page”. Motta’s February re-election bid faces right-wing resistance after Nikolas Ferreira threatened to quit the Liberal Party if it backs him and Novo launched Paulo Martins for speaker; Ferreira also won a federal injunction blocking a Senate tender for 95 iPhone 18 Pro handsets. At the STF, Deputy Prosecutor-General Luiza Frischeisen indicted Senator Flávio Bolsonaro for criminal slander against President Lula over an X post after Nicolás Maduro’s capture linking Lula to drug and arms trafficking. In state politics, Paraná Governor-elect Sergio Moro named former Lava Jato prosecutor Carlos Fernando dos Santos Lima as Anti-Corruption Secretary, while São Paulo Governor Tarcísio de Freitas put “super-secretary” Natália Resende in charge of a new Planning, Budget, and Investments portfolio to tighten oversight of public concessions and kept Osvaldo Nico at Public Security. (Folha, Folha, MacMagazine, Folha, Folha, Folha, Folha)

Anthropic prices Nasdaq IPO at $83 for $1.5tn valuation as Democrats probe Lutnick’s Nvidia stake and Google launches Gemini 4 Neon

Anthropic priced its initial public offering at $83 a share on Wednesday night—above its $70-to-$78 target range after orders were more than ten times oversubscribed—valuing the five-year-old artificial intelligence firm at up to $1.5 trillion (£1.14 trillion) as it begins trading on the Nasdaq today under the ticker ANT. Chief executive Dario Amodei, his sister Daniela Amodei, and five co-founders who left OpenAI over safety disputes in late 2020 will ring the opening bell, each holding a stake above $18 billion and selling $260 million in shares. As the first pure-play generative AI developer to list after Sam Altman delayed OpenAI’s debut, Anthropic tests whether surging revenues can outrun data-centre costs: sales climbed from $41 million in 2023 to $4.59 billion in 2025 and $16.29 billion in the first half of 2026—with analysts forecasting $52 billion this year and $135 billion in 2027—yet half-year net losses hit $80 billion alongside $70 billion in 12-month compute commitments and $900 billion in leases through 2045. (Guardian, BBC, BBC)

That hardware boom is drawing sharp political scrutiny. Senior House Democrats Jamie Raskin, Gregory Meeks, and Sydney Kamlager-Dove demanded answers from Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick over whether he compromised US national security during the Nvidia affair to enrich his adult children and Cantor Fitzgerald, the firm he led for decades that held hundreds of millions of dollars in Nvidia stock as the chipmaker’s pure profit reached $8,300 a second. Facing local opposition to power-hungry US server farms, the Trump administration is promoting its “Pax Silica” initiative to build chip fabs and AI data centres in the Philippines, where protesters marched with a prop casket in Manila during Trump’s visit. Meanwhile, the US Justice Department indicted Russian national Igor D’iu for unlawfully entering the country via a Telegram network to spy on Palo Alto employees of defence contractor Palantir. (Guardian, The Verge, The Verge, Guardian)

In enterprise and consumer AI, Google launched Gemini 4 Neon for AI Pro and Ultra subscribers, retiring Gemini 3.1 Pro and Argon with a one-million-token model that matches Gemini 4 Carbon on coding and reasoning at Flash speeds to rank third overall on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index. Google’s drive to bundle Gemini sparked a backlash from the New York City, Los Angeles, and Chicago school districts, which are considering dropping Google Search after education vice-president Chris Phillips said schools opting out of AI features next year will lose Search access—a policy criticised by Mayor Zohran Mamdani and Senator Bernie Sanders. On Capitol Hill, Senators Ron Wyden, Ed Markey, and Ben Luján pressed Apple, Google, and Meta over AI “nudify” apps in their stores as lawmakers pushed the DEFIANCE Act and a ban on forced arbitration for online harms. (9to5Mac, The Verge, 9to5Mac, MacMagazine, The Verge)

Across Apple’s platforms, the second developer betas of iOS 27.3, iPadOS 27.3, and watchOS 27.3 activated daily usage caps on complex Siri AI queries, added the iPhone’s Siri Visual Intelligence mode to the iPad Camera app, introduced two styles for the Siri Modular watch face, and gave the foldable iPhone Duo front-camera Face Unlock when closed alongside Attention Aware controls—while leaks pointed to a rounded titanium frame and quad-curved display for next year’s 20th-anniversary iPhone 20 Pro alongside an iPhone Duo 2. Apple also shipped HomeView 27.2.1 bug fixes, updated Pages, Numbers, Keynote, Final Cut Pro 13 (with MotionVFX), and Pixelmator Pro under Apple Creator Studio, and named 45 finalists across 13 categories for the 2026 App Store Awards, including Brazilian studio Minimol Games’ Case Solved: The London Files for Apple TV. Elsewhere, Google released Google Wallet for iOS in the US without tap-to-pay, Mac launcher Alfred 6 added a native workflow UI toolkit, MacPaw brought its Moonlock security app to iPhone, OpenAI lifted ChatGPT’s ten-minute Live Dictation limit, and Instagram allowed users to edit published Stories for 15 minutes. Grand Theft Auto VI launches to record-tying 99 Metacritic score as Van Gogh sells for $190.4m and Glastonbury names youngest headliners

Thirteen years after its predecessor, Rockstar Games releases Grand Theft Auto VI worldwide today to rave reviews that give the crime epic a 99 out of 100 average on Metacritic—tying Nintendo’s 1998 The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time as the highest-rated video game in history. Critics hailed the fictional Florida state of Leonida and Vice City as the most detailed virtual world ever built, moving seamlessly between bank heists, in-game social-media doomscrolling that reveals side missions, beach walks, and flat-pack furniture assembly, while praising the chemistry of fugitive lovers Lucia Caminos and Jason Duval. That central romance led Rockstar to explain that it removed the series’ option to pick up sex workers so gameplay would remain true to a story about “lovers on the run”. (Guardian, Guardian, BBC, IGN, IGN)

The launch strained digital infrastructure before midnight in Europe and North America. Sony’s PlayStation Network suffered login and download outages on Wednesday evening, while US provider Xfinity reported a fourfold surge in Xbox traffic as players switched console regions to New Zealand for early access, pushing concurrent viewership across Twitch, YouTube, and Kick to a record 6.38 million—nearly four times Valorant’s previous peak. Reclusive Rockstar president Sam Houser issued a rare statement celebrating games as essential modern culture, while benchmarks showed a steady 30 frames per second on PS5 and Xbox Series X with occasional dips to 24fps on Xbox Series S. In Georgia, 43 soldiers in the US Army’s 9th Engineer Battalion at Fort Stewart re-enlisted under an incentive offering four days of paid leave to play the title, while Edinburgh animation programmer Nestor Uriarte urged Rockstar staff to vote yes in this month’s ballot for union recognition. (Guardian, IGN, IGN, IGN, IGN, IGN, Guardian)

In the art market, Vincent van Gogh’s Châtaigniers en fleurs (Chestnut Trees in Blossom)—painted in Auvers-sur-Oise during the final two months of his life in 1890—sold to dealer William Acquavella at Sotheby’s New York for $190.4 million (£144.9 million), breaking the artist’s $117.2 million record after an eleventh-hour settlement between the Blaquier-Arrieta Collection and the heirs of German Jewish owners the Fischer family over its Nazi-era sale. In British live music, Glastonbury announced the youngest Pyramid Stage headliner trio of the 21st century for June 2027: Sam Fender and Harry Styles (both 33 next summer) make their headline debuts on Friday and Saturday before 25-year-old Billie Eilish closes Sunday, alongside Cher in the Legends slot and a Worthy Farm debut for Kraftwerk. (Guardian, BBC, Guardian)

In film and stage news, Puck reported that The Crown star Josh O’Connor was director Denis Villeneuve’s early favourite for James Bond ahead of Callum Turner and Jack Lowden before O’Connor withdrew after his screen test over the role’s baggage, opening the door for Robert Pattinson. Meanwhile, Florence Pugh rebuked “disturbing” online speculation over her Governors Awards photos by denying cosmetic surgery or Ozempic use; 25-year-old Peruvian rapper Renata Flores is reclaiming the Inca language of Quechua for eight million Andean speakers through urban trap and hip-hop; London critics hailed the National Gallery’s reunion of Jan van Eyck’s oil portraits and Kelly Gough’s performance in Ebenezer Bamgboye’s staging of Eugene O’Neill’s Desire Under the Elms at the Almeida Theatre; and Oxford historian Theodore Zeldin died aged 93. (Guardian, Guardian, Guardian, Guardian, Guardian, Guardian)

Pochettino takes charge of relegation-threatened Manchester United ahead of Anfield trip as Chelsea beat PSG and Netflix eyes $2bn World Cup deal

Mauricio Pochettino arrived at Manchester United’s Carrington training ground on Wednesday after flying overnight from Cincinnati following his final match in charge of the United States against Haiti, calling his appointment “an honour” and “adrenaline” ahead of Sunday’s Premier League trip to Liverpool at Anfield. As assistants Jesús Pérez, Toni Jiménez, and Sebastiano Pochettino took charge of non-internationals and the 54-year-old Argentinian vowed to challenge Manchester City’s local dominance, United’s position in 18th place after ten games—their first time in the relegation zone at this stage in 40 years, alongside Sunderland and Thomas Frank’s Crystal Palace—drew comparisons with the post-Sir Matt Busby decline that led to their 1974 relegation and warnings that in 19 of 31 Premier League seasons only one of the bottom three after ten games survived. In the US, women’s coach Emma Hayes joined the panel picking Pochettino’s successor, while Opta analysis highlighted how Arsenal full-backs Ben White and Riccardo Calafiori lead all Premier League defensive partnerships with six goal involvements this season. (Guardian, Guardian, BBC, Guardian, Guardian)

In the Women’s Champions League, teenage Dutch defender Veerle Buurman scrambled home a late near-post corner at AFC Wimbledon’s stadium on Wednesday night to give Sonia Bompastor’s Chelsea a 1–0 victory over Paris Saint-Germain and top spot in the league phase. Off the pitch, Netflix and Fox lead a $1.5 billion-to-$2 billion (£1.13 billion-to-£1.5 billion) bidding war ahead of today’s first-round deadline for combined English- and Spanish-language US rights to the 2030 men’s World Cup, with Apple TV, Amazon, Disney, YouTube, DAZN, ESPN, NBCUniversal, and Skydance also circling—and FIFA tender documents revealing that three-minute mid-half hydration breaks with split-screen commercial slots will stay in 2030. Meanwhile, English Football Association chair Debbie Hewitt confirmed the FA explored a legal challenge against Gianni Infantino’s eligibility for a fourth FIFA presidential term in March before concluding a court bid would fail and backing French challenger Philippe Diallo. (Guardian, Guardian, Guardian, BBC)
